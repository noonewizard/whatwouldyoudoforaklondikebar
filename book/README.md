## The argument

Freemasonry did not inherit the Western magical tradition. It built the vehicle that
carried it — and then three generations of occultists climbed aboard and told everyone the
vehicle was ancient.

There is no documented transmission of Hermetic, Kabbalistic, alchemical or Rosicrucian
teaching into Freemasonry before the eighteenth century. The best-evidenced connection
between the Craft and magic runs the other way and runs late: outward, from Masonry into
the nineteenth-century occult revival, through named men with dated memberships.

## Standing rules observed throughout

1. **Parallel is not descent.** Resemblance requires a documented route.
2. **Never fabricate.** 88 `[SOURCE VERIFICATION REQUIRED]` markers and 2 `p. TK`
   placeholders stand where a particular could not be verified. No page number, quotation,
   date, manuscript or organisation has been invented to fill a gap.
3. **Name the scholar.** Never "scholars agree."
4. **Five claim-types kept separate.** The narrator asserts no metaphysical claim, in
   either direction.
5. **Respect fraternal obligation.** No private ritual material reproduced or invented;
   published historical sources only; jurisdictional variation stated.
