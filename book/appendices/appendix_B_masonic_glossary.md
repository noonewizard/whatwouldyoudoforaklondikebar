# APPENDIX B
## Glossary of Masonic Terms

Usage varies by jurisdiction, period and rite. Definitions here describe historical usage
as it appears in the sources cited in this book, not current practice in any particular
obedience.

**Accepted Mason.** A member of a masons' lodge who is not a working mason. English usage
from at least the seventeenth century.

**Acception.** A body within or alongside the London Company of Masons, recorded from the
1620s, into which men could be admitted; an early English non-operative practice.

**Adoption, Rite of.** A Masonic-form rite for women, worked in France from 1744 under the
authority of men's lodges, with its own degrees and a symbolism drawn largely from Genesis.

**Antients and Moderns.** Rival English grand lodges. The "Moderns" (the premier Grand
Lodge, from 1717 or 1721) and the "Antients" (from 1751, appealing to Irish immigrants and
men outside the established elite). Reconciled at the Union of 1813.

**Apron.** The principal badge of a Freemason; originally a working mason's leather
protective garment, later a ceremonial object of white lambskin, frequently decorated.

**Ashlar, rough and perfect.** A stone as quarried and a stone squared for building; in
Masonic allegory, the candidate before and after improvement.

**Cable-tow.** The cord placed about the candidate in the admission ceremony.

**Catechism.** The memorised question-and-answer sequence by which a Mason's standing is
tested and the lodge's symbolism rehearsed.

**Charges, the.** Regulations of conduct read to a candidate. In the Old Charges, appended
to the legendary history; in Anderson, printed as "The Charges of a Free-Mason."

**Craft, the.** The three symbolic degrees — Entered Apprentice, Fellow Craft, Master Mason
— as distinct from appendant and high-degree bodies.

**Degree.** A grade of membership, conferred by ceremony, admitting to further instruction.

**Deputation / warrant / charter.** The document by which a grand body authorises a lodge to
work.

**East.** The principal station of the lodge, occupied by the Master; the direction from
which light is said to come.

**Entered Apprentice, Fellow Craft, Master Mason.** The three Craft degrees.

**Exposure.** A published disclosure of Masonic ritual. The English and French exposures of
1723–1762 are the principal ritual evidence for the period.

**Great Architect of the Universe.** The formula by which the deity is referred to in Masonic
ritual; deliberately non-confessional.

**Hiram Abiff.** The craftsman of the Masonic legend, murdered at the building of Solomon's
Temple; derived from the bronze-worker of 1 Kings 7, who in scripture is neither architect
nor murdered.

**Hoodwink.** The blindfold used in the admission ceremony.

**Irregular / clandestine.** Terms used by a grand body for another body it does not
recognise. Jurisdictional and political, not historical, and in the American case carrying a
racial history.

**Jachin and Boaz.** The two pillars of Solomon's Temple (1 Kings 7:21), represented in the
lodge; also the title of the most successful eighteenth-century exposure (1762).

**Lodge.** Originally a shed beside a building site; from the Schaw Statutes onward, a
permanent constituted body; also the room in which that body meets, and the symbolic space
it represents.

**Obligation.** The oath taken by a candidate.

**Old Charges.** The manuscript constitutions of the English masons, from the Regius (c.
1390) and Cooke (c. 1410) manuscripts through more than a hundred later copies, containing a
legendary craft history and regulations.

**Operative / speculative.** Working stonemasons as against non-working members. The older
narrative of a straightforward transition from one to the other is contested.

**Prince Hall Freemasonry.** The tradition descending from African Lodge No. 459, warranted
by the Grand Lodge of England on 29 September 1784 with Prince Hall as Master.

**Provincial / District Grand Lodge.** An administrative body governing lodges in a region
or territory under a grand lodge.

**Regular.** Recognised by a given grand body as legitimate; see *irregular*.

**Royal Arch.** A degree centred on the recovery of a lost divine name, developing in England
and Ireland in the mid-eighteenth century; included within "pure Ancient Masonry" by the
Act of Union of 1813.

**Scottish Rite (Ancient and Accepted).** A system of high degrees, thirty-three in its
developed form, of substantially French origin, organised in America from 1801.

**Tracing board.** A design containing the symbols of a degree; originally drawn on the floor
in chalk and erased after the meeting, later painted on cloth or board.

**Three Great Lights.** The volume of sacred law, the square and the compasses.

**Working tools.** Square, level, plumb-rule, gauge, gavel, trowel, compasses; presented with
moral interpretations that vary by jurisdiction.

**York Rite.** A grouping of appendant American degrees including the Royal Arch, cryptic
degrees and the Knights Templar.
