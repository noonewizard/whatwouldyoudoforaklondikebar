# APPENDIX C
## Glossary of Magical and Esoteric Terms

**Alchemy (*chymistry*).** The early modern art of transformation of substances; both a
laboratory practice with material goals and an allegorical literature, frequently in the same
hands. The purely spiritual reading is a nineteenth-century development.

**Astral light.** Éliphas Lévi's term (1854) for a universal subtle medium, plastic to the
will; a synthesis of mesmeric fluid and the Renaissance *spiritus*.

**Baphomet.** A name of uncertain meaning recorded in the Templar trial depositions of
1307–12, probably a corruption of *Mahomet*; given its modern visual form by Lévi's drawing
of 1856, which illustrated a doctrine of equilibrium.

**Cabala (Christian).** The adaptation of Jewish Kabbalistic material for Christian
apologetic and magical purposes, from Pico (1486) and Reuchlin (1494, 1517) through
Agrippa (1533) to Knorr von Rosenroth's *Kabbala Denudata* (1677–84).

**Ceremonial magic.** Ritual operation addressed to spirits, angels or intelligences by name,
using circles, seals and formulae; the tradition of the medieval grimoires and of the Golden
Dawn.

**Correspondence.** The doctrine that levels of reality answer to one another, so that
material things participate in celestial and divine ones; the working assumption of
Renaissance magic and the basis of every table of attributions from Agrippa to Crowley.

**Decknamen.** Cover-names: the allegorical vocabulary by which alchemical texts concealed
technical procedures.

**Emanation.** The Neoplatonic doctrine that reality proceeds from a single source by
overflow rather than by creation, producing a graded cosmos.

**Enochian.** The angelic language and system of tables recorded by John Dee and Edward
Kelley in the 1580s, elaborated into a magical system by the Golden Dawn.

**Gematria.** The Kabbalistic technique of numerical equivalence between words.

**Hermeticism.** Four distinct referents: (1) the Greek *Corpus Hermeticum* of the 2nd–3rd
centuries CE; (2) the technical Hermetica of astrology, alchemy and magic; (3) Renaissance
Hermetism after Ficino's 1471 *Pimander*; (4) "Hermeticism" as a modern occult
self-description. Never one unchanged tradition.

**Kabbalah.** The Jewish mystical tradition: *Sefer Yetzirah*, the *Zohar* (c. 1280),
Cordovero, Luria. Distinct from Christian Cabala and from Hermetic Qabalah.

**Magic.** Not a stable category. Always qualified in this book: natural, celestial,
ceremonial, sympathetic, talismanic, angelic, planetary, theurgic, thaumaturgic. *Magick*
with a *k* is Crowley's usage from c. 1904.

**Mesmerism / animal magnetism.** Franz Anton Mesmer's doctrine of a universal magnetic
fluid; discredited as to mechanism by the French royal commission of 1784, and enormously
productive thereafter as a source of trance technique and of a vocabulary of invisible
influence.

**Natural magic.** The manipulation of hidden (*occult*, in the strict sense) properties of
physical things; substantially pre-modern natural science.

**Occultism.** A mid-nineteenth-century coinage and formation, not an ancient category.

**Pentagram.** The five-pointed star; its contrast of point-up and point-down orientations as
signifying spirit over matter and the reverse is Lévi's, from the 1850s, with no earlier
pedigree traced.

**Prisca theologia.** The doctrine that a single ancient revelation underlies all traditions,
transmitted through a chain of sages; the framework within which resemblance counts as
evidence of descent. The principal object of this book's criticism.

**Qabalah (Hermetic).** The nineteenth- and twentieth-century occult system deriving from
Lévi and the Golden Dawn, incorporating tarot, elemental and planetary attributions that have
no Jewish precedent.

**Rosicrucianism.** Five distinct referents: the manifestos (1614–16); the seventeenth-century
furore; the eighteenth-century Gold- und Rosenkreuz; nineteenth-century revivals (SRIA,
Golden Dawn); and modern bodies claiming ancient descent.

**Sephiroth / sefirot.** The ten emanations of the Kabbalistic Tree of Life.

**Solve et coagula.** Dissolve and coagulate; the fundamental rhythm of the alchemical work.

**Sunthēmata.** Iamblichus's term for the material tokens through which the divine is present
at the material level.

**Talismanic magic.** The construction of images at astrologically determined times to
concentrate celestial influence.

**Theurgy.** Ritual operation directed at the ascent of the soul, distinguished by its
Iamblichan practitioners from *goēteia* or sorcery. Not a synonym for magic.

**Thaumaturgy.** Wonder-working; the production of visible marvels.

**Tree of Life.** The diagrammatic arrangement of the ten *sefirot* and the twenty-two paths
connecting them.
