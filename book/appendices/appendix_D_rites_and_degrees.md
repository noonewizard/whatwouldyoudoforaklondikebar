# APPENDIX D
## Major Masonic Rites and Degrees

A guide to the principal systems referred to in this book. It is not exhaustive: the
eighteenth century produced many hundreds of degrees, most of which were worked briefly by
small numbers of people and left only manuscripts.

Dates are of origin or of the earliest reliable evidence. Where a body's own claimed date of
origin differs from the documented one, both are given.

---

### The Craft

**The three symbolic degrees.** Entered Apprentice, Fellow Craft, Master Mason. Two degrees
are evidenced in the Scottish catechisms from 1696 and in the English exposures of 1723–24;
the third appears in print in 1730 and was being worked in London lodges from the
mid-1720s. Worked, with substantial variation, by every regular jurisdiction in the world.

**The Royal Arch.** Developing in England and Ireland from the mid-eighteenth century;
centred on the recovery of a lost divine name. Declared part of "pure Ancient Masonry" by
the English Act of Union of 1813. Its origin is an open question.

---

### English and Irish

**Premier Grand Lodge ("Moderns").** Founded 1717 according to Anderson (1738), or 1721
according to Prescott and Sommers. Minutes from 1723.

**Antients Grand Lodge.** 1751. Laurence Dermott's *Ahiman Rezon* (1756) is its
constitution.

**United Grand Lodge of England.** 1813, uniting the two.

**Knights Templar (Great Priory).** A commemorative chivalric order within English Masonry;
no claim of literal descent.

---

### The French and continental high degrees

**Écossais grades.** "Scottish" degrees, so called after Ramsay's Oration of 1736/37; the
general term for French high-degree Masonry, with essentially no connection to Scotland.

**Elect (Élus) degrees.** From the 1740s; narratives of the pursuit and punishment of
Hiram's murderers. Among the earliest high degrees.

**Rose-Croix.** From the 1750s; a Christological reworking of the Masonic legend, the lost
word identified with the Word. Became the eighteenth degree of the Scottish Rite. Its name
has no documented connection to the Rosicrucian manifestos.

**Rite of Strict Observance.** Karl Gotthelf von Hund, from 1754. Claimed direct descent
from the Knights Templar under Unknown Superiors. Dominant in the German lands in the 1760s
and 1770s. Its Templar claim was set aside at the Convent of Wilhelmsbad in 1782.

**Order of Knight-Masons Elect Priests of the Universe (Élus Coëns).** Martinez de
Pasqually, 1761. Theurgic; the first documented entry of operative magical practice into a
Masonic structure.

**Régime Écossais Rectifié / Rectified Scottish Rite.** Jean-Baptiste Willermoz; Convent of
Lyon 1778, endorsed at Wilhelmsbad 1782. Built on the Strict Observance's structure with
Martinez's doctrine of the fall and reintegration, and Saint-Martin's interiority. Its
members are the Chevaliers Bienfaisants de la Cité Sainte. Still worked.

**Rite of Adoption.** From 1744. Four principal degrees — Apprentice, Companion, Mistress,
Perfect Mistress — with symbolism drawn from Genesis. Worked by women's lodges under men's
lodges. See Chapter 29.

**Egyptian Rite.** Cagliostro, 1784–85. Egyptian in costume, eighteenth-century in
substance.

**Rites of Memphis and of Misraim.** Nineteenth century; systems of very large numbers of
degrees (ninety and upward), of irregular provenance, which supplied much of the fringe-
Masonic material later assembled by Theodor Reuss.

---

### American

**Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite.** Supreme Council founded at Charleston, 1801. Thirty-
three degrees. Albert Pike, Sovereign Grand Commander of the Southern Jurisdiction
1859–1891, rewrote the rituals and published *Morals and Dogma* (1871) as their commentary.

**York Rite.** A grouping of appendant degrees: Royal Arch, cryptic degrees, and the
Knights Templar commanderies.

**Prince Hall Freemasonry.** African Lodge No. 459, warranted by the Grand Lodge of England
29 September 1784, Prince Hall Master; Provincial Grand Master 1791. The tradition
descending from it.

**Order of the Eastern Star.** Nineteenth century; an American body admitting women related
to Master Masons, distinct in structure from the Adoption Rite.

---

### Mixed and women's

**Le Droit Humain.** Founded 1893 by Maria Deraismes and Georges Martin; admits men and
women on equal terms in the same lodges. International.

**Co-Masonry in Britain and the Empire.** From 1902, promoted by Annie Besant; the principal
route by which full Masonic ritual entered the Theosophical movement and spread
internationally.

**The Order of Women Freemasons; the Honourable Fraternity of Ancient Freemasons.**
Twentieth-century English women's bodies.

---

### Bodies requiring Masonic membership

These are not Masonic rites but are included because the requirement is the mechanism this
book identifies as the principal route of transmission.

**Gold- und Rosenkreuz.** Central Europe, from the 1750s, reorganised 1777. Nine grades.
Alchemical. Required Master Mason status for admission.

**Societas Rosicruciana in Anglia (SRIA).** 1866. Grade structure derived from the Gold- und
Rosenkreuz. Required Master Mason status. Its members founded the Golden Dawn.
