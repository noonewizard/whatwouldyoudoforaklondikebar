# APPENDIX E
## Major Occult Orders

Each entry gives: founding, founders, structure, content, relationship to Freemasonry, and
the evidentiary status of its origin claim.

---

### Order of the Gold and Rosy Cross (Gold- und Rosenkreuz)

**Founded** Central Europe, from the 1750s; reorganised 1777.
**Structure** Nine grades, from Juniores to Magus.
**Content** Alchemy, Rosicrucian and theosophical material.
**Masonic relation** Required Master Mason status for admission. The clearest institutional
evidence of the carriage thesis in the eighteenth century.
**Origin claim** Descent from a seventeenth-century Rosicrucian brotherhood. **Unsupported:
no such organisation existed.**
**Notes** Its members Wöllner and Bischoffwerder reached ministerial power in Prussia after
1786. Its grade structure descends to the SRIA and thence to the Golden Dawn.

---

### Order of Knight-Masons Elect Priests of the Universe (Élus Coëns)

**Founded** 1761, Bordeaux, by Martinez de Pasqually.
**Content** Theurgic operations aimed at the *réintégration* of fallen beings; doctrine set
out in the unfinished *Traité de la réintégration des êtres*.
**Masonic relation** Built on Masonic degree structure; recruited from lodges.
**Origin claim** A patent of doubtful provenance attributed to Charles Edward Stuart.
**Unverified.**
**Notes** The first documented entry of operative magic into a Masonic structure. Its
students Saint-Martin and Willermoz carried the doctrine in opposite directions.

---

### The Bavarian Illuminati

**Founded** 1776, Ingolstadt, by Adam Weishaupt.
**Content** Enlightenment rationalism, anticlericalism, reformist politics.
**Method** Systematic infiltration of Masonic lodges.
**Fate** Suppressed 1784–87; internal papers published by the Bavarian state, Munich, 1787.
**Origin claim** None of consequence.
**Notes** The documented facts are the basis of the entire modern conspiracy tradition,
constructed from the published papers by Robison and Barruel in 1797–98. **No evidence of
survival after 1787.**

---

### Theosophical Society

**Founded** 1875, New York, by H. P. Blavatsky, H. S. Olcott and W. Q. Judge.
**Content** A universal ancient wisdom-religion underlying all traditions; karma,
reincarnation, spiritual evolution; Asian material filtered through Victorian
Orientalism.
**Masonic relation** Indirect but substantial, through Annie Besant's promotion of
Co-Masonry from 1902.
**Origin claim** Direction by the Mahatmas or Masters, a hidden brotherhood of adepts.
**Unsupported.**
**Notes** Among the most culturally consequential organisations in this book. Its effects on
Indian and Sinhalese cultural nationalism, on modern astrology, and on twentieth-century
alternative spirituality are large and documented.

---

### Societas Rosicruciana in Anglia (SRIA)

**Founded** 1866, by Robert Wentworth Little and a circle of English Masons.
**Structure** Grades derived from the Gold- und Rosenkreuz.
**Masonic relation** Master Mason status required for membership. **The pipeline.**
**Origin claim** Rosicrucian descent. **Unsupported as institutional descent; the material
inheritance from the Gold- und Rosenkreuz is real.**
**Notes** Its members included Kenneth Mackenzie, Frederick Hockley, W. R. Woodman, W. W.
Westcott and S. L. MacGregor Mathers.

---

### Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

**Founded** 1888, London, by Westcott, Mathers and Woodman, on the Cipher Manuscripts and
the "Sprengel" correspondence.
**Structure** Grades mapped onto the Sephiroth of the Tree of Life; an Outer Order and a
Second Order with the Vault of the Adepti.
**Content** Hermetic Qabalah, tables of correspondence, ritual invocation and banishing,
scrying, astral work, talismanic magic, tarot, Enochian magic, Egyptian god-forms.
**Masonic relation** Structural, not doctrinal: grades, temple, officers, obligation,
staged revelation, regalia — a Masonic machine running non-Masonic software.
**Origin claim** Authority from a German Rosicrucian adept, Anna Sprengel of Nuremberg.
**Unsupported: the correspondence is spurious, and Mathers said so in 1900.**
**Notes** Admitted women on equal terms from the beginning. Collapsed 1900–1903 into
successor bodies. Its system, published by Crowley (1909–13) and Regardie (1937–40), is the
basis of modern Western ceremonial magic.

---

### Martinist Order (revived)

**Founded** 1891, Paris, by Papus (Gérard Encausse) and others.
**Content** Christian theosophy in the tradition of Saint-Martin.
**Origin claim** Institutional descent from Saint-Martin's circle. **Unsupported: Saint-
Martin had readers, not an order. The doctrinal inheritance is real.**

---

### A∴A∴

**Founded** 1907, by Aleister Crowley and George Cecil Jones.
**Structure** Golden Dawn grades on the Tree of Life, rationalised, with a curriculum and a
reading list. Each member knows only his initiator and his initiates.
**Origin claim** Crowley's own attainment and the authority of *Liber AL vel Legis*.
**Notes** The structural rule is a deliberate solution to the authority crisis that
destroyed the Golden Dawn.

---

### Ordo Templi Orientis (O.T.O.)

**Founded** c. 1902–06, by Carl Kellner and Theodor Reuss; Crowley chartered for Great
Britain and Ireland in 1912 and later head of the order.
**Structure** A degree ladder built on Masonic high-degree models drawn from irregular
continental rites, including Memphis and Misraim.
**Content** After Crowley, Thelema; sexual magic in the higher degrees.
**Masonic relation** Derived from fringe-Masonic degree systems. **Not recognised by any
regular Grand Lodge.**
**Origin claim** Templar and Masonic descent. **Unsupported as descent; the borrowing of
degree structure from irregular Masonic rites is documented.**
