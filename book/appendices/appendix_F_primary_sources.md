# APPENDIX F
## Primary Sources

A working list of the documents on which this book chiefly depends, with a note on what each
can and cannot be used to establish. Full bibliographical details are in the Bibliography.

---

### Masonic manuscripts

**Regius MS** (c. 1390, BL Royal 17 A.I) and **Cooke MS** (c. 1410, BL Add. 23198).
*Establish:* that the masons' craft carried a legendary history running through Euclid and
Solomon's Temple, and a body of regulations, by the late fourteenth century.
*Do not establish:* any esoteric content. The Temple here is a guild origin story.

**Schaw Statutes** (28 December 1598; 28 December 1599).
*Establish:* the permanent Scottish lodge system, its record-keeping requirement, its
jurisdiction, and the art-of-memory clause.
*Do not establish:* which art of memory was meant.

**Minutes of the Lodge of Edinburgh (Mary's Chapel)**, from 1599, and **Aitchison's Haven**,
from 1598.
*Establish:* continuous institutional existence, and non-operative attendance from 1600.
*Do not establish:* any ritual content; the minutes concern money, discipline and dinner.

**Edinburgh Register House MS** (1696), **Chetwode Crawley MS** (c. 1700), **Kevan MS**
(c. 1714).
*Establish:* the earliest recoverable ritual: two degrees, a word, a grip, a catechism.
*Do not establish:* continuity with anything earlier.

---

### Diaries and contemporary observation

**Elias Ashmole, diary**, 16 October 1646 and March 1682 (ed. Josten, 1966).
*Establish:* the best-documented early English non-operative initiation, and a thirty-five-
year gap in his participation.
*Do not establish:* his reason for joining. He never states one.

**Robert Plot, *The Natural History of Staffordshire*** (1686).
*Establish:* that the custom was widespread, that persons of quality joined, that admission
involved a collation and secret signs, and that a written craft history circulated.
*Note:* a skeptical witness, which increases its value.

**John Aubrey, *Natural History of Wiltshire*** (MS 1686).

**William Stukeley, diary**, 6 January 1721 (Surtees Society edition).
*Establish:* his initiation at the Salutation Tavern, and his belief that he was the first
man made a Mason in London for many years and that members were hard to assemble.
*Bearing:* the principal external evidence against the 1717 foundation date.

---

### Printed constitutions

**Anderson, *The Constitutions of the Free-Masons*** (1723).
*Establish:* the institution's official self-presentation; the legendary history in print;
the religious formula; the charge against women and bondmen.
*Do not establish:* what happened in lodges.

**Anderson, *The New Book of Constitutions*** (1738).
*Establish:* the 1717 foundation narrative. **Sole source. Retrospective. Interested.**

**Dermott, *Ahiman Rezon*** (1756); **Preston, *Illustrations of Masonry*** (1772).

**Anderson's *Constitutions*, Philadelphia: Franklin** (1734). First Masonic book printed in
America.

---

### Exposures

*A Mason's Examination* (1723); *The Grand Mystery of Free-Masons Discover'd* (1724);
Prichard, *Masonry Dissected* (1730); Pérau, *Le Secret des Francs-Maçons* (1742);
*L'Ordre des Francs-Maçons Trahi* (1745); *Three Distinct Knocks* (1760); *Jachin and Boaz*
(1762).

*Establish, comparatively:* the ritual content at datable moments; the emergence of the
third degree and the Hiramic legend between 1724 and 1730; the accretion of symbolism
decade by decade; **and the absence, across forty years and two countries, of Kabbalah,
Hermeticism, alchemy, ceremonial magic, Egypt, Templars and Rosicrucians.**
*Caution:* commercial and sometimes hostile documents. Use comparatively, never singly.

---

### Esoteric primary texts

Ficino's *Pimander* (1471); Pico's *Conclusiones* (1486); Reuchlin (1494, 1517); Agrippa's
*De occulta philosophia* (1533) and its English translation (1651); the Rosicrucian
manifestos (1614–16); Knorr's *Kabbala Denudata* (1677–84); Böhme.

*Establish:* what was available to read, when, and in what language.
*Do not establish:* that any Mason read it. **Availability is opportunity, not
transmission.**

---

### Nineteenth- and twentieth-century occult sources

Lévi, *Dogme et rituel* (1854–56) and *Histoire de la magie* (1860); Constant, *La Bible de
la liberté* (1841); Blavatsky, *Isis Unveiled* (1877) and *The Secret Doctrine* (1888);
Mathers, *The Kabbalah Unveiled* (1887); Pike, *Morals and Dogma* (1871) and the de Hoyos
annotated edition (2011); Crowley, *The Equinox* (1909–13), *Liber 777* (1909), *Magick in
Theory and Practice* (1929–30); Regardie, *The Golden Dawn* (1937–40).

**The rule of direction applies.** These are primary sources for their own century and
unreliable secondary sources for every earlier one. Blavatsky on 1880s Theosophy is
evidence; Blavatsky on ancient Egypt is not.

---

### Anti-Masonic and conspiracy sources

Robison, *Proofs of a Conspiracy* (1797); Barruel, *Mémoires* (1797–98); *Einige
Originalschriften des Illuminatenordens* (Munich, 1787); Morgan, *Illustrations of Masonry*
(1826); Taxil's fabrications (1886–97); de la Rive (1894); Taxil's confession, *Le Frondeur*,
25 April 1897; the papal bulls of 1738 and 1884.

*Establish:* the existence, dating and content of accusations.
*Do not establish:* anything about Freemasonry. Used exclusively as evidence of belief —
except the Illuminati papers, which are genuine documents of a real organization.
