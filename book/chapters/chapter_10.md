# CHAPTER TEN
## The Christian Kabbalah

There is a word in this book that will be spelled three different ways, and the spellings
are not decoration. They mark three different things, and the confusion between them has
done more damage to the popular understanding of Western esotericism than any other single
error.

**Kabbalah** — the Jewish mystical tradition.

**Cabala** — the Christian adaptation of it, from the 1480s onward.

**Qabalah** — the nineteenth- and twentieth-century occult system that took the name.

They are connected. Each descends from the one before by a traceable route. And the thing
at the end of the chain would be unrecognisable to the people at the start of it.

---

### What the Jewish tradition is

A short and inadequate summary, offered because the reader needs a baseline against which
to measure what happens to it.

The word *kabbalah* means "received" — that which is handed down. The tradition it names
emerged in Provence and Catalonia in the twelfth and thirteenth centuries, with older roots
in Jewish mystical speculation about the divine chariot of Ezekiel's vision and about the
work of creation. Its central classic, the *Zohar*, appeared in Castile around 1280,
written in an artificial Aramaic and attributed to a second-century rabbi; Gershom Scholem's
demonstration that its principal author was Moses de León, a near-contemporary, is one of
the foundational achievements of modern Jewish scholarship.[^1]

Its central structure is the ten *sefirot*: emanations or aspects through which the
unknowable God — *Ein Sof*, the Infinite — becomes manifest. They have names (Crown,
Wisdom, Understanding, Mercy, Severity, Beauty, Eternity, Splendour, Foundation, Kingdom)
and they are arranged in a pattern that later diagrams render as a tree.

Two further features matter for what follows. First, **the letters are real**. Hebrew is
not a system of signs pointing at a pre-existing creation; in the Kabbalistic account,
creation is effected through the letters, and therefore the letters are the joints of
being. The *Sefer Yetzirah*, a short and very old text of disputed date, works this out in
terms of twenty-two letters and ten numbers. Second, **the divine names are the highest
level of this**, and the tradition surrounding them is correspondingly guarded.

And it is a tradition with contexts. Kabbalah developed inside Jewish law, Jewish
liturgy, Jewish communal life and Jewish suffering. Isaac Luria's sixteenth-century system
in Safed — the contraction of God to make room for the world, the shattering of the vessels
that were to hold the divine light, and the human task of gathering the scattered sparks —
is, among other things, a theology written by and for a people who had been expelled from
Spain within living memory.

Detach it from that and you have something else. Detaching it was the whole Christian
project.

---

### What Pico's successors did with it

We met Pico in Chapter 8 asserting that magic and Kabbalah are the best proofs of Christ's
divinity. The man who turned that assertion into a discipline was Johannes Reuchlin.

Reuchlin was a German humanist and a serious Hebraist — he produced the first Hebrew
grammar written by a Christian for Christians, and in 1510 he risked his career defending
Jewish books against a campaign to have them burned. His two Kabbalistic works, *De verbo
mirifico* (1494) and *De arte cabalistica* (1517), established Christian Cabala as a field
with a literature.[^2]

His method can be illustrated by his most famous argument. The Tetragrammaton — the
four-letter divine name YHWH — is, in Jewish practice, unpronounceable. Reuchlin proposed
that inserting the letter *shin* into the middle produces a pronounceable five-letter name,
which transliterates to something very like *Yeheshuah*: Jesus. The unspeakable name of
God becomes speakable at the Incarnation.

It is ingenious. It is also, as an account of the Hebrew tradition, without foundation. No
Jewish Kabbalist had ever performed that operation or would have recognised its logic.

That pattern holds across the whole Christian Cabalistic enterprise. The techniques —
gematria (numerical equivalence of letters), notarikon (treating words as acronyms), temurah
(letter substitution) — are genuinely Jewish. The results are Christian, because the
operator decided in advance what he was looking for and the techniques are flexible enough
to supply it.

I want to be precise about the criticism. The charge is not that Christian Cabalists were
dishonest. Most were sincere, some were learned, and Reuchlin's defence of Jewish books was
an act of real courage in a dangerous moment. The charge is methodological: they were
operating inside the *prisca theologia*, where all traditions must agree, and so they could
not find disagreement. A method that cannot produce a negative result is not finding
anything.

### The chain

Now the part that matters most for this book, and it can be set out as a sequence of four
named links.

**Link one: Pico, 1486.** Kabbalistic material enters Christian intellectual life, mediated
by converted Jewish scholars, selected for Christological utility.

**Link two: Reuchlin, 1494 and 1517.** The selection becomes a discipline with a method and
a literature. Agrippa builds Book Three of the *Occult Philosophy* on it in 1533, which puts
Cabala permanently inside the magical tradition rather than beside it.

**Link three: Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–1684.** Christian Knorr von Rosenroth publishes
*Kabbala Denudata* — "the Kabbalah Unveiled" — an enormous Latin anthology of Kabbalistic
texts in translation, including substantial portions of the *Zohar* and Lurianic material.
This is the most important single act of transmission in the chain, because it made the
material available to Europeans who could read Latin and not Hebrew. Which was, by then,
nearly all of them.[^3]

**Link four: Mathers, 1887.** Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mathers publishes *The Kabbalah
Unveiled* in English. Its title translates Knorr's, and so does its content: Mathers is
translating Knorr's Latin. He is not working from Hebrew.[^4]

Four links, two languages of intermediation, four centuries. Each link is documented; you
can hold every one of these books. This is transmission evidence of the highest quality,
and it is worth saying plainly that the Christian Kabbalistic tradition is one of the very
few places in this book where a claimed line of descent turns out to be *real*.

But look at what arrives.

At link four, the material has passed through a Christianising selection (Pico), a
Christological method (Reuchlin), an absorption into operative magic (Agrippa), a Latin
anthology made by a man with his own agenda (Knorr), and an English translation by a man
who could not read the originals (Mathers). And Mathers and his colleagues then add
components with no Jewish precedent whatever: the tarot trumps assigned to the paths
between the *sefirot*; the four elements and the seven planets mapped onto the structure;
a system of grades in which an initiate ascends the Tree by ceremonial advancement.

The result is Hermetic Qabalah. It is a genuine and powerful system, it has produced a
century of serious practice, and it is *not the Kabbalah*. It shares a skeleton and a
vocabulary and almost nothing else.

Anyone who tells you that Freemasonry, or the Golden Dawn, or Aleister Crowley preserved
ancient Jewish mystical teaching is describing link four and calling it link zero.

---

### And Freemasonry?

Where does the Craft sit in this?

Almost nowhere, for a long time, and then suddenly everywhere.

The early ritual evidence — the Scottish catechisms of the 1690s, the English exposures of
1723 to 1762 — contains no Kabbalah. No *sefirot*. No Tree. No gematria. No divine names
used as operative instruments. The Hebrew that appears is the ordinary biblical Hebrew of
the Temple narrative: the names of the pillars, the name of a builder, the name of God as
any Protestant would use it.

This is a case where the negative evidence is unusually strong, because the exposures were
written by men trying to sell scandal. If there had been Kabbalistic machinery in an
English lodge in 1730, Samuel Prichard would have printed it, and the pamphlet would have
sold even better.

The change comes with the high degrees, from the 1740s. Here Hebrew letters, divine names
and Kabbalistic vocabulary begin appearing in French and German rituals — sometimes as
decoration, sometimes with real intent. And the change becomes total in the nineteenth
century, when Éliphas Lévi fuses Kabbalah with tarot and everything else, and Albert Pike
transcribes large portions of Lévi into a book given to every Scottish Rite Mason in the
southern United States.

By 1900 an American Mason could reasonably believe that his order had always been
Kabbalistic. The books said so. The degrees said so. What he could not easily discover was
that the source of the claim was a French author of the 1850s, and that the material had
entered his tradition within his grandfather's lifetime.

There is one genuine complication, and honesty requires it. The Royal Arch degree, which
took shape in England and Ireland in the middle of the eighteenth century, centres on the
recovery of a lost divine name. That is a theme with real resonance in the Jewish mystical
tradition, and it is not obviously borrowed from Lévi or anyone after him, because it
predates them by a century. Where the Royal Arch came from is one of the genuinely open
questions in Masonic history, and I have no confident answer. It is on the list in Chapter
52.

---

Kabbalah is the second major current assembled and we now have a method for tracing these
things: name the links, or admit there are none.

The third current is different in kind. It is not primarily a body of texts to be
transmitted but a practice with furnaces and glassware and years of failed experiments —
and the reason it matters to this book is that its central image is a death followed by a
resurrection, which is also the central image of the Masonic third degree.

That coincidence has launched a thousand books. We are going to test it properly.
