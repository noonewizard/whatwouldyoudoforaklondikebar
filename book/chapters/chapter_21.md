# CHAPTER TWENTY-ONE
## The Pillars, the Ladder, the Star

This chapter is a dating exercise, and it is deliberately unglamorous.

For each of six symbols, three questions: what does the Masonic tradition say it means;
when does that meaning first appear in a source we can hold; and what has been added
since, by whom, and when.

The result is a stratigraphy. Symbols that look like a single ancient system turn out to
have been deposited in layers, over about two hundred years, by identifiable processes. And
the layers do not run in the direction most people expect: the elements that feel most
archaic are frequently the most recent.

---

### The Pillars

**The claim.** Two pillars stand at the entrance of the lodge, as they stood at the porch
of Solomon's Temple. They are named. In most workings they carry globes — one celestial,
one terrestrial.

**The source.** Direct and unambiguous: 1 Kings 7:15–22 and 2 Chronicles 3:15–17. The
pillars, their bronze, their capitals, their lily-work and pomegranates, and their names —
Jachin on the right, Boaz on the left — are all biblical. This is one of the rare
occasions in this book where a Masonic symbol has a completely documented ancestor, and
the ancestor is scripture, available to every member in his own house.

**The dating.** The pillars are in the earliest Masonic material we have. The Scottish
catechisms of the 1690s are concerned with them. They are in the English exposures from
the 1720s. There is no period of Masonic history without them.

**The additions.** Two, both later and both traceable.

The *globes* are early modern. Terrestrial and celestial globes were instruments and status
objects of the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries — you can see them in portraits of
natural philosophers. Putting them atop the pillars converts a biblical architectural
feature into an emblem of universal knowledge, and it is an eighteenth-century decision. A
pillar of the tenth century BCE did not have a globe on it, and the anachronism is worth
noticing precisely because nobody minds it: the Craft was never attempting historical
reconstruction.

The *Kabbalistic reading* — the pillars as Severity and Mercy, the two outer columns of the
Sephirotic Tree, with a middle pillar of equilibrium between them — is nineteenth century.
It is Lévi's world, transmitted through Pike and the Golden Dawn. It is not in any
eighteenth-century Masonic source. It is now so widespread that many Masons encounter it
before they encounter 1 Kings.

**Verdict:** biblical descent, documented; Kabbalistic interpretation, Victorian.

### Jacob's Ladder

**The claim.** A ladder reaching from earth to heaven, with three principal rungs — Faith,
Hope and Charity — appears on the first-degree tracing board.

**The source.** Genesis 28:12, Jacob's dream at Bethel: a ladder set up on the earth with
its top reaching to heaven, and the angels of God ascending and descending on it. The three
virtues are 1 Corinthians 13:13.

**The dating.** The ladder is not in the earliest catechisms. It appears in the developed
tracing-board imagery of the later eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, when the
visual symbolism of the degrees was being elaborated and standardised.[^1]

**The additions.** The mapping of the ladder onto a Kabbalistic or planetary ascent —
seven rungs for seven spheres, in the Hermetic manner of Chapter 6 — is an occult reading
of the nineteenth century. There is an obvious *structural* affinity between a ladder of
ascent and the Neoplatonic and Hermetic ladders, and I do not want to pretend otherwise.
But affinity is not derivation: the biblical image was universally available, it required
no esoteric source, and the Masonic version's three rungs are Pauline, not planetary.

**Verdict:** biblical descent; the planetary and Sephirotic mappings are later overlays.

---

### The Blazing Star

**The claim.** A star, usually five-pointed, in the centre of the lodge floor or on the
tracing board. Interpretations offered in the Masonic sources include Providence, the
guiding star, the sun, and glory.

**The dating.** Present in eighteenth-century material; the interpretations offered in that
period are broad and vary between workings, which is itself informative — the Craft's own
sources are not consistent about what it means.

**The additions.** The identification of the five-pointed star with the *pentagram* as a
magical figure, and the elaborate discussion of its point-up and point-down orientations as
signifying spirit over matter or the reverse, is Lévi. He published the contrast in the
1850s, and it is one of the single most successful pieces of nineteenth-century occult
image-making: the point-down pentagram as an emblem of evil is, so far as anyone has
traced, his invention and has no earlier pedigree.[^2]

The star was in the lodge for a century before it acquired that freight.

**Verdict:** old within Masonry, ambiguous in its own sources, and given its most famous
meaning by a French author in 1856.

### The Letter G

**The claim.** The letter G, suspended in the east of the lodge or set within the square
and compasses, signifying Geometry, and also God.

**The dating.** Eighteenth century. It is in the exposures.

**The observation that settles everything.** The symbol is a letter of the Roman alphabet
whose meaning depends on the initial letter of two English words, *Geometry* and *God*.

That single fact is the most efficient demonstration in this book of the anachronism test
from Chapter 5. The symbol cannot be ancient. It cannot be Egyptian, Hebrew, Phoenician,
Greek or Templar, because in none of those languages does the relevant concept begin with
that letter. It is a symbol that could only have been generated by English-speaking people,
and it therefore dates from the period in which English-speaking people were making Masonic
symbols, which is the eighteenth century.

The point survives translation, too: in French lodges the letter persists, sometimes with
*Gloire*, *Grandeur*, *Génie* supplied — that is, the letter travelled as a letter and
acquired new meanings to justify itself, which is exactly backwards from how an ancient
symbol behaves.

**The additions.** The occult readings are numerous and inventive: the Hebrew letter *yod*,
the generative principle, the number three, the tetragrammaton. All are nineteenth century
or later, and all are attempts to give an English initial an ancient parentage.

**Verdict: demonstrably modern, and the cleanest available proof that at least some Masonic
symbolism was manufactured in the eighteenth century.**

### Sun and Moon

**The claim.** Sun and moon appear among the lodge's "lesser lights," with the Master; the
sun to rule the day, the moon the night.

**The source.** Genesis 1:16 supplies the formula almost verbatim.

**The dating.** Eighteenth century, in the catechisms.

**The additions.** The alchemical reading — Sol and Luna, the king and queen, the
conjunction of opposites — maps onto the Masonic pair without difficulty, and has been
applied to it constantly since the nineteenth century. As with the pillars, the mapping is
elegant. As with the pillars, the eighteenth-century Masonic sources give no sign of it.
The Craft's own explanation is calendrical and biblical: these are the two lights God set
in the firmament, and the Master rules the lodge as they rule day and night.

**Verdict:** biblical; the alchemical reading is an overlay.

---

### The All-Seeing Eye

Kept for last, because it is the symbol about which the public is most confident and the
evidence is least satisfactory.

**The claim.** An eye, often within a triangle, often radiant, signifying the watchfulness
of God.

**The source.** Christian devotional iconography. The Eye of Providence in a triangle —
the triangle for the Trinity — is a commonplace of European religious art from the
Renaissance onward. It is on churches, on tombs, in Bibles, in emblem books. It was not
invented by, and was never proprietary to, Freemasonry.

**The dating within Masonry.** Later than most people assume. It is not in the earliest
Masonic material; it appears in the second half of the eighteenth century, and it becomes
prominent in the nineteenth with the growth of printed and manufactured Masonic imagery.
Establishing the precise earliest datable appearance in Masonic material culture — as
opposed to commentary — requires work on tracing boards, aprons and certificates that I
have not been able to complete, and I flag it as an open question rather than guess.[^3]

**The additions.** Here the overlay is not occult but conspiratorial, and it is very recent.
The identification of the Eye with the Bavarian Illuminati, and the reading of the reverse
of the Great Seal of the United States as a Masonic-Illuminati emblem, belongs
substantially to the twentieth century and overwhelmingly to the period after about 1960.

The Great Seal's designers — a committee process running from 1776 to 1782, with the final
design by Charles Thomson, Secretary of Congress — used the Eye of Providence because it
was a standard emblem of divine favour in the visual vocabulary of the period. Thomson was
not a Freemason.[^4] The unfinished pyramid comes from a different committee proposal
entirely. The design was not reproduced on the dollar bill until 1935.

**Verdict:** Christian iconographic descent, documented; Masonic adoption in the later
eighteenth century; conspiratorial reading entirely modern.

### The stratigraphy

Set the six side by side and the pattern is unmistakable.

| Symbol | Documented source | In Masonry by | Principal later overlay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pillars | 1 Kings / 2 Chron. | 1690s | Kabbalistic pillars, C19 |
| Jacob's Ladder | Genesis 28; 1 Cor. 13 | later C18 | planetary/Sephirotic ascent, C19 |
| Blazing Star | — (broad) | C18 | Lévi's pentagram, 1856 |
| Letter G | English initials | C18 | Hebrew *yod* etc., C19 |
| Sun and Moon | Genesis 1 | C18 | alchemical Sol/Luna, C19 |
| All-Seeing Eye | Christian art | later C18 | Illuminati reading, C20 |

Every documented source is biblical or ordinary Christian iconography. Every esoteric
reading is nineteenth century. Every conspiratorial reading is twentieth.

Nothing in the Craft's symbolic vocabulary requires an ancient hidden channel, because
everything in it was in a book the members already owned.

That is the finding of this chapter, and it is worth stating without triumph. It does not
make the symbols less interesting. It makes them differently interesting: they are not an
inheritance, they are a *construction*, and we can watch it being built.

Which leaves the one symbol that cannot be handled this way, because its source is not in
the Bible at all. There is a craftsman named Hiram in 1 Kings who makes two pillars and
then disappears from the narrative alive.

Somebody killed him. We do not know who, or when, or why.
