# CHAPTER TWENTY-EIGHT
## The Gold and Rosy Cross

In 1786 the King of Prussia died and was succeeded by his nephew, and within a short time
two men who had been the new king's instructors in a secret alchemical order were among the
most powerful figures in the Prussian state.

Frederick William II had been initiated into the Order of the Gold and Rosy Cross — the
*Gold- und Rosenkreuz* — in 1781. His initiators, Johann Christoph Wöllner and Johann
Rudolf von Bischoffwerder, became respectively his minister responsible for religious
affairs and one of his closest advisers. Wöllner's Religious Edict of 1788 imposed doctrinal
orthodoxy on the Prussian clergy and censorship on theological publishing, in a state that
under Frederick the Great had been a byword for enlightened toleration. Kant himself was
eventually silenced on religious subjects.[^1]

This is the closest thing in the eighteenth century to the scenario the conspiracy
literature has always alleged: an esoteric order, recruiting through Freemasonry, whose
members reached the summit of a European state and used the position.

It is worth examining exactly, because the reality differs from the legend in ways that are
more interesting than the legend — and because it is, on the evidence, the most consequential
junction between an occult tradition and Masonic structure in the entire century.

---

### The order

The Gold- und Rosenkreuz emerged in Central Europe around the 1750s and was reorganised on
a new basis in 1777. Christopher McIntosh's study of it remains the standard account, and
what follows depends on his work throughout.[^2]

Its content was alchemical and Rosicrucian. It taught a system of nine grades —
Juniores, Theoreticus, Practicus, Philosophus, Minor, Major, Adeptus Exemptus, Magister,
Magus — through which a member ascended, receiving at each stage instruction, symbolic
material and, at the higher levels, alchemical processes.

Its mythology was the Rosicrucian one of Chapter 12: an ancient brotherhood, hidden
superiors, a wisdom preserved from antiquity. Its actual materials were seventeenth- and
eighteenth-century alchemical and theosophical literature, some of it excellent, some of it
the ordinary dross of the genre.

And its entry requirement was Freemasonry.

---

### The requirement

That clause deserves its own section, because it is the evidence.

To be admitted to the Gold and Rosy Cross, a man had to be a Master Mason. Not as a
formality — as a structural condition. The order treated the Craft degrees as the
preliminary stage of its own system: Masonry was the outer court, the Rosicrucian grades the
inner.

Consider what this means for the direction of influence.

If Rosicrucianism had descended into Freemasonry, as the popular story has it, we would
expect to find Rosicrucian content inside Masonic ritual, carried there by transmission.
We do not: Part III established its absence across forty years of published evidence.

What we find instead is an alchemical order, founded in the 1750s and reorganised in 1777,
which *uses Freemasonry as its recruiting ground and its structural model*, and which
requires Masonic membership because Masonry supplies the graded initiatory apparatus on
which its own system is built.

The Craft is the platform. The Rosicrucian material is the application running on it.

That is the carriage thesis with a documented institutional requirement attached, and the
Gold- und Rosenkreuz is the cleanest single proof of it in the eighteenth century.

---

### What they actually did

The order's members practised alchemy, and I want to be careful about what that means,
because Chapter 11 established that the word covers a range.

Some of it was laboratory work. Bischoffwerder appears to have conducted actual operations.
The order circulated processes, recipes and instructions of the kind that had been passing
through alchemical networks for two centuries.

Some of it was theatre. The order's higher grades involved elaborate ceremonial, and there
are accounts — including from the Prussian court — of demonstrations that were, to put it
carefully, assisted. Bischoffwerder is said to have staged spirit manifestations for the
future king, and whether or not the specific stories are reliable, the general phenomenon
was common enough in the period to need no special explanation.

And some of it was genuinely intellectual: the transmission and study of a substantial
literature.

The order also produced one of the great artefacts of eighteenth-century esotericism, the
*Geheime Figuren der Rosenkreuzer* — the Secret Symbols of the Rosicrucians — published at
Altona in the 1780s, a folio of coloured alchemical and theosophical plates that has been
reproduced continuously ever since and is one of the most visually influential esoteric
works ever printed.[^3]

---

### The politics

Now the difficult part, because this is where the legend and the evidence diverge.

The Wöllner ministry was reactionary. It rolled back Frederician toleration, imposed
orthodoxy, censored, and made Prussia briefly a less free country than it had been. Its
architects were members of an occult order who had reached power through their relationship
with a prince they had initiated.

That is all true, and any honest account must say so. It is the one case in this book where
something close to the conspiracy scenario actually happened.

But notice the shape of it, because the shape is the opposite of the legend.

The Gold- und Rosenkreuz was not a radical or revolutionary body. It was *counter*-
Enlightenment: religiously orthodox, politically conservative, monarchist, and explicitly
hostile to the rationalism of the age. McIntosh's argument is that Rosicrucianism of this
period constituted a kind of third force, sometimes allying with the Enlightenment and
sometimes against it, and in Prussia in the 1780s it came down firmly against.

Meanwhile, and in the same decade and the same language, the Bavarian Illuminati — the
order that the conspiracy literature accuses of destroying throne and altar — were doing the
opposite: infiltrating lodges in order to promote rationalism, anticlericalism and
enlightened reform.

So in the 1780s there were two occult-political networks operating through German
Freemasonry, and they were on opposite sides. One reached actual state power and used it to
impose religious orthodoxy. The other was suppressed in 1784–87 and its papers published,
and it became the permanent villain of Western conspiracy theory.

The one that won is almost never mentioned.

I find that a useful corrective, and not only about the eighteenth century. The conspiracy
imagination reliably selects the group that lost, was suppressed, and left a documentary
record — because a suppressed group with published papers is easier to build a story on
than a successful one that simply became the government.

---

### The collapse

The Gold- und Rosenkreuz did not last. By about 1787 the order had largely suspended its
activity, for reasons that included internal exhaustion, the death or withdrawal of key
figures, and the general collapse of confidence in high-degree systems that followed
Wilhelmsbad.

Its afterlife, however, is long.

Its nine grades — Zelator, Theoricus, Practicus, Philosophus and upward — reappear, with
modifications, in the Societas Rosicruciana in Anglia in 1866, and from there in the
Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn in 1888. The Golden Dawn's grade structure is
recognisably this order's, transmitted through nineteenth-century English Masonic
Rosicrucianism.[^4]

Its plates were reprinted and became part of the standard visual vocabulary of modern
occultism.

And its basic arrangement — Masonry as the outer court, an esoteric order as the inner —
became the template. The SRIA required Masonic membership. So, in its way, did the O.T.O.
The idea that Freemasonry is the antechamber to something more is not an ancient Masonic
doctrine. It is a marketing structure invented in Central Europe in the middle of the
eighteenth century, and it has never gone out of use.

---

### The verdict

**Documented.** An alchemical and Rosicrucian order, active from the 1750s, reorganised
1777, requiring Masonic membership, teaching a graded esoteric system, reaching state power
in Prussia in the later 1780s, and transmitting its grade structure into the nineteenth-
century occult revival.

**Not documented.** Any descent from a seventeenth-century Rosicrucian organisation, because
there was none.

**The direction of influence: outward from Masonry, not inward.**

We have now examined four eighteenth-century cases — Ramsay's chivalric genealogy, von
Hund's Templars, Martinez's theurgy, and the Prussian Rosicrucians — and in every one the
Masonic contribution is the same. Not doctrine. Not antiquity. Not secret knowledge.

A structure. A graded, secret, initiatory, portable structure, available to anyone who
wanted to organise men around a teaching.

There is one more group to whom it became available in this century, and they were not
supposed to have it at all.
