# CHAPTER FORTY-TWO
## Kabbalah Reconstructed

Take a deck of tarot cards. Find the twenty-two trumps — the Fool, the Magician, the High
Priestess, and so on to the World. Now take the Tree of Life: ten Sephiroth connected by
twenty-two paths.

Twenty-two and twenty-two.

Assign each trump to a path. Assign each path a Hebrew letter, since the Hebrew alphabet
also has twenty-two letters. Now assign the four suits to the four elements, and the court
cards to the elemental subdivisions, and the numbered cards to the Sephiroth in the four
worlds. Add the planets and the zodiac, which distribute across the letters according to a
scheme in the *Sefer Yetzirah*. Add colours, in four scales. Add gods, from every pantheon.
Add perfumes, plants, stones, animals, and diseases.

You now have a system in which everything corresponds to everything else, in which any
symbol can be located on a map, and in which a practitioner can construct a ritual for any
purpose by assembling the appropriate column.

It is magnificent. It is one of the most complete symbolic architectures ever built.

And essentially all of it was constructed between 1854 and 1900, by about five people.

---

### The chain, restated

Chapter 10 laid out the links. They are worth having in front of us again, because this
chapter is where they arrive.

**Jewish Kabbalah** — the *Zohar*, the *Sefer Yetzirah*, Luria — developed within Jewish
law, liturgy and community, from the twelfth century onward.

**Pico, 1486**, extracts material for Christian apologetic use.

**Reuchlin, 1494 and 1517**, makes Christian Cabala a discipline.

**Agrippa, 1533**, folds it into operative magic as Book Three of the *Occult Philosophy*.

**Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–84**, publishes *Kabbala Denudata*, a Latin anthology — the
decisive act of transmission, because it makes the material available to people who cannot
read Hebrew.

**Mathers, 1887**, publishes *The Kabbalah Unveiled* in English. He is translating Knorr's
Latin. He says so.

**The Golden Dawn, 1888–1900**, builds the system above on it.

Seven stages. Every one documented. Every one a book you can hold.

---

### What was added at the last stage

The Golden Dawn's additions are the substance of Hermetic Qabalah, and they have no Jewish
precedent. It is worth being specific.

**The tarot attribution.** Lévi proposed the twenty-two/twenty-two correspondence in the
1850s. The Golden Dawn made it operational, assigning specific trumps to specific paths —
and, crucially, adopting an attribution that differs from Lévi's at two points, most
famously in the placing of the Emperor and the Star. The order of the trumps on the Tree is
a Victorian editorial decision and it was argued about.

**The elemental and planetary system.** The four elements, the seven planets and the twelve
signs mapped onto the letters and paths, with the "Rose Cross" diagram binding them
together.

**The colour scales.** Four scales of colour, one per Kabbalistic world, assigned to every
Sephirah and path, used for visualisation and talismanic work. This is entirely the Order's
own.

**The grade system as ascent.** Each initiatory grade corresponds to a Sephirah, so that
advancing through the Order is, formally, climbing the Tree. This is the fusion point: a
*Masonic* structure of degrees mapped onto a *Kabbalistic* diagram. Neither tradition
contains it. It is made where they meet.

**The Egyptian, Greek and Hindu attributions.** God-names from every available pantheon
assigned to positions on the Tree, on the *prisca theologia* assumption that all
mythologies encode one system.

---

### What a Kabbalist would say

I want to put the objection plainly, because it is usually either ignored or made
aggressively, and it deserves neither.

From the standpoint of the Jewish tradition, Hermetic Qabalah is not a variant or a
development. It is a different thing that has taken the name.

The *sefirot* in Jewish Kabbalah are aspects of divine manifestation, held within a
theology, a liturgical practice and a legal framework. They are not a filing system for
correspondences. The letters are the substance of creation within an account of revelation,
not an indexing scheme for tarot cards. Lurianic *tikkun* — the gathering of the scattered
sparks — is a theology of exile and repair, written by and for a community that had been
expelled from Spain. The practice is inseparable from Torah, from Hebrew, from the
commandments, and from a people.

Detach the diagram from all of that, assign Egyptian gods to it, hang a card game on its
paths, and use it to structure the grades of a Victorian magical society, and you have
produced something the tradition's own practitioners would not recognise as theirs.

That is a fair criticism and it is correct.

The complicating fact is that the appropriation was not clandestine. Mathers named his
source in his title. The Golden Dawn's members were, some of them, genuinely learned in the
Hebrew literature available to them. And the chain runs back, link by link, to real Jewish
texts, however transformed.

So it is neither theft in the crude sense nor inheritance in the claimed sense. It is what
Chapter 10 called it: a real lineage delivering, at the far end, a different object.

**The correct formulation, and the one this book will hold to: Hermetic Qabalah is a
nineteenth-century Western esoteric system, built from Christian Cabalistic materials at
several removes from their Jewish sources, and it should never be described as ancient,
Jewish, or a preservation of anything.**

---

### Why the disguise?

Which brings the question this chapter is really about.

The Golden Dawn's system is an achievement. Whatever one thinks of magic, the construction
of a complete, internally consistent symbolic architecture capable of organising the whole
of Western religious imagery is a serious intellectual feat, and it was performed by
identifiable people in about a decade.

Why did they not say so?

Why the Cipher Manuscripts, the German adept, the Rosicrucian descent, the claim to be
transmitting rather than making?

Three answers, and I think all three are true.

**Because authority in that period required antiquity.** This is the deepest reason and it
runs through the whole book. In a culture formed by the *prisca theologia* — where truth is
old, one, and scattered — a system announced as new is announced as false. Novelty was not
a selling point; it was a disqualification. The founders needed a pedigree not to deceive
but to be *credible*, including to themselves.

**Because the material felt found rather than made.** Anyone who has assembled a large
system knows the experience of discovering that pieces fit. When the twenty-two trumps
match the twenty-two paths, it does not feel like a coincidence you have exploited. It
feels like a structure you have uncovered. The step from *this fits* to *this was always
here* is very short, and a person can take it in complete sincerity.

**And because the initiatory form demands it.** A graded order needs superiors. If the
Chiefs are simply three men in London who wrote the rituals last year, the grades confer
nothing but each other's opinion. Someone must be above them, or the ladder has no top.

That third reason is structural and it is why this keeps happening. Von Hund needed Unknown
Superiors. Blavatsky needed Mahatmas. Westcott needed Fräulein Sprengel. Mathers needed
Secret Chiefs. None of them could produce one. Every one of their organisations broke on the
demand.

**An initiatory hierarchy requires an ultimate authority, and if there is no real one, one
will be manufactured, and the manufacture will eventually be discovered.**

That is as close to a law as this book is prepared to state.

There is one figure who saw the problem clearly and solved it in the only way available. He
did not claim a German adept or a Tibetan master. He claimed that a discarnate intelligence
had dictated a book to him in Cairo in 1904, that he himself was its prophet, and that the
authority was therefore his.

It is not a solution most people can use. But it is, at least, an honest answer to the
question of where the ladder ends.
