# NOTE — ALCHEMY

**Against the psychological reduction.** Principe and Newman have shown that early
modern alchemy was substantially laboratory chemistry pursuing real material goals with
real apparatus, and that the "purely spiritual alchemy" reading is largely a
nineteenth- and twentieth-century construction (Atwood, Waite, later Jung). The book
follows them: laboratory and allegory coexisted in the same practitioners, and reading
the allegory as a code for psychotherapy is an anachronism.

**Vocabulary:** prima materia; sulphur–mercury–salt; *solve et coagula*; nigredo,
albedo, rubedo; putrefaction; the philosophers' stone; *Deckname* (cover-name) practice.

**The tempting parallel, and why it fails as evidence.** The third degree stages a
death and a raising. Alchemy stages a putrefaction and a rebirth. The resemblance is
genuine and it is the single most-cited "proof" of an alchemical Masonry. It fails the
transmission test: no document connects the composition of the Hiramic legend to
alchemical literature, the legend's own materials are biblical and craft-legendary, and
death-and-rebirth is among the most widely distributed ritual patterns on earth — which
makes it useless as a marker of specific descent.

**Where alchemy genuinely enters Masonry:** the Gold- und Rosenkreuz, 1760s–80s, where
it is explicit, doctrinal, and datable — and where Masonic membership is the entry
requirement. That is the real story, and it is better than the speculative one.

**Ashmole caution:** Elias Ashmole was an alchemist and a Freemason. He is evidence that
one man could be both. He is not evidence of a channel between the two.
