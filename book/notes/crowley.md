# NOTE — ALEISTER CROWLEY

**Why he belongs here.** Not for sensation. Crowley is the terminus of the line this
book traces: a man who received the Golden Dawn's Masonic-derived initiatory machinery,
rebuilt it twice (A∴A∴, 1907; O.T.O. under Reuss's charter, 1912), and articulated more
explicitly than anyone before him what the machinery was *for* — "the Science and Art of
causing Change to occur in conformity with Will."

**Masonic status — handled with precision, not polemic.** Crowley held initiations of
irregular and fringe-Masonic provenance and wrote about Masonry constantly and with
real knowledge. The O.T.O.'s degree ladder is explicitly modelled on Masonic high
degrees via Reuss. Regular Grand Lodges did not recognise the status he claimed.
**The book states all of this and refuses to adjudicate the fraternal question**, which
is jurisdictional, not historical.

**The category test.** Crowley is the reason the six-category framework exists. He is
simultaneously: an occult appropriator of Masonic form (A∴A∴), a fringe-Masonic office
holder (O.T.O.), a co-Masonic initiate, a sharp reader of Masonic symbolism, and a
figure whom conspiracy literature uses to indict Freemasonry as a whole. Collapse any
two of those and the history becomes nonsense.

**Content:** Thelema; *Liber AL vel Legis* (1904); sexual magic (Urban, 2006);
Egyptian symbolism as nineteenth-century Egyptology refracted through ritual; Kabbalah
as inherited from Mathers.

**Scholarship:** Bogdan & Starr (OUP, 2012); Pasi (2014); Kaczynski (2010).

**The closing line of the argument.** Crowley's Masonry is not Freemasonry's magic. It
is what happened after a three-hundred-year-old ritual technology passed out of the
hands of the people who built it — which is, in the end, what this book is about.
