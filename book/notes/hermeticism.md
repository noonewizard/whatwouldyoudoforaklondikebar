# NOTE — HERMETICISM

**Four things the word names, never to be merged:** (1) the Greek *Corpus Hermeticum*,
2nd–3rd c. CE, Roman Egypt; (2) the technical Hermetica — astrology, alchemy, magic,
often older and more practical; (3) Renaissance Hermetism after Ficino's Latin
*Pimander* of 1471; (4) "Hermeticism" as a modern occult self-description.

**The central event: 1614.** Isaac Casaubon demonstrates on linguistic grounds that the
*Corpus* is post-Christian, not Mosaic-era Egyptian. The scholarly case is decisive and
the cultural effect is almost nil. **This is the book's founding case study in the
survival of a tradition after the destruction of its credentials** — and the model for
understanding every later Masonic and occult genealogy.

**Core themes that travel:** microcosm/macrocosm; correspondence; spiritual ascent
through spheres; the divinity of *nous*; nature as legible.

**The Masonic question.** No documented transmission into the Craft before the
eighteenth century. What is documented is *availability*: Agrippa, Fludd, the Latin
Hermetica were in circulation, and men like Ashmole owned such books. Availability is
opportunity, not influence — and the Butler method (reconstruct the actual library)
is the only way to move from one to the other.

**Where it genuinely enters:** the high degrees from the 1740s, the Gold- und
Rosenkreuz, and above all the nineteenth century, where "Hermetic" becomes the
self-description of the Golden Dawn — an order founded in 1888 naming itself after a
tradition it reconstructed from print.
