# NOTE — THE OCCULT REVIVAL

**The pivot: Éliphas Lévi (Alphonse-Louis Constant, 1810–1875).** Seminarian, socialist
pamphleteer (*La Bible de la liberté*, 1841), imprisoned, then from 1854 the author of
*Dogme et rituel de la haute magie*. **Strube's revision (2016)** shows his magic
emerging out of 1840s French socialism and Catholic heterodoxy — occultism as a
nineteenth-century political-religious formation, not the retrieval of an older one.
Everything downstream — Pike, the Golden Dawn, Blavatsky's borrowings, Crowley —
depends on a synthesis barely older than the men using it.

**The institutional pipeline, documented:**
Craft Masonry → **SRIA (1866, Masonic membership required)** → Westcott, Mathers,
Woodman → **Golden Dawn (1888)** → A∴A∴ (1907) and, via Reuss's fringe-Masonic
lineage, **O.T.O.** → Thelema → (via Gardner) modern Wicca.

**The parallel pipeline:** Theosophy (1875) → Besant → **Co-Masonry**, carrying Masonic
ritual into a theosophical frame and into the international women's movement.

**Spiritualism and Mesmerism** supply the mass audience and the experiential vocabulary
from 1848 onward, without which the revival has no public.

**Taxil.** Gabriel Jogand-Pagès, 1886–1897: the Palladium, Diana Vaughan, the forged
Pike letter printed by de la Rive (1894). Public confession, Paris, 19 April 1897;
printed in *Le Frondeur*, 25 April 1897. The hoax has outlived its confession by more
than a century — the book's central demonstration of how false history acquires
momentum independent of evidence.

**The finding that matters.** This is the **strongest documented connection in the
entire book**, and it runs in the direction almost nobody expects: *out* of Freemasonry
into occultism, in the nineteenth century, through named men with dated memberships.
