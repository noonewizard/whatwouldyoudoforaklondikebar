# CONTROVERSIAL-CLAIM MATRIX

Each claim is processed under the eight-step protocol: (1) state accurately;
(2) earliest traceable appearance; (3) promoters; (4) primary evidence; (5) scholarly
interpretations; (6) chronological problems; (7) what remains demonstrable;
(8) what remains speculative.

Status values: **Unsupported · Speculative · Disputed · Mixed · Strong · Established**

---

## 1. Freemasonry descends from the ancient Egyptian mysteries
- **Earliest appearance:** 18th c. — Ramsay's ambient antiquarianism; explicit in
  Cagliostro's Egyptian Rite (1784–85); systematised by Oliver (1820s), then by
  Blavatsky, Pike and Hall.
- **Promoters:** Cagliostro; George Oliver; Blavatsky; Manly P. Hall.
- **Evidence for:** symbolic parallels (pillars, light, ordeal, solar imagery).
- **Evidence against:** no transmission document; a gap of over a millennium; the
  "Egyptian" content derives from Ficino's *Pimander* and Renaissance Egyptomania, and
  Casaubon dated the Hermetica to the Christian era in **1614**.
- **Status: UNSUPPORTED.** Demonstrable residue: 18th–19th-century Masons *believed*
  it, and that belief is itself a major historical fact. **Chapters 6, 28, 45.**

## 2. Freemasonry descends from the Knights Templar
- **Earliest appearance:** Ramsay's *Oration* (1736/37) gestures at crusader orders;
  the explicit Templar-descent system is von Hund's Strict Observance (1754 onward).
- **Evidence against:** 400-year documentary void; the Wilhelmsbad Convent (1782) —
  composed of the claim's own adherents — repudiated it; von Hund's "Unknown
  Superiors" never materialised.
- **Status: UNSUPPORTED** as descent; **ESTABLISHED** as an 18th-century invention with
  traceable authors and motives. **Chapters 26, 45.**

## 3. Rosicrucianism was an existing secret order that became Freemasonry
- **Earliest appearance:** 18th-c. conflation; canonical in Waite (1924) and after.
- **Evidence against:** the manifestos (1614–16) describe a fictional brotherhood;
  Andreae called the *Chymical Wedding* a *ludibrium*; the real institutional
  Rosicrucianism (Gold- und Rosenkreuz, 1760s–80s) is *later* than Grand Lodge and
  *required Masonic membership* — the influence runs the other way.
- **Status: UNSUPPORTED** as origin; **STRONG** in reverse: Masonry carried
  Rosicrucianism into the modern world. **Chapters 12, 24, 45.**

## 4. Freemasonry preserves genuine ancient Kabbalistic teaching
- **Earliest appearance:** high-degree rituals from the 1740s; systematised by Lévi
  (1850s), Pike (1871), Mathers (1887).
- **Evidence against:** what reaches Masonry is *Christian* Kabbalah at several
  removes — Pico → Reuchlin → Knorr's *Kabbala Denudata* (1677–84) → Mathers's English
  version (1887) — reworked into what scholars call **Hermetic Qabalah**, whose tarot
  and elemental correspondences have no Jewish precedent.
- **Status: MIXED.** Real textual lineage, radically transformed content. The book must
  never let "Kabbalah" stand unqualified. **Chapters 10, 36.**

## 5. Albert Pike taught Luciferian doctrine
- **Earliest appearance:** de la Rive (1894), from Taxil's fabrications (1886–97).
- **Primary evidence:** the "letter to the 23 Supreme Councils" is a forgery. Taxil
  confessed publicly on 19 April 1897; *Le Frondeur*, 25 April 1897.
- **What remains:** Pike *did* write about Lucifer as light-bearer and about Baphomet,
  in a comparative-mythological register, much of it lifted from Lévi (de Hoyos's
  annotated edn., 2011).
- **Status: UNSUPPORTED** as charged; the underlying passages are **ESTABLISHED** and
  deserve close reading rather than denial. **Chapters 31–33, 46.**

## 6. The Bavarian Illuminati survived and control world events
- **Earliest appearance:** Robison and Barruel, 1797–98.
- **Evidence against:** the order was suppressed 1784–87 and its own papers published
  by the Bavarian state in 1787; no post-1790 institutional continuity.
- **Status: UNSUPPORTED.** What is **ESTABLISHED**: the Illuminati existed, infiltrated
  lodges, and terrified governments — and the *conspiracy tradition itself* is a
  datable 1790s counter-revolutionary product (Roberts, 1972). **Chapters 23, 46.**

## 7. The Priory of Sion and a Merovingian bloodline
- **Earliest appearance:** Pierre Plantard's association, registered in France in 1956;
  forged *dossiers secrets* deposited in the Bibliothèque nationale in the 1960s;
  popularised 1982 and 2003.
- **Status: UNSUPPORTED — documented modern fabrication.** Useful as a controlled
  experiment in how fast a fake lineage can become "common knowledge." **Chapter 46.**

## 8. Masonic ritual encodes real knowledge of Solomon's Temple
- **Evidence against:** the ritual Temple derives from 1 Kings and 2 Chronicles as read
  through Renaissance reconstructions (Villalpando and others), not from any
  independent tradition; archaeology of the First Temple is famously scant.
- **Status: UNSUPPORTED** archaeologically; **STRONG** as literary-architectural
  genealogy. **Chapter 17.**

## 9. The Golden Dawn received authentic German Rosicrucian authority
- **Evidence against:** Howe (1972) on the "Sprengel" letters; Butler (2011) on what
  was actually in Westcott's and Hockley's libraries — the material the founders
  demonstrably had to hand accounts for the Ciphers without a German order.
- **Status: UNSUPPORTED** as warrant; **ESTABLISHED** as a creative synthesis.
  **Chapter 35.**

## 10. Crowley's Thelema is "Masonic"
- **Evidence:** O.T.O. was built on a fringe-Masonic high-degree template (Reuss);
  Crowley held irregular and co-Masonic memberships and wrote about Masonry constantly.
- **Against:** no regular Grand Lodge recognised his Masonic status in the form he
  claimed; A∴A∴ is Golden Dawn-derived, not Masonic.
- **Status: MIXED — and precisely the kind of case the book's category distinctions
  exist to handle.** **Chapter 37.**

## 11. Masonry controls governments / D.C.'s occult street geometry
- **Earliest appearance:** the governmental claim, 1790s; the D.C. geometry claim,
  20th c. (and chiefly post-1980 popular publishing).
- **Evidence against:** L'Enfant's plan follows Baroque planning convention; the
  "pentagram" requires selective street inclusion.
- **What is true and more interesting:** Masonic membership among 18th–19th-c. American
  elites was genuinely high and genuinely consequential for civic culture (Bullock,
  1996) — a documented sociological fact that conspiracism replaces with a worse story.
- **Status: UNSUPPORTED** as control; **ESTABLISHED** as elite network. **Chapters 40, 46.**

## 12. Freemasonry influenced the nineteenth-century occult revival
- **Evidence:** SRIA's Masonic entry requirement (1866); the Golden Dawn founders'
  Masonic careers; the O.T.O.'s degree architecture; Co-Masonry; Pike's dependence on
  Lévi and Lévi's reception by Masons.
- **Status: STRONG — the best-evidenced connection in the entire book,** and the one
  that runs in the opposite direction from the popular assumption. **Chapters 29–37.**

## 13. Masonic ritual produces genuine psychological transformation
- **Status: PLAUSIBLE, UNDER-EVIDENCED HISTORICALLY.** Handled strictly under the
  Chapter XXI claim-type discipline: what participants reported, what ritual theory
  predicts, what sociology shows — never a metaphysical assertion, never a retroactive
  diagnosis. **Chapters 42, 44.**

## 14. "The Craft was always esoteric — the exposures prove the secrets were real"
- **Counter-observation:** the printed exposures (1723–1762) show that the informational
  content was thin and rapidly public, while the *institution* survived perfectly well.
- **Status: DISPUTED, resolvable.** The secret's function was social and performative,
  not informational — which is why publication never destroyed it. **Chapters 3, 40.**
