# PRIMARY SOURCES — WORKING NOTES

The full citations live in `citations/bibliography.md` (entries 1–95). This file
records **what each body of primary material can and cannot be used to prove.**

## The Old Charges (Regius c. 1390; Cooke c. 1410; 100+ later copies)
**Proves:** that the masons' craft carried a legendary history running through Euclid
and Solomon's Temple, centuries before any speculative lodge existed.
**Does not prove:** any esoteric content. The Temple in the Old Charges is a guild
origin story, not a mystery.

## The Scottish record (Schaw Statutes 1598/99; Edinburgh and Aitchison's Haven minutes)
**Proves:** a permanent, statutory, minute-keeping lodge system in Scotland from 1598,
admitting non-operatives from 1600.
**Does not prove:** Renaissance esoteric content. Stevenson's memory-art connection is
inference from Schaw's court milieu, not from the statutes' text.

## The English evidence (Ashmole 1646/1682; Plot 1686; Aubrey 1686)
**Proves:** non-operative "accepted" Masonry existed in England by the 1640s; that
learned men with alchemical and antiquarian interests joined it.
**Does not prove:** that they joined *because* of those interests. Ashmole never says
why. This silence is Unresolved Question no. 2.

## The catechisms (Edinburgh Register House 1696; Chetwode Crawley; Kevan)
**Proves:** the earliest recoverable ritual content — two degrees, a word, a grip, a
set of questions.
**Does not prove:** continuity with any earlier tradition, or with later ritual.

## The printed Constitutions (Anderson 1723, 1738; Dermott 1756; Preston 1772)
**Proves:** the official self-presentation of the institution at datable moments, and
the ideology it wished to project.
**Does not prove:** what happened in lodges. Anderson 1738 in particular is a
*retrospective, interested* source — the sole authority for 1717.

## The exposures (1723–1762, English and French)
**Proves:** the ritual content in circulation at datable moments; the emergence of the
third degree and the Hiramic legend by 1730.
**Does not prove:** accuracy in detail. Exposures were commercial, sometimes hostile,
sometimes padded. Cross-check between them, and against the catechisms.
**Also proves, powerfully:** that publication of the secrets did not destroy the
institution — the central evidence for Chapter 23 and Chapter 46.

## The esoteric primary texts (Hermetica, Agrippa, Knorr, the manifestos, etc.)
**Proves:** what was available to read, and when, and in what language.
**Does not prove:** that any Mason read it. Availability is opportunity, not
transmission. The Butler method — reconstructing actual libraries — is the corrective.

## Nineteenth-century occult texts (Lévi, Blavatsky, Mathers, Pike, Crowley)
**Proves:** what these authors wrote and when — and, with the de Hoyos apparatus, who
copied whom.
**Does not prove:** that their claims about earlier periods are true. These are
**primary sources for their own century and secondary (usually unreliable) sources for
every earlier one.** This distinction is enforced throughout.

## Anti-Masonic and conspiracy primaries (Barruel, Robison, Taxil, de la Rive)
**Proves:** the existence, dating and content of the accusation.
**Does not prove:** anything about Freemasonry. Used exclusively as evidence of belief.
