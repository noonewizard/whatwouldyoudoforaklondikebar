# SECONDARY SOURCES — WORKING NOTES

Full citations in `citations/bibliography.md` (entries 96–174). This file records
**what each major work is for, and where it must be used with caution.**

## Foundational for Masonic history
- **Bogdan & Snoek, *Handbook of Freemasonry* (Brill, 2014).** The reference spine of
  the project; 669 pp., four sections, chapter bibliographies. First stop for every
  Masonic chapter.
- **Stevenson, *Origins of Freemasonry* (CUP, 1988).** The Scottish thesis. Use for the
  institutional argument; flag the memory-art connection as inference.
- **Berman, *Foundations of Modern Freemasonry* (2012).** The London 1714–40 circle.
  Strong on personnel and politics.
- **Jacob, *Living the Enlightenment* (OUP, 1991).** Sociability, civil society, gender
  in continental lodges.
- **Bullock, *Revolutionary Brotherhood* (UNC, 1996).** The American standard.
- **Prescott & Sommers (2017–18).** The 1717/1721 revision. **[SVR on citation]**
- **Snoek, *Initiating Women in Freemasonry* (Brill, 2012).** The Adoption Rite, from
  ~130 manuscripts and printings. Indispensable and without rival.
- **Hinks & Kantrowitz, eds., *All Men Free and Brethren* (Cornell, 2013).** Prince Hall
  scholarship; Sesay's chapter is the key text for Chapter 32.
- **Harland-Jacobs, *Builders of Empire* (UNC, 2007).** Masonry as imperial network.

## Foundational for Western esotericism
- **Hanegraaff, *Esotericism and the Academy* (CUP, 2012).** The field's
  self-examination; the argument that "esotericism" is a container built by its
  opponents. Governs the book's Chapter 2 and Chapter 51.
- **Bogdan, *Western Esotericism and Rituals of Initiation* (SUNY, 2007).** The single
  most important theoretical source for this book's thesis.
- **Faivre (1994, 2010); Goodrick-Clarke (2008).** Typology and survey; use for
  orientation, not for causal claims.
- **Copenhaver, *Hermetica* (CUP, 1992) and *Magic in Western Culture* (CUP, 2015).**
  The corrective to Yates.
- **Yates (1964, 1966, 1972).** Brilliant, foundational, and substantially superseded.
  **Cite as historiography, not as evidence** — with the correction stated in-text.
- **Scholem; Idel; Dan; Coudert.** Kabbalah, in that order of use.
- **Principe; Newman & Principe; Moran.** Alchemy as chemistry-and-more.
- **McIntosh, *The Rose Cross and the Age of Reason* (Brill 1992 / SUNY 2011).** The
  Gold- und Rosenkreuz; the best evidence for magic entering via Masonic structure.
- **Strube (De Gruyter, 2016).** Lévi's socialist-Catholic genealogy. The most important
  recent revision affecting Part VI.
- **Butler, *Victorian Occultism* (Palgrave, 2011).** The library method. Model for
  Chapter 41.
- **Howe (1972); Gilbert (1983, 1987); Owen (2004).** Golden Dawn.
- **Bogdan & Starr, eds., *Aleister Crowley and Western Esotericism* (OUP, 2012);
  Pasi (2014); Kaczynski (2010); Urban (2006).** Crowley.
- **Introvigne, *Satanism: A Social History* (Brill, 2016).** Taxil, Palladism, and the
  Pike accusation in their proper context.

## Method and theory
- **van Gennep; Turner; Bell; Whitehouse; McCauley & Lawson.** Ritual. Used as lenses,
  never as proof, and never retro-applied to individuals.
- **Simmel (1906); Urban (1998).** Secrecy.
- **Hobsbawm & Ranger (1983); Grafton (1990).** Invented tradition; forgery.
- **Roberts (1972).** Conspiracy historiography — still unsurpassed.
- **Styers (2004); Otto & Stausberg (2013).** The category "magic."

## USED WITH EXPLICIT CAUTION
- **Schuchard, *Restoring the Temple of Vision* (Brill, 2002).** Enormous erudition,
  weak transmission evidence; reviewers have said so. Mine for leads and for the primary
  material it surfaces. **Never load-bearing.**
- **Yates.** As above.
- **Manly P. Hall; Baigent/Leigh/Lincoln; the popular Templar literature.** Level D.
  Cited only as evidence that a belief exists, and labelled as such *in the text*.
