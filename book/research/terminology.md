# TERMINOLOGY CONTROL FILE

Binding usage rules. Read before drafting every chapter. Violations are caught by the
esoteric audit.

---

## THE SIX CATEGORIES (never collapsed)

1. **Historical Freemasonry** — what lodges, constitutions, members and records show.
2. **Masonic esotericism** — symbolic, allegorical and initiatory readings developed
   *within* Masonic traditions.
3. **Occult Freemasonry** — Masonic systems explicitly tied to magical, Hermetic,
   Kabbalistic, theurgical or alchemical practice (Élus Coëns; Gold- und Rosenkreuz).
4. **Co-Masonic / fringe Masonry** — bodies using Masonic forms outside mainstream
   regular recognition (Le Droit Humain; Reuss's rites).
5. **Occult appropriation of Masonry** — borrowing of Masonic structure by non-Masonic
   orders (Golden Dawn; O.T.O.; SRIA as a hybrid).
6. **Conspiracy theory** — claims exceeding the evidence.

---

## THE FIVE CLAIM-TYPES (never silently exchanged)

| Type | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Historical | "Practitioners believed X." | Always permitted where sourced. |
| Phenomenological | "Practitioners reported experiencing X." | Requires a first-person source. |
| Psychological | "Ritual may produce effect Y." | Hedged; theory, not diagnosis. |
| Sociological | "Ritual creates structure Z." | Evidence from records. |
| Metaphysical | "Mechanism X objectively exists." | **Never asserted by the narrator.** |

## CONTROLLED VOCABULARY

**Kabbalah / Cabala / Qabalah.** Three words, three referents, no interchange:
- *Kabbalah* — Jewish mystical tradition (*Sefer Yetzirah*, *Zohar*, Luria).
- *Christian Cabala* — Pico, Reuchlin, Knorr: Jewish material read for Christian ends.
- *Hermetic Qabalah* — the nineteenth/twentieth-century occult system (Lévi, Mathers,
  Golden Dawn, Crowley), with tarot and elemental correspondences **absent from Jewish
  sources**. Never call it "ancient."

**Hermeticism.** Not one unchanged tradition. Distinguish (a) the Greek *Corpus
Hermeticum* of the 2nd–3rd c. CE; (b) *technical* Hermetica (astrology, alchemy,
magic); (c) Renaissance Hermetism after Ficino's 1471 *Pimander*; (d) "Hermeticism" as
a modern occult label. Always signal which.

**Rosicrucianism.** Distinguish (a) the 1614–16 manifestos; (b) the seventeenth-century
furore over a brotherhood that left no members; (c) the eighteenth-century Gold- und
Rosenkreuz, a real order; (d) nineteenth-century revivals (SRIA); (e) modern bodies
claiming descent. Never assume (a) implies an organisation.

**Magic.** Always qualified: natural, celestial, ceremonial, sympathetic, talismanic,
angelic, planetary, theurgic, thaumaturgic. Reserve *Magick* for Crowley's usage and
its descendants. Never let the word stand bare as an analytic category.

**Theurgy.** Iamblichan ritual work with the divine — self-consciously *distinguished*
by its practitioners from *goeteia*. Never a synonym for "magic."

**Alchemy.** Never reduced to Jungian psychology. Distinguish laboratory *chymistry*
(Principe, Newman) from spiritual-allegorical alchemy, and note that early modern
practitioners frequently did both.

**Speculative / operative.** Use with care: the older "operative-became-speculative"
narrative is contested. Prefer describing what the records show.

**Regular / irregular / clandestine.** These are jurisdictional-political terms, not
historical judgments. When used, attribute them to the body using them. Applied to
Prince Hall Masonry they carry a racial history that must be stated, not implied.

**Degree / grade / rite / order / obedience.** Fixed usage; define at first occurrence
in Ch. 3 and in Appendix B.

**"The Craft."** The three symbolic degrees. Not appendant bodies, and not Wicca's
later borrowing of the same word.

## PROHIBITED CONSTRUCTIONS

- "Scholars generally agree…" — name the scholars, or cut.
- "Historians have proven…" — unless the evidence genuinely is that strong.
- "Ancient wisdom preserved unchanged…" — never in the narrator's voice.
- "It is said that…" — attribute or delete.
- "The Masons believed…" — which Masons, where, when?
- "This dates back to time immemorial…" — the phrase is a Masonic formula; quote it as
  one, never use it as a statement of fact.
- Any parallel presented as descent without a transmission document.

---

## SIGNALLING LITERARY RECONSTRUCTION

Where the prose reconstructs an atmosphere rather than reporting a source, it must be
marked in the text — by an explicit frame ("The record does not tell us how the room
smelled. It tells us…"), by conditional grammar, or by a note. Never by tone alone.
