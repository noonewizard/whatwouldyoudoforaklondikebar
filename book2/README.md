## The hypothesis under test

> Freemasonry may be understood not as a system that teaches magical spells, but as a ritual
> architecture designed to transform the participant through controlled experience, symbolic
> compression, embodied action, moral instruction, secrecy and progressive revelation.

**Tagged honestly:** the *effects* are E3 (plausible). The *intentional design* is E4–E5
(contested to speculative). Masonic ritual is a technology in the sense that a violin is —
refined over generations by people optimising for an effect they could recognise but could
not explain. Not in the sense that a circuit is.

## Standing rules

1. **Five claim-types separate** — historical, phenomenological, psychological,
   sociological, metaphysical. The narrator asserts no metaphysical claim, in either
   direction.
2. **Five magic-types classified** — practitioner, scholarly, anthropological,
   psychological, metaphysical. Never blurred.
3. **Parallel is not descent.** Carried from Book I.
4. **Contested findings reported with their contestation.** Aronson and Mills (1959) and
   Meyers-Levy and Zhu (2007) are flagged ⚠ throughout so no chapter can lean on them quietly.
5. **No retroactive diagnosis.** Nothing claimed about what any named historical person
   experienced.
6. **Every modern framework carries its inferential distance** in the sentence applying it.
7. **Never fabricate.** `[PAGE VERIFICATION REQUIRED]` stands wherever a particular could
   not be verified.
8. **Respect fraternal obligation.** No private ritual material reproduced or invented;
   published sources 1723–1940 only; jurisdictional variation stated; no working described
   is current or universal.
