## THE ARGUMENT

The book proceeds as a descent into a Temple in twelve parts and returns.

**Findings, at the strength the evidence supports** (fully tagged in Appendix E §E.5):

- Masonic initiation employs sensory restriction, disorientation, staged death, public
  obligation and progressive disclosure. **E1.**
- These techniques appear independently in unrelated societies, and the best explanation is
  convergence on effective design rather than common descent. **E2 / E3.**
- Craft ritual contains no operative magical apparatus. The occult reading enters the record
  after Lévi (1854–56), reaching Masonry chiefly through Pike in 1871. **E1 / E2.**
- Lodge records show mass lapse and exclusion: most initiates were not durably transformed.
  **E1.**
- **The unit of analysis is the career, not the ceremony.** **E3** — the book's central
  methodological claim, and the one it most wants tested.
- Whether supernatural operation occurs: **no evidence tag available, by design.**

**Thesis:** Freemasonry and ceremonial magic are not related by descent. They are related by
having independently discovered the same set of techniques for working on a human being.

## RELATION TO BOOK I

Book I (*The Carrier Flame*) asked **where did it come from?** and found that Freemasonry did
not inherit the Western magical tradition — it built the vehicle that carried it.

Book II asks **what does it do?** Where a question was settled in Book I, Book II states the
finding in a sentence and cites the earlier volume rather than re-arguing it.

## HONEST LIMITS

Stated here rather than buried, because the book's method requires it.

- **The evidentiary asymmetry (Appendix G §G.5).** Excellent evidence for what the ceremony
  was; moderate evidence for what participants believed it did; almost none for what it
  actually did to anyone. No measurements, no controls, no longitudinal data.
- **75 verification markers** remain, chiefly page numbers. The most consequential is
  Chapter 44 note 1: the lodge-attrition evidence, which carries the book's central
  disconfirming claim, rests on published summaries rather than archival work.
- **No load-bearing interpretive claim exceeds E3**, and Appendix E §E.6 states in advance
  what would falsify each of them.
