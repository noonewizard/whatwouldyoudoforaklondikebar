# APPENDIX A
## Masonic Symbol Dictionary

Each entry gives: the symbol; where it appears in ritual and furnishing; the interpretation
the ritual itself supplies (the *anchored* meaning); interpretations supplied from outside
the system (*unanchored* accretions); and the chapter where it is analyzed.

The anchored/unanchored distinction is the one set out in Chapter 20, following Peirce's
division of sign-types. An anchored meaning is one the ritual states or demonstrably
implies. An unanchored meaning is one imported by a later commentator. Both are real
historical facts about how the symbol has been used; only the first is evidence of what the
compilers intended.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Furnishing, wording, and in some cases the presence of a symbol
vary between constitutions. Nothing below should be taken as universal Masonic practice.

### A note on the second column

The unanchored column is not a list of errors to be dismissed. Some of these readings are
two centuries old and have shaped how members actually experience the symbols; a symbol's
meaning in practice includes what its users take it to mean. The column exists to keep two
different claims apart — *this is what the ritual says* and *this is what someone later said
about it* — because the literature routinely merges them, and because the second kind of
claim cannot be used as evidence of origin. See Chapter 40 on the occult mirror, and the
five-claim-type distinction in Chapter 2.
