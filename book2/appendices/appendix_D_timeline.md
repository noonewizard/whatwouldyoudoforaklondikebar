The right records the development of the disciplines this book uses to analyse it. Reading
them together makes visible a fact that is otherwise easy to miss: **almost every tool used
to explain what initiation does to a person was invented long after the ritual was.**

### What the two columns show

The Hiramic legend was in place by the 1730s. Van Gennep's account of what a rite of passage
does appeared in 1909; Turner's in 1969; Bell's critique of the category itself in 1992; the
cognitive science of ritual after 2000. The ritual precedes every analytic vocabulary used to
explain it by between one and three centuries.

Two consequences follow, and both are load-bearing for this book.

First, **no eighteenth-century compiler can have been applying any of these theories.** When
a modern commentator observes that the third degree exhibits a textbook separation-liminality-
incorporation structure, the observation may be correct and is still not evidence of design.
Chapter 9 works through the distinction.

Second, **the esoteric interpretation of Masonry is younger than it looks.** The vocabulary
in which Masonry is most often explained as magical — correspondences, astral planes, the
operator's will, Hermetic Qabalah — enters the historical record in and after 1854. Anything
written in that vocabulary is evidence about the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, not
about the eighteenth. Chapters 40–41.
