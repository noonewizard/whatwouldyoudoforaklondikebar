# APPENDIX E
## Evidence Classification, and the Status of the Book's Principal Claims

Every substantive claim in this book carries an internal evidence tag. The tags appear in the
body text only where they clarify rather than clutter. This appendix sets out the scale and
then applies it to the book's own conclusions, so that a reader can see exactly how much
weight each is being asked to bear.

---

## E.1 — THE SCALE

| Tag | Level | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | Direct primary evidence | A period document says so |
| **E2** | Strong scholarly reconstruction | Specialists converge on the reading from good evidence |
| **E3** | Plausible interpretation | Consistent with the evidence; alternatives exist |
| **E4** | Contested interpretation | Competent scholars disagree |
| **E5** | Speculative | Chronologically possible; no transmission or corroboration |
| **E6** | Unsupported | Fails the tests; often chronologically impossible |

## E.2 — THE MAGIC-TYPE CLASSIFICATION

Claims involving "magic" carry a second tag, because the word does five different jobs.

| Type | Sense | Status in this book |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Practitioner's sense — what magicians say they do | Reported, historically |
| **B** | Scholarly-historical — the Western esoteric currents as studied | Used |
| **C** | Anthropological — ritual efficacy as a social fact | Used |
| **D** | Psychological — effects on attention, memory, belief, identity | Used |
| **E** | Metaphysical — that supernatural operation is real | **Never asserted, never denied** |

Type E is outside the competence of historical and psychological method. The book neither
affirms nor refutes it; it declines the question, and says so each time the question arises.

## E.3 — THE SIX QUESTIONS FOR A TRANSMISSION CLAIM

1. **Provenance** — where does the document come from, and who vouches for it?
2. **Chronology** — does the proposed source predate the proposed recipient?
3. **Transmission** — is there a documented route, or only a possibility of one?
4. **Intertextuality** — is there verbal dependence, or only thematic resemblance?
5. **Institutional continuity** — did an organization persist, or is continuity inferred?
6. **Anachronism** — does the claim require someone to have known what was not yet known?

A claim failing question 2 is dead. A claim failing only question 4 may still be true but is
not yet evidenced.

## E.4 — THE FOUR QUESTIONS FOR A DISCOVERY CLAIM

New in this volume, because Book II deals with pattern-finding as well as transmission. Set
out in Chapter 38 and repeated in Appendix F: procedure specified in advance? search space
stated? survives a control? makes a falsifiable prediction?

## E.6 — WHAT WOULD FALSIFY THE MAIN ARGUMENT

Stated in advance, as the discipline of Chapter 38 requires of anyone else.

- **Row 2 fails** if an eighteenth-century Craft ritual text of good provenance is produced
  containing invocation, correspondence tables, or claimed operative results.
- **Row 3 fails** if pre-1850 Masonic sources are produced using the occult vocabulary in a
  sustained and non-idiosyncratic way.
- **Rows 7 and 20 fail** if a documented transmission route is established between a
  ceremonial-magical corpus and the compilers of the degrees — verbal dependence, not
  thematic resemblance.
- **Row 13 fails** if a controlled study shows large durable effects from a single initiatory
  event, independent of subsequent participation.
- **Row 17 fails** if a gematria procedure specified in advance yields significantly more
  structure from Masonic vocabulary than from a matched control corpus.

None of these has occurred. All of them could.
