# CHAPTER NINETEEN
## Number, Proportion, and the Great Architect

Three degrees. Three principal officers. Three great lights, three lesser lights, three
knocks, three steps, three grand masters at the building of the Temple.

Five orders of architecture, five noble orders, five points, five senses.

Seven liberal sciences. Seven required to make a perfect lodge, in some workings.

Twelve. Thirty-three.

The numbers are unmistakably there, and there is an entire literature devoted to explaining
what they encode. This chapter is about why almost all of that literature commits the same
error, and about the one diagnostic that identifies it.

---

### Over-determination

The error has a name in statistics and no name in the popular literature, which is part of
the problem. I will call it **over-determination**, and the test it generates is simple.

*Before treating a recurrence as evidence of a scheme, ask whether the recurrence already
has a sufficient explanation.*

Run the Masonic numbers through it.

**Three.** The Craft has three degrees because the trade had three grades: apprentice,
journeyman, master. That is the structure of every European guild, it is documented in the
Old Charges and the Schaw Statutes, and it requires no numerology whatever. It is also the
minimum number of stages that permits a middle — a beginning, a passage and an arrival — so
any graded system with an intermediate stage will have three.

**Five.** The five orders of architecture are Tuscan, Doric, Ionic, Corinthian and
Composite. That is not a mystical five; it is the canonical list from Renaissance
architectural theory, fixed by Serlio and Vignola in the sixteenth century, and known to
every educated person in eighteenth-century Europe. Five senses is the classical inheritance.

**Seven.** The seven liberal sciences are the medieval curriculum: trivium plus quadrivium.
They are in the Old Charges from 1390 because they were in every schoolroom.

**Thirty-three.** The Scottish Rite has thirty-three degrees because that is the number
arrived at when the Supreme Council was constituted at Charleston in 1801, consolidating
existing continental systems. There is no documented rationale beyond the systems being
consolidated.

**In every case there is an independently sufficient explanation, and it is prosaic.**

That does not prove no further meaning was intended. It means the recurrence carries no
evidential weight, because it is fully accounted for before numerology is invoked. A number
with a sufficient cause cannot also be evidence of a hidden cause. You have to show that the
sufficient explanation fails.

Nobody does. The numerological literature proceeds by listing the recurrences, which is the
one step that establishes nothing.

---

### Why we find the patterns anyway

Chapter 5 set out the reason and it applies here with particular force.

Small integers are not scarce. Any institution with a structure will have counts of things,
and the counts will be small numbers, and small numbers recur across all human traditions
because there are not many of them. Three, five and seven are the workhorses of every
symbolic system on earth.

Which means that a search for numerical correspondences between Freemasonry and any other
tradition **cannot fail**. There is no possible configuration of Masonic practice that would
not yield matches, and a procedure that cannot fail is not a test.

This is the multiple-comparisons problem in its simplest form, and Chapter 38 will run it
formally. Here it is enough to state the consequence: **the Masonic numbers are real, they
are used, they are not a code, and the reason we know they are not a code is that nobody has
ever proposed a scheme that could have come out otherwise.**

### Proportion

One related claim deserves separating out, because it is more respectable and gets less
attention.

Renaissance architectural theory genuinely did hold that proportion carried meaning — that
the harmonic ratios of music, the proportions of the human body, and the dimensions of
well-made buildings expressed a single order. Rudolf Wittkower's *Architectural Principles in
the Age of Humanism* established the scholarship on this, and it is not esoteric: it was
mainstream theory, taught and practised.[^1]

Villalpando's reconstruction of the Temple, which supplied Europe's visual idea of it, is
explicitly a proportional argument — the claim being that the Temple's proportions were
divinely revealed and that the classical orders are a degraded memory of them.

**So the idea that building proportions encode metaphysical order was available, respectable
and current in exactly the period when speculative Freemasonry was forming.**

Does it appear in Masonic ritual?

Essentially not. The Craft names the five orders and says they are to be studied. It does not
give ratios, does not derive them, and does not build anything. The proportional tradition is
adjacent to Freemasonry and was known to some of its members, and it did not enter the
ceremonial.

That is a small finding and it is characteristic. Time and again the esoteric material was
*available* and did not come in. Availability is not transmission, and Masonic ritual is
remarkably consistent in its refusal of content that was lying around.

### The Great Architect

Which brings us to the phrase, and it is the most misunderstood formula in Freemasonry.

The Craft refers to God as the Great Architect of the Universe. Anti-Masonic writing has
treated this as evidence of a deity other than the Christian one — a demiurge, a
nature-god, an occult construction. Masonic apologetics has sometimes treated it as simply a
polite name for God.

Both are wrong, and the truth is more interesting and entirely datable.

**The phrase belongs to the theological vocabulary of the period, not to Masonry.** Calvin
uses *architect* of God. The image of the divine geometer is medieval — the famous
frontispiece of the thirteenth-century *Bible moralisée* shows God leaning from heaven with a
pair of compasses, measuring the world — and its scriptural warrant is Proverbs 8:27, where
wisdom is present when he set a compass upon the face of the depth.

**Its Masonic function is subtractive.** Anderson's settlement of 1723 required a formula
that a Catholic, an Anglican, a Dissenter, a Jew and a deist could all use without any of
them conceding anything. "The Great Architect of the Universe" performs precisely that: it
affirms a creator and an order, and it specifies nothing else. No Trinity, no covenant, no
incarnation, no revelation.

It is, in other words, **a deliberate vacancy** — the theological equivalent of the undefined
symbol described in Chapter 14, and produced by the same institutional pressure.

**And it acquired its Newtonian resonance by good fortune.** The formula was in use before
Newton and would have been intelligible in 1500. But in the London of the 1720s, among men
who attended Desaguliers's demonstrations, "the Great Architect of the Universe" landed in a
culture that had just been shown that the universe obeys mathematical law. A phrase from
medieval devotional iconography arrived in a room full of Newtonians and meant something it
had not meant before.

That coincidence has been mistaken, repeatedly, for a doctrine.

### What Part IV establishes

Four chapters on geometry, and the finding is largely negative in a way I did not anticipate
when I began.

**Freemasonry does not do geometry.** It has no constructions, no proofs, no ratios, no
technique. It names one theorem and does not prove it.

**What it took from geometry was an epistemology, not a content.** Demonstration rather than
assertion; standards that do not consult the observer; certainty available without shared
belief. Those properties, transferred to conduct, are the whole moral architecture of the
Craft.

**And it took a vocabulary of shapes that crosses languages.** A square is a square in every
tongue, which is why the system travelled.

The numbers are prosaic. The proportional tradition did not come in. The Great Architect is a
vacancy.

What remains is a set of instruments, an ethical posture, and one theorem that happens to be
true.

That is less than the literature claims and more than I expected, and it is the last of the
Craft's daylight symbolism. Everything from here is darker: light, and then death.
