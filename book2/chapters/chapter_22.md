# CHAPTER TWENTY-TWO
## The Eye and the Letter G

Two symbols, and between them they account for most of what the general public believes
about Freemasonry.

One of them is not Masonic in origin and never was. The other cannot be ancient, and the
demonstration takes a single sentence.

They are worth taking together because the method that disposes of them is the same, and
because what remains after the disposal is more interesting than the myth in both cases.

---

## I. THE EYE

### Where it comes from

An eye, often within a triangle, usually radiant. It appears on Masonic tracing boards,
certificates, aprons and buildings, and it is the image that the twentieth century made into
the emblem of secret control.

Its origin is not obscure and it is not Masonic. **The Eye of Providence is a commonplace of
European Christian art**, from the Renaissance onward. It is on churches, on tombs, in
Bibles, in emblem books, on pulpits, above altars. The triangle signifies the Trinity. The
radiance signifies glory. The eye signifies that God sees.

It was in use across Catholic and Protestant Europe for two centuries before anyone put one
on a Masonic document, and it continued in ordinary devotional use long afterwards. You can
find it today on eighteenth-century parish churches in England whose builders had no
connection to any lodge.

### When Masonry adopted it

Later than most people assume, and later than most of the Craft's other symbols.

It is not in the earliest Masonic material. It appears in the second half of the eighteenth
century and becomes prominent in the nineteenth, with the expansion of printed and
manufactured Masonic imagery — certificates, aprons, jewels, tracing boards produced
commercially.

**Establishing the earliest datable Masonic appearance requires systematic work on dated
material culture** — aprons, certificates and boards with secure dates — and that work has
not, so far as I know, been done comprehensively. I am not going to assert a year. The
preceding volume flagged this as an open question and it remains one.

### The Great Seal

The claim that makes this symbol famous concerns the reverse of the Great Seal of the United
States, and thence the one-dollar bill: an unfinished pyramid surmounted by an eye in a
triangle, with two Latin mottoes.

The facts are these.

The Seal was produced by a committee process running from 1776 to 1782, involving three
successive committees and several designers, and the final design was assembled by Charles
Thomson, Secretary of the Continental Congress. **Thomson was not a Freemason.** The eye in a
triangle came from the standard European emblematic vocabulary described above, where it had
meant divine providence for two hundred years. The unfinished pyramid came from a different
committee's proposal.

The reverse of the Seal was not engraved for use and appeared on no official object until it
was placed on the one-dollar bill in 1935.[^1]

So the sequence is: a conventional Christian emblem, adopted by a non-Mason on a
congressional committee in 1782, unused for a century and a half, printed on currency in
1935, and identified as a Masonic-Illuminati signal in the later twentieth century.

### What is actually true

Two things, and they are both more interesting than the myth.

**Masonic membership among the American founding generation was genuinely high and genuinely
consequential** — as a network, a source of connection, a form of associational life. That
is well documented and the preceding volume treated it at length. It simply has nothing to do
with the Seal.

**And the Eye's Masonic meaning is theologically ordinary.** In Masonic usage it signifies
that the Great Architect observes — that conduct is witnessed whether or not anyone is
watching. That is a moral proposition about accountability, available to every religion in
the room, and it is the same thing the emblem meant on a parish church.

The symbol accused of representing surveillance does in fact represent surveillance. It is
just God's, and the Craft borrowed it from the Church.

## II. THE LETTER G

### The sentence that settles it

The letter G appears in the lodge, suspended in the east or set within the square and
compasses. The Craft explains it as standing for Geometry, and also for God.

**It is a letter of the Roman alphabet whose significance depends on the initial letters of
two English words.**

That is the whole demonstration. The symbol cannot be Egyptian, because Egyptian does not use
the Roman alphabet and the relevant concepts do not begin with that letter. It cannot be
Hebrew, for the same reasons. It cannot be Phoenician, Greek, Latin or Templar. It can only
have been generated by people thinking in English, which places it in the period when
English-speaking people were making Masonic symbols — the eighteenth century, which is
exactly where the documentary evidence puts it.

This is the anachronism test from Chapter 5, and it is the cleanest kill in either of these
volumes. No transmission argument is required, no archive needs to be searched, and no
counter-evidence is possible. A symbol whose meaning is an English pun is an English symbol.

### The confirming evidence

There is a second piece of evidence, and it is the sort of thing that makes historical work
satisfying.

When Masonry crossed to France, the letter went with it — and it did not work, because the
French for geometry is *géométrie*, which begins with the right letter, but the French for
God is *Dieu*, which does not.

So French Masonic sources supply alternatives. *Gloire*. *Grandeur*. *Génie*. The letter was
retained and new meanings were found to justify it.[^2]

**That is precisely backwards from how an ancient symbol behaves.** A symbol carrying
inherited meaning arrives with its content and the content constrains its use. This one
arrived as a *shape* and had to have content supplied locally, in each language, to explain
why it was there.

The letter travelled as a letter. The meanings were improvised behind it.

### The overlay

And then the nineteenth century, in the usual way.

The G has been identified with the Hebrew letter *yod*, the initial of the divine name; with
gematria; with the generative principle; with the number three. Each of these is offered as
the symbol's hidden true meaning, and each is an attempt to find an ancient parentage for an
English initial.

They are all late, and they are all, on the evidence of the previous paragraph, unnecessary.

## WHAT THE TWO CASES SHARE

I have put them in one chapter because the pattern is identical and it is the pattern of this
entire book.

**Both symbols are ordinary.** One is standard Christian iconography. The other is an
initial.

**Both acquired esoteric readings in the nineteenth century.** The Eye as consciousness or
the watcher; the G as *yod* or the generative principle.

**Both acquired conspiratorial readings in the twentieth.**

**And in both cases the documented meaning is morally serious and entirely unmysterious.**
The Eye says that you are seen. The G says that this is a society founded on geometry and
on God, and that the two are connected.

That is what the Craft meant. It is what the sources say it meant. And it has been buried
under two centuries of accumulated interpretation by people who found the plain meaning
insufficiently interesting.

I want to end Part V by saying something about that, because it has been building since
Chapter 17 and it is the most general finding in this book about symbolism.

**The Masonic symbols that have generated the most esoteric literature are the ones with the
least to say.** The square, the level and the plumb — anchored to instruments, with clear
documented meanings — produced almost no speculative interpretation, because there was
nothing to supply. The Blazing Star, the point within a circle, the Eye and the letter G —
unanchored, decorative, or arbitrary — produced enormous quantities of it.

Vacancy attracts furniture. That has been the argument since the first volume, and here it
can be stated as a rule with the evidence in hand:

**The interpretive literature on a symbol grows in inverse proportion to how much the symbol
originally meant.**

Which is a useful thing to know when reading about any tradition, and it will be the
operating principle of Chapter 38.

We are done with the daylight symbols now. What remains at the centre of Freemasonry is a
murder.
