# CHAPTER TWENTY-FOUR
## The Lost Word

The most consequential sentence in Freemasonry is a negative one.

The word is lost. It died with the master. What is given in its place is a substitute, and
the ritual says so — not as a concession, not as an apology, but as a doctrine. The
substitute is adopted *until future generations shall find out the right*.[^1]

Every other initiatory system in this book promises to deliver something. This one promises
that the thing cannot be delivered, hands over a placeholder, and describes the placeholder
accurately.

I have come to think that this is the single most important fact about Freemasonry, and this
chapter is an attempt to say why.

---

### What a substitute word is

Take the structure seriously before interpreting it.

A word is given at each degree, and the words are used for recognition — the practical
function described in Chapter 9. The master's word would be the last and highest of these.

The ceremony arranges for it to be unavailable. Three men attempt to extract it by force and
the man holding it dies rather than give it, which destroys it for everyone. What the
candidate receives at the summit of the Craft is therefore a *known deficiency*: a word that
everyone present agrees is not the word, adopted because there has to be one.

And the lodge goes on using it. For three centuries. Knowing.

That is a strange thing for an institution to do, and it is stranger still that the
institution tells every new member about it explicitly, in the ceremony, at the moment of
his highest advancement.

---

### The obvious consequence

A system that declares its central content missing has built an unresolved ending into
itself, and unresolved endings generate activity.

**This is where the high degrees come from.**

Within fifteen years of the Hiramic legend appearing in print, French Masons were
constructing additional degrees at an extraordinary rate, and the preceding volume traced
the phenomenon in detail: hundreds of grades, most now lost, composed by identifiable people
in identifiable decades.

Look at what they are *about*. The Elect degrees pursue and punish the murderers. The Royal
Arch recovers the lost name. The Rose-Croix supplies a Christological fulfillment in which
the Word is the Word made flesh. The Templar degrees provide a continuing order that
preserved what was hidden.

**Every one of them is an answer to the third degree's declaration that the answer is
missing.** They are not additions to a complete system; they are attempts to complete an
incomplete one, and the incompleteness was announced by the system itself.

The Craft opened a hole in 1730 and the eighteenth century spent seventy years filling it.

---

### The Royal Arch

The most serious of those answers deserves its own treatment, and it is also the point at
which I have to admit that I do not know something important.

The Royal Arch degree developed in England and Ireland in the middle of the eighteenth
century. Its subject is the recovery of a lost divine name — discovered, in the ceremony's
narrative, during work on the Temple's foundations. In England it was eventually declared
part of pure Ancient Masonry by the Act of Union of 1813, so that the English Craft
officially consists of three degrees *including* the Royal Arch.[^2]

Where it came from is genuinely unresolved. It has no clear parentage in the Craft degrees,
no clear continental source, and its origin was already obscure to Masonic antiquarians in
the nineteenth century. The preceding volume left it as an open question and I am leaving it
open here.

What I want to note is the theological texture, because it is unlike anything else in the
Craft.

A degree whose subject is the recovery of a concealed divine Name is operating in territory
that Jewish mystical tradition had occupied for centuries. The Name, its concealment, its
pronunciation, the substitution of another word for it in reading — these are real and
central Jewish concerns, and the resemblance is not trivial.

**And I cannot demonstrate a route.** The four-link chain by which Christian Cabala reached
European esotericism is documented and was set out in the preceding volume, and it does not
obviously pass through mid-eighteenth-century English Freemasonry. The Royal Arch predates
Lévi by a century and is not explained by the nineteenth-century occult reinterpretation
that accounts for so much else.

Three possibilities remain. It is an independent development from the third degree's own
logic — the word is lost, therefore a degree in which it is found. It reflects some contact
with Hebraic material that has not been traced. Or the resemblance is convergence, arising
because any tradition thinking hard about an unsayable name will arrive at similar
territory.

I incline to the first, and I hold it loosely, and this is one of the places where the
honest answer is that the evidence is not sufficient.

---

### Why deferral works

Set aside the history and ask the functional question. What does a permanently deferred
secret do to the people who hold it?

**It cannot be exhausted.** A delivered secret is consumed. Once you have it, the
transaction is complete and the institution has nothing further to offer. A deferred one
remains in front of the member indefinitely.

**It cannot be falsified.** Nobody can demonstrate that the true word does not exist,
because its absence is the doctrine. The claim is structurally immune.

**It converts membership into search.** A Mason is not someone who possesses the word; he is
someone who is looking for it, in company, with others who are also looking. That is a
description of an ongoing activity rather than a completed status, and it is the reason a man
attends for forty years.

**And it makes the institution modest at exactly the point where institutions are usually
grandiose.** The Craft's climax is an admission of loss. It tells its members, at the moment
of their highest attainment, that the fraternity does not have the thing it exists around.

I find that last point genuinely admirable, and I want to register that it cuts against the
grain of everything else this book has found. An institution that manufactured an antique
pedigree for itself, that absorbed whatever interpretation was poured into it, and that
generated three centuries of speculative literature — nevertheless placed, at its own
summit, a formal acknowledgment that the answer is not in its possession.

---

### The dangerous reading

There is a darker way to describe the same structure and it should be stated.

**A permanently deferred secret is also the perfect instrument of an institution that has
nothing to give.**

If the content is always one degree further on, then no member can ever establish that there
is no content. The mechanism that sustains a genuine search is identical to the mechanism
that sustains an empty one, and from inside they are indistinguishable.

The preceding volume documented what happens when this structure is operated by people
supplying their own authority: von Hund's Unknown Superiors, who never appeared in thirty
years; Blavatsky's Masters; Westcott's German adept who did not exist; Mathers's Secret
Chiefs, whose non-production destroyed the Golden Dawn in 1900. Every one of those is the
lost-word structure with a fraudulent answer attached.

**Freemasonry is the case that did not do this.** It did not claim to have the word. It
declared the word lost and offered a substitute, which is the opposite of the manufactured
superior — an institution declining to supply an authority it did not have.

The structure is the same. The honesty is not.

---

### What is actually transmitted

So what does a Master Mason receive?

Not the word. A substitute, acknowledged as such. A set of instruments with moral
interpretations. A drama in which he lay on the floor while men searched for him. An
obligation. A place in a room. And the information that the thing everyone is looking for
was lost before any of them were born.

Stated that way it sounds like very little, and a strictly informational audit would find
almost nothing.

But consider what the last item does. **He has been given, at the climax of a ceremony
engineered for maximum receptivity, the proposition that the search is the inheritance.**

That is a real thing to be told, and it is told to him by means he will not forget, and it
is the only content in the entire system that could not be conveyed any other way. You cannot
tell a man that the answer is unavailable and have it land. You can arrange for him to reach
for it and close his hand on a substitute.

Whether that changes anybody is Part XI's question.

What it certainly did was set the entire subsequent history of Western esotericism in
motion, because a great many people took it literally and went looking.
