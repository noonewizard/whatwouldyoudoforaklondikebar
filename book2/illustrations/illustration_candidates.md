## TWO NEW CATEGORIES

**Items 45 and 46 are diagrams made for this book**, not historical images, and will be
captioned unambiguously as such. They exist to do in a picture what Chapter 38 does in
prose: show the reader that the method producing the "discovery" also produces it from
nothing. A reader who sees a pentagram pulled out of an arbitrary street grid has learned
the lesson faster than any paragraph can teach it.

**Items 22–24 and 26** support the argument that the lodge's architectural power is a
property of *arrangement*, not grandeur — with the nineteenth-century temples as the
exception that Chapter 12 must explain.

## CAPTIONING PRINCIPLE

Every caption states what the object is, its date, where it survives, and **what it does and
does not prove**. Items 34 (the *Rosarium* death woodcut) and 37 (Lévi's Baphomet) are the
two most frequently misused images in the popular literature, and their captions will say so.

## PERMISSIONS

Institutional reproduction fees for the Library and Museum of Freemasonry will be the
largest single cost. A fallback set of roughly 22 items drawn entirely from Internet
Archive, Wellcome Collection (CC BY) and Library of Congress public-domain holdings can
carry the book at minimal cost, plus the two original diagrams.
