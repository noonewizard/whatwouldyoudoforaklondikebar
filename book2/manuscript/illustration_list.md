# ILLUSTRATION LIST — FINAL PROGRAMME

**46 plates**, within the brief's range of 40–60. Every item is a real, locatable object,
with two declared exceptions (items 45 and 46) which are diagrams made for this book and
captioned unambiguously as such. **No image is invented.** Rights status must be confirmed
with each holding institution before publication; **[SVR]** marks a shelfmark or accession
number still to be verified.

The programme illustrates **mechanism**, not provenance: the objects a candidate handled,
the spaces he moved through, the diagrams that carried the system, and — a category this
book needs and most do not — the figures belonging to the methodological chapters.

## CAPTIONING PRINCIPLE

Every caption states four things: what the object is, its date, where it survives, and **what
it does and does not prove**. The last clause is not decoration. Items 34 (the *Rosarium
philosophorum* death woodcut) and 37 (Lévi's Baphomet) are the two most frequently misused
images in the literature on this subject — reproduced in book after book as though their
mere presence established a connection to Freemasonry — and their captions will say so
explicitly.

Where an image is used by the popular literature to support a claim this book rejects, the
caption names the claim and states the objection. A picture makes an argument whether or not
the text endorses it, and a caption is the only place to answer it.

## THE TWO ORIGINAL DIAGRAMS

Items 45 and 46 are made for this book and do in a picture what Chapter 38 does in prose.

**45.** A pentagram fitted to an arbitrary 12 × 8 lattice, with the fitting procedure
shown. Caption: *this proves nothing about the street layout, and demonstrates that the
method which finds sacred geometry in a cathedral plan will find it anywhere.*

**46.** The gematria control test of Appendix F, set out as two columns of twenty words —
Masonic vocabulary and root vegetables — with the matching pairs highlighted on both sides.
Caption: *three matches on each side.*

A reader who sees a pentagram pulled out of a suburban street grid has learned the lesson
faster than any paragraph can teach it.

## ARCHITECTURE OF ARRANGEMENT

Items 22–24 and 26 support the argument that the lodge's architectural effect is a property
of *arrangement* rather than grandeur — an ordinary upstairs room, correctly organised,
doing the work. The nineteenth-century purpose-built temples are the exception Chapter 12
has to explain rather than the rule it rests on, and the plate sequence is ordered to make
that visible.

## PERMISSIONS AND A FALLBACK SET

Institutional reproduction fees, chiefly for the Library and Museum of Freemasonry in London,
will be the largest single production cost. A fallback programme of approximately 22 plates
drawn entirely from Internet Archive, Wellcome Collection (CC BY) and Library of Congress
public-domain holdings, plus the two original diagrams, can carry the book at minimal cost
without losing any argument. The items lost under the fallback are illustrative rather than
evidential, with one exception: item 5, the 47th Proposition as printed in Anderson 1723,
should be retained under any scenario because Chapter 18 turns on it.
