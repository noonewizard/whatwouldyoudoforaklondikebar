# NOTE — HERMETICISM (BOOK II)

Four referents, never merged: the Greek *Corpus* (2nd–3rd c. CE); the technical Hermetica;
Renaissance Hermetism after Ficino's *Pimander* of 1471; and "Hermeticism" as a modern
occult self-description. Casaubon dated the *Corpus* to the Christian era in 1614 and almost
nobody noticed.

**What Book II takes from the tradition is not its history but its *instrument*: the table
of correspondences.**

Agrippa's Book II gives, for each number and planet, long columns — divine name, angelic
order, metal, stone, animal, plant, faculty. To a modern eye this looks like arbitrary
list-making. Given the metaphysics, it is a **technical document**: a map of the places
where the levels of reality touch.

Chapter 28's question: what is a table of correspondences *for*, considered as a cognitive
artefact? Candidate answer, to be tested — it is a memory system and a generative grammar.
It lets a practitioner construct a novel ritual for an arbitrary purpose by assembling a
column. That is a real intellectual achievement and it has nothing to do with whether the
correspondences are true.

**The Masonic relevance is negative and should be stated as such.** Masonic ritual has no
table of correspondences. It has a Bible, a square, a pair of compasses and some builder's
tools. The absence is as informative as the presence would have been.

**The continuity that is real:** Agrippa in English from 1651 → the antiquarian book trade →
Westcott's Hermetic library of 286 items → the Golden Dawn's tables → Crowley's *Liber 777*
(1909). A documented route, established in Book I.
