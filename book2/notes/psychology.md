# NOTE — PSYCHOLOGY AND COGNITIVE SCIENCE

**The rule.** Every application is an inference across centuries and cultures, labelled as
one. See `research/psychology_matrix.md` for the full grid with populations, distances and
replication status.

**The four findings Book II leans on, in descending order of security.**

1. **Arousal modulates memory encoding.** Robust, general, uncontroversial. The ceremony is
   memorable because it is alarming. The least exciting claim in the book and the safest.
2. **Procedural and spatial memory outlast propositional memory.** Robust. Explains why
   ritual knowledge survives illiteracy and centuries, and why Masonic ritual is learned by
   heart rather than read.
3. **Whitehouse's imagistic mode** predicts, for infrequent high-arousal ritual: vivid
   episodic memory, strong small-group cohesion, transmission by participation, and
   **interpretive diversity**. Masonry shows all four. A framework built on other material
   predicting four independent features is worth recording.
4. **Public, voluntary, effortful commitment** shapes later self-description more than
   private assent. Robust in outline; some classic studies contested.

**The two ⚠ flags.**

*Aronson and Mills (1959)* — severity of initiation increases liking for the group. One
small study; mixed replication; **the most-cited result in discussions of fraternal
initiation.** Any chapter using it states this.

*Keltner and Haidt on awe* — growing literature, replication caveats, and the Masonic lodge
is usually a small ordinary room.

**The five prohibitions.** No claim about a named historical individual. No claim that an
effect occurred in the eighteenth century. No claim of moral improvement. No contested
finding used as settled. No inference from modern initiates to historical ones.

**The disconfirming evidence that must not be buried:** lodge minutes record mass
non-attendance and exclusion for non-payment. Most members left no trace of interest in the
symbolism. If this is a transformative technology, its commonest outcome was a man who liked
the dinners.
