# EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION

Per brief §XXXIII. Every substantive claim in the manuscript is tagged internally. Tags
appear in the text only where they clarify rather than clutter.

---

## THE SCALE

| Tag | Level | Meaning | Example from this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** | Direct primary evidence | A period document says so | Anderson 1723 names the 47th Proposition |
| **E2** | Strong scholarly reconstruction | Specialists converge on the reading from good evidence | The third degree emerges in London in the 1720s |
| **E3** | Plausible interpretation | Consistent with the evidence; alternatives exist | Arousal in the ceremony aided memory encoding |
| **E4** | Contested interpretation | Competent scholars disagree | Schaw's art-of-memory clause is esoteric |
| **E5** | Speculative | Chronologically possible; no transmission or corroboration | The rough ashlar derives from alchemical *prima materia* |
| **E6** | Unsupported | Fails the tests; often chronologically impossible | Masonry descends from the Egyptian mysteries |

---

## COMBINING WITH THE MAGIC TYPES

Each claim carries **two** tags: an evidence level and a magic-classification type where
"magic" is involved (A–E, `magic_classification.md`).

Worked examples:

| Claim | Tags |
|---|---|
| "Lévi defined magic as an operation of the will upon the astral light" | **E1 / Type A** |
| "Agrippa's *Occult Philosophy* was available in English from 1651" | **E1** |
| "Westcott owned a Hermetic library of 286 items" | **E2** |
| "Ritual of this design would be expected to produce durable memory" | **E3 / Type D** |
| "The ceremony produced moral improvement" | **no available tag — unevidenced in principle** |
| "The astral light exists" | **Type E — never asserted** |

## THE SIX QUESTIONS (carried from Book I)

Applied to any claim that something old was carried into something newer:

1. **Provenance** — where is the physical object?
2. **Chronology** — does the timeline permit it?
3. **Transmission** — can you name the route? *Availability is not transmission.*
4. **Intertextuality** — does the later source quote the earlier, or merely resemble it?
5. **Institutional continuity** — did the organisation actually persist?
6. **Anachronism** — does the claim contain something that could not yet exist?

**Governing rule: a parallel is not a descent.**

---

## THE PATTERN-DETECTION TEST (new to Book II)

For any claim that a hidden pattern has been *discovered* — cipher, numerical scheme,
geometric alignment, hidden name — four additional questions apply.

**7. Was the procedure specified in advance, or fitted to the result?**
A method chosen after seeing the answer is not a method.

**8. What is the search space?**
How many words, letters, numbers, buildings or angles were available to try? A hit is
meaningful only relative to how many chances there were.

**9. Does it survive a control?**
Run the same procedure on matched material that could not contain the message — another
text, another city plan, another century's ritual. **If the control produces comparable
hits, the finding is noise.** This is the McKay test, and it is the single most useful
instrument in Book II.

**10. Does the claim make a prediction that could fail?**
A pattern that accommodates any outcome explains none.

**A claim that cannot pass 7–10 is E5 or E6 regardless of how striking it looks.**

## THE FIVE CLAIM-TYPES (carried from Book I)

| Type | Form | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Historical | "Practitioners believed X." | Their texts |
| Phenomenological | "Practitioners reported experiencing X." | First-person testimony |
| Psychological | "Ritual of this kind may produce Y." | Modern literature, hedged, never individual |
| Sociological | "Ritual creates structure Z." | Records |
| Metaphysical | "Mechanism X objectively exists." | **Never asserted** |

## SIGNALLING IN THE PROSE

- Reconstruction is labelled **in the body text**, not only in a note.
- Uncertainty is stated in fixed formulae used precisely.
- Attribution is specific: never "scholars agree."
- Contested findings are reported **with** their contestation.
- Unverifiable particulars carry **[PAGE VERIFICATION REQUIRED]** — never an invented page.
- Modern frameworks applied to historical material carry the inferential distance in the
  sentence that applies them.
