# SECONDARY SOURCE NOTES — BOOK II

What each major work is *for*, and where it must be handled with care.

## The ritual-studies spine
- **van Gennep (1909)** — the tripartite schema. Use as vocabulary, not as law.
- **Turner (1969)** — liminality, communitas. Built on Ndembu material; the fit with
  Masonry is good and is a fit, not a derivation.
- **Bell (1992)** — *the necessary objection.* "Ritual" may be a scholar's category. Book II
  states this in Chapter 6 rather than burying it.
- **Grimes (1990, 2014)** — ritual criticism; the demand that rites be assessed in their
  particularity. The methodological conscience of Part VIII.
- **J. Z. Smith (1987)** — **load-bearing.** Sacrality as emplacement. The reason the lodge
  works in a tavern.
- **Rappaport (1999)** — ritual as the acceptance of obligation. Directly relevant to the oath.
- **Eliade (1958)** — historiographically important, universalist, and the source of most
  bad Masonic comparativism. Cite as a position, not as authority.

## The cognitive spine — with caveats attached
- **Whitehouse (2004)** — modes of religiosity. **Predicts four features Masonry
  independently shows.** Built on other material; the convergence is interesting, not proof.
- **McCauley and Lawson (2002)** — ritual form and cognitive representation.
- **Xygalatas (2012, 2013, 2022)** — the experimental programme. Different populations,
  different century. Inferential distance must be stated at every use.
- **Boyer and Liénard (2006)** — why ritualised action has its *form*. A theory of form, not
  of effect.
- **⚠ Aronson and Mills (1959)** — severity of initiation. **Mixed replication. Flagged
  here so that no chapter can use it quietly.**

## Architecture — with caveats attached
- **⚠ Meyers-Levy and Zhu (2007)** — the cathedral effect. Priming research; the field has
  been badly hit by the replication crisis. Report with effect sizes.
- **Pallasmaa (1996)** — phenomenological, not experimental. A framework for description.
- **Curl (1991)** — Masonic architecture, direct.
- **Wittkower (1949); von Simson (1956)** — proportion and theology. Background for the
  Temple chapters.

## Masonic history
- **Bogdan (2007)** — initiation as the transmission vehicle of Western esotericism. The
  theoretical spine of both books.
- **Bogdan and Snoek, *Handbook* (2014)** — the reference apparatus.
- **Stevenson (1988); Berman (2012); Jacob (1991); Bullock (1996)** — the institutional and
  social history, carried from Book I.
- **Dachez** — the French counterweight. The Anglophone account of Masonic origins is not
  the only one, and Book II should not pretend otherwise.
- **Önnerfors (2017)** — the best short scholarly orientation.
- **Snoek (2012)** — the Adoption Rite; essential for testing whether the ritual map is
  general.

## Esotericism
- **Hanegraaff (2012)** and **von Stuckrad (2005, 2010)** — the two positions on what
  "esotericism" names. **Book II adopts von Stuckrad's discursive analytic and accepts
  Hanegraaff's historical caution**, and says so in Chapter 5.
- **Butler (2011)** — the library method; the model for establishing what was actually to hand.
- **Strube (2016)** — Lévi's genealogy; resets Part X.
- **Principe and Newman** — alchemy against the psychological reduction.
- **Scholem, Idel, Dan, Coudert** — Kabbalah in its own terms.

## Pattern detection and method
- **McKay, Bar-Natan, Bar-Hillel and Kalai (1999)** — **the single most useful instrument in
  Book II.** The control test.
- **Grafton (1990)** — forgery and criticism developing together.
- **Hobsbawm and Ranger (1983)** — invented tradition.
- **Simmel (1906); Urban (1998)** — secrecy as structure and as a methodological problem.
- **Roberts (1972)** — conspiracy historiography.

## Philosophy
- **Polanyi (1966)** — *the tacit dimension.* **Load-bearing for Chapter 39:** the strongest
  respectable argument that initiation transmits something a book cannot — and the argument
  that also makes empty secrets unfalsifiable.
- **Peirce** — icon, index, symbol, for Chapter 32.
- **Gadamer** — hermeneutics, for the interpretation chapters.

## Used with explicit caution
- **Eliade** — as above.
- **Cialdini (2006)** — useful on commitment; not a theory of ritual and not to be used as one.
- **Manly P. Hall; Leadbeater; the popular Masonic-symbolism literature** — Level D. Cited
  only as evidence that a belief exists, and labelled in the text.
