## WHAT THE COLUMNS SHOW

**1. The Masonic word is old; the Lost Word is not.** A word, a grip and a catechism
are in place by 1696. The *loss* is not attested before the 1720s. Any derivation
from Hebrew name-traditions must cross sixteen centuries with no documented
carrier.

**2. Atbash is the oldest item in the cryptography column and has nothing to do with
the third.** Attested c. 600 BCE, in Hebrew, in scripture, on proper nouns. Its next
appearance here is 1984, applied by a modern author to a name the Templars were
alleged to have used. There is no intervening Masonic use.

**3. The available cryptography far exceeded what Masonry used.** Frequency
analysis: ninth century. Alberti: 1467. Vigenère: 1586. Masonry adopted an unkeyed
monoalphabetic grid in the 1700s — obsolete for concealment for three hundred years.
**A body with something to hide had better options and took none of them.**

**4. Kabbalah was available in Latin from 1677**, which is the one genuine
opportunity for transmission, and the point at which the Royal Arch question (1740s)
sits. Availability is not transmission.

**5. Every Hermetic-Qabalistic reading of the Masonic word postdates 1854.** The
vocabulary in which the Lost Word is usually explained did not exist when the Lost
Word appeared.
