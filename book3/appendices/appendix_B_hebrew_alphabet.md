# APPENDIX B
## The Hebrew Alphabet

Twenty-two letters, all consonants. Vowels were supplied by the reader until the
Masoretes devised a pointing system between roughly the seventh and tenth centuries
CE.

## NOTES ON THE TABLE

**Final forms.** Five letters take a distinct shape at the end of a word. These are
a scribal convention, not separate letters. In the standard reckoning they carry
the same value as the medial form; an alternative convention (*mispar gadol*)
assigns them 500, 600, 700, 800 and 900 respectively.

**This choice is not fixed by the tradition**, which means any gematria result
depends on a decision the analyst makes. See Appendix E.

**Matres lectionis.** Four letters — **א ה ו י** — can mark a long vowel as well as
representing a consonant. Their use expands over time, which makes fuller spelling
a rough dating criterion. It also means that many Hebrew words admit more than one
correct spelling, which is the source of the search space counted in Chapter 39.

**Letter names are words.** *Alef* connects to the word for ox, *bet* to house,
*dalet* to door, *ayin* to eye, *pe* to mouth, *resh* to head. The signs descend
from pictographs and the names preserve the memory. This is philology and it is
solid; everything built on it is interpretation.

**Letters as numerals.** Hebrew had no separate numerals and the letters served.
The system is transparent — units, tens, hundreds — and entirely practical. Greek
does the same thing, with the same consequence (isopsephy), which is why both
traditions independently developed number-word interpretation.

## THE THREE GROUPS OF *SEFER YETZIRAH*

The text divides the alphabet as follows. The division is the basis of a great deal
of later Kabbalistic correspondence.

**Three mothers** — א מ ש — corresponding to air, water and fire.

**Seven doubles** — ב ג ד כ פ ר ת — letters with two pronunciations, corresponding
to the planets, the days of the week, and the openings of the head.

**Twelve simples** — the remaining twelve — corresponding to the zodiac, the months,
and principal organs and functions.

Note that the "seven doubles" reflect a phonological fact about Hebrew — these
letters have both plosive and fricative realizations — and that resh's inclusion is
disputed in the manuscript tradition. **A correspondence system built on a
phonological feature is built on something real**, and the assignments laid over it
are interpretation.

## VOWEL POINTING

The Masoretic system is not part of the consonantal text and is a thousand years or
more later than most of the material it records. It preserves a reading tradition
of real antiquity; it is not itself ancient.

The single most consequential instance is the pointing attached to יהוה, which is
the pointing of *Adonai* — an instruction to substitute, not the vowels of the Name.
Reading those points as though they belonged to the consonants produces "Jehovah."
See Chapter 12.
