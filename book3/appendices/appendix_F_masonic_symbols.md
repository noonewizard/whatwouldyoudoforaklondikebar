# APPENDIX F
## Masonic Symbols: Anchored Meanings and Later Accretions

Each entry gives the meaning the ritual itself supplies — the **anchored** meaning —
and the readings imported by later commentators, which are **unanchored**.

The distinction is not a way of dismissing the second column. Some of those
readings are two centuries old and have shaped how members actually experience the
symbols, and a symbol's meaning in practice includes what its users take it to
mean. The column exists to keep two claims apart: *this is what the ritual says* and
*this is what somebody later said about it.* **Only the first is evidence of
intention.**

**Jurisdictional caution.** Furnishing, wording and in some cases the presence of a
symbol vary between constitutions. Nothing below is universal Masonic practice.

## THE TWO DIAGNOSTIC PATTERNS

**Where the tradition's own sources disagree**, the most economical explanation is
that the symbol arrived without a settled meaning. The **letter G** is the cleanest
case: present in some workings and absent in others, explained as Geometry in some
sources and as God in others, with both offered simultaneously by authorities who
plainly did not regard the matter as settled.

And the G has a further tell. **It is an English letter.** It works because *God* and
*Geometry* both begin with it in English, and does not work in French, Latin or
Hebrew. A symbol whose significance depends on the initials of two English words is
eighteenth-century English wordplay, not an inheritance from antiquity.

**Where the sources agree**, agreement is evidence of transmission only if the
agreed meaning is *arbitrary* rather than obvious. The **square and compasses** are
explained with striking consistency across three centuries and many jurisdictions —
and the consistency proves nothing, because a square makes right angles and
right-angled conduct is the first metaphor anyone would reach for.

**Consistency of interpretation is worthless as evidence when the interpretation is
the obvious one.** This disposes of a large quantity of argument in which
cross-traditional agreement is offered as proof of a common source. Many societies
have used the sun as an image of what is life-giving and inescapable. That is not a
transmission; it is what the sun is like.
