# APPENDIX G
## Divine Names in Historical Context

There is no such thing as *the* name of God in the Hebrew Bible. There are several,
with different forms, histories and functions, and merging them makes every claim
about "the Name" ambiguous from the outset.

## THE TETRAGRAMMATON: WHAT IS AND IS NOT KNOWN

**Known.** Four consonants, yod-he-waw-he. Some six thousand occurrences.
Continuously legible for three thousand years. Related to the verbal root *h-y-h*,
"to be," as Exodus 3 itself suggests.

**Not known.** The pronunciation.

**Why not.** Hebrew was written consonantally. Use narrowed over centuries — free in
earlier biblical material, restricted by the later Second Temple period, confined
to priestly and Temple contexts, and ended as a public practice with the Temple in
70 CE. **There was no decree.** A practice narrowed until there was nothing left to
transmit.

**The reconstruction.** Scholarship generally favours something like *Yahweh*, on
three kinds of indirect evidence: Greek transcriptions in patristic sources
(*Iao*, *Iabe*); theophoric personal names, which preserve *Yeho-* and *-yahu* in a
living tradition; and the verbal root.

**This is an inference and should be labelled as one.** Nobody has heard the word
pronounced in a living liturgical tradition. It is a reconstruction of the general
reliability of other historical-linguistic reconstructions, which is good, and not
the same as a recording.

**And the pronunciation is not the interesting question**, because a person
possessing the correct vowels would lack the office, the setting and the condition,
and could therefore do nothing with them. See Chapters 3 and 13.

## "JEHOVAH": THE ANATOMY OF AN ARTEFACT

The single best worked example in this book of a valid operation producing a
spurious result.

**The convention.** The Masoretes supplied vowel points to a consonantal text they
would not alter. Where the written and spoken forms diverge, the consonants of the
written word stay on the line and the vowels of the spoken word are pointed onto
them — *ketiv* and *qere*. For יהוה this happens so universally that no marginal
note is needed: a *qere perpetuum*.

**So יהוה appears with the vowels of *Adonai*. The points are an instruction to say
a different word.**

**The misreading.** Renaissance Christian Hebraists, unfamiliar with the convention,
did what any reader does with a pointed word: they pronounced it. Yod-he-waw-he with
*Adonai*'s vowels yields *Yehovah*, and thence **Jehovah**.

Generally traced to Petrus Galatinus, *De arcanis catholicae veritatis* (1518);
earlier instances have been argued for and the priority question is unsettled.

**What the case demonstrates**, and each point recurs in Part IX:

1. **A technically correct operation on real data can produce a spurious result.**
   The consonants are those consonants; the points are those points; the
   combination sounds like that.
2. **The error is invisible from inside the operation.** Detecting it requires
   knowledge from outside the transformation.
3. **A result that circulates becomes evidence for itself.** Later writers found
   *Jehovah* in their sources and reproduced it.
4. **And the error is stable.** Scholarly opinion has been clear for two centuries
   and it has made no difference to usage.

## THE COMMANDMENT, AND WHAT THE NAME ACTUALLY DID

The prohibition on taking the Name *lashav* is heard in the modern West as a rule
about swearing. Its scope in the Hebrew is wider and its context is **oath-taking**.

**The Name's principal function in daily life was contractual.** It is what a
promise was sworn on, and its power was the power to make an undertaking binding.

A society securing contracts by oath has an interest in the oath retaining force.
Restricting the Name to occasions that warrant it is how you keep the currency from
inflating.

**Which is a performative in Austin's exact sense** (Chapter 3), and it is why the
Masonic parallel that actually holds is not the word but the **obligation** — sworn
aloud, before witnesses, in prescribed form, by a person the procedure has prepared.

## NAMES IN MAGICAL PRACTICE

For Part VI. Hebrew divine names circulate widely in Greek, Latin and vernacular
magical literature, and the borrowing is documented — texts, dates, geography,
verbal dependence.

**Genuine names**: Adonai, Elohim, Shaddai, Eheieh.

**Artefacts**: **AGLA** is a notarikon of *Atah Gibor Le'olam Adonai* — a real
Hebrew phrase compressed by a real Hebrew technique — which then circulated in Latin
magical texts as though it were itself a name. **A technique's output became an
object.**

**Corruptions**: forms transliterated by scribes without Hebrew, recopied by
scribes with less, until the manuscript form is several removes from anything
pronounceable.

**Note the direction of travel:** from a tradition that used these names
reverentially and never operatively, into one that used them operatively with no
access to the original context. Jewish practice restricted the Name; the grimoire
tradition multiplied it.

**And note that this is what a documented transmission looks like** — which is
precisely what is absent from every proposed route into Masonic ritual.
