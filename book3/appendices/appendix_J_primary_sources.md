# APPENDIX J
## Primary Source Guide

What each body of material can and cannot establish. Readers intending to check
this book's arguments should read this before the bibliography, because most of the
book's caution derives from the constraints listed here.

---

## TIER ONE — WHAT THE RITUAL CONSISTED OF

**The Scottish catechisms.** Edinburgh Register House MS (1696); Chetwode Crawley
MS (c. 1700); Kevan MS (c. 1714). Texts in Knoop, Jones and Hamer, *The Early
Masonic Catechisms* — **the indispensable collection for this book.**

*Establish:* the earliest recoverable ritual — a word, a grip, a question-and-answer
sequence held by heart. Two degrees.
*Do not establish:* any drama, any third degree, any loss, any interpretation.
**Decisive for Chapter 18**, since the absence of the loss in these texts is the
book's central negative finding.

**The exposures, 1723–1762.** Prichard, *Masonry Dissected* (1730); *Three Distinct
Knocks* (1760); *Jachin and Boaz* (1762); the French *réception* literature.

*Establish:* ritual content at datable moments; the emergence of the third degree
between 1724 and 1730; the manner of communicating words.
*Caution:* commercial, sometimes hostile, occasionally padded. Use comparatively —
where three independent exposures agree, the element is probably real.
*Also establish, powerfully:* that publication did nothing to the institution.

**Anderson, *Constitutions* (1723; 1738).**
*Establish:* official self-presentation; the religious formula; the traditional
history; the 47th Proposition attribution **[PAGE VERIFICATION REQUIRED]**.
*Do not establish:* what happened in lodges.

**Lodge minutes, registers and disciplinary records.**
*Establish:* attendance, finance, charity, exclusion, lapse.
*Limitation:* this study worked from published transactions and secondary
summaries rather than archival originals. Declared.

## TIER TWO — HEBREW AND KABBALISTIC SOURCES

**The Masoretic text.** *Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia* for the consonantal text
and apparatus. Tov's *Textual Criticism* for how to use it.

*Establish:* the consonantal text, the pointing, the *qere/ketiv* apparatus.
*Do not establish:* pronunciation before the Masoretes.

**The lexica.** Brown-Driver-Briggs (1906); Koehler-Baumgartner (1994–2000).

**A note on this book's own practice, per Chapter 39's requirement that a target
lexicon be fixed independently.** The Atbash controls used a hand-built target list,
which is precisely why its contamination was detectable. A proper run against a
machine-readable independent lexicon has not been performed and is listed as a gap.
**The book should not ask of others what it has not done itself, and this is
recorded rather than glossed.**

***Sefer Yetzirah*.** Hayman's critical edition (2004) is the necessary starting
point. Dating disputed across seven centuries; the book states the range and does
not pick.

**The *Zohar*.** Matt's Pritzker edition for the text. Scholem for the standard
attribution, Liebes and Huss for the qualifications.

**Knorr von Rosenroth, *Kabbala Denudata* (1677–84).**
*Establish:* what was available in Latin, from when.
*Do not establish:* that any Mason read it. **Availability is not transmission** —
the rule that governs this book's treatment of the whole question.

---

## TIER THREE — MAGICAL AND CRYPTOGRAPHIC

**The Greek Magical Papyri** (Betz). *Establish:* the practice of *voces magicae*
and the borrowing of Hebrew divine names into non-Jewish operative contexts.
**This is what documented transmission looks like** — texts, dates, geography,
verbal dependence — and its features are exactly what the Masonic claims lack.

**Agrippa, *De occulta philosophia* (1533).** *Establish:* what magical alphabets
and correspondence tables were in print, from when.

**Dee's manuscripts** (British Library, Sloane MSS). *Establish:* the complete
documented production of Enochian, 1582–89. **The control case of Chapter 29.**

**Alberti (c. 1467), Trithemius (1499/1606), Vigenère (1586).** *Establish:* the
cryptographic sophistication available to Europe long before the Craft's cipher
use. **A negative finding of real strength.**

## THE RULE OF DIRECTION

**A source is primary for its own period and secondary — often unreliably — for any
earlier one.**

| Source | On its own period | On an earlier period |
|---|---|---|
| Preston (1772) | Primary | Level F speculation |
| Pike (1871) on 19th-c. American Masonry | Primary | Level F on Egypt or Kabbalah |
| Lévi (1854–56) on his own system | Primary | Level F on ancient magic |
| Mathers (1887) on Golden Dawn practice | Primary | Level F on Jewish Kabbalah — he translated Knorr's Latin, not the Aramaic |

A nineteenth-century writer describing what Masonry meant to nineteenth-century
Masons is a witness. The same writer describing 1730 is a reader of the same
documents we have, with fewer of them and no critical apparatus.

**His conclusions deserve consideration; his authority does not transfer.**

---

## THE EVIDENTIARY ASYMMETRY

**Excellent evidence for what the ritual was.** Published, dated, cross-checkable,
in two languages, across four decades.

**Good evidence for when interpretations entered.** Lévi 1854–56, Pike 1871, Golden
Dawn 1887–88 — every link datable.

**Almost no evidence for what any of it did to anyone.** No measurements, no
controls, no longitudinal data, and none obtainable for any historical period.

The honesty of this book depends on never letting the richness of the first
category disguise the poverty of the third.
