# CHAPTER TWENTY-ONE
## The Word and the Degree

Three degrees, three words, and a pattern that is easier to see when the degrees
are laid side by side than when they are undergone one at a time.

---

### The progression

The Craft system as it stabilised confers a word at each stage, and the words are
handled differently at each stage. Since the particulars vary between jurisdictions
and this book does not reproduce private material, what follows describes the
*shape* rather than the contents — and the shape is public property, printed since
1730 and discussed in Masonic literature ever since.

**First degree.** A word is given. It is given as a possession: here is a thing you
now have, which you did not have an hour ago. The candidate's relation to it is
simple ownership.

**Second degree.** Another word is given. The structure is the same but the
candidate is no longer a novice, and the second word arrives in a context where he
understands what such a thing is for.

**Third degree.** A word is *not* given. The one that would complete the sequence is
declared unavailable, and something else is supplied in its place with its status
announced.

Laid out that way, the progression is not additive. It is **additive, additive,
subtractive** — and the subtraction is the climax.

---

### What changes across the three

Four things shift as the candidate moves through, and they shift in the same
direction each time.

**From receiving to producing.** In the first degree the candidate is largely
acted upon; he answers when asked and is told things. By the third he has a part in
a drama, and in the years afterwards he will perform the ceremonies for others. The
preceding volume made this the centre of its argument: the unit of analysis is the
career, not the evening, and the career is a long migration from the middle of the
room to the chair.

**From possession to performance.** The first word is a thing he has. The third
degree's substitute is a thing he *uses*, knowing it to be provisional — which is a
different relation entirely. You cannot own a placeholder in the way you own a
token.

**From being told to being told there is nothing to tell.** This is the reversal and
it lands at exactly the point where the system has taught him to expect the
opposite.

**And from information to condition.** What the third degree actually confers is
not a string. It is a status — Master Mason — with obligations, entitlements and a
relation to a body of men. The word he is given is the smallest part of what he
receives, and the ceremony tells him so.

---

### Why the order matters

A candidate who was told at the outset that the highest word is unavailable would
learn nothing from it.

The structure only works because the first two degrees establish an expectation.
They teach him what a Masonic word is, how it is given, what it is for — and they
do this by giving him two of them, without qualification, in the ordinary way. By
the third degree he knows the form.

**Then the form is broken, and he notices, because he has been trained to notice.**

This is elementary dramatic construction and it is also elementary pedagogy. You
cannot subvert an expectation you have not created. The first two degrees are, among
other things, the setup.

Which is one more reason to think the arrangement was arrived at rather than
designed: a designer would have had to plan across three ceremonies developed
decades apart, and there is no evidence anyone did. What is more likely is that the
third degree was built *against* the two that already existed, by people who knew
what those two did and wanted something different — which requires no foresight at
all, only a sense of what would land.

---

### The degrees do not explain themselves

One further feature, and it is the one that produced the entire interpretive
literature.

At no point is the candidate told what any of it means.

He is given words and not told what they signify. He is shown the working tools and
given moral applications for them — this much the ritual does supply — but the
larger architecture is left unglossed. Why a lost word. What it stands for. Whether
the substitution is temporary in any meaningful sense. The ceremony declines to say.

**And no authority within the Craft can say.** This is the consequence of
Anderson's 1723 settlement, which removed doctrinal authority in order to admit men
of differing religions, and which therefore left the Craft permanently unable to
adjudicate an interpretation. There is no body competent to rule that a reading is
wrong.

The result is exactly what one would predict. **Interpretations multiply and are
never removed.** Two centuries of commentators have proposed that the Lost Word
signifies truth, the divine name, moral character, innocence, the knowledge of God,
consciousness, identity, the soul, the self before the Fall, and the secret of
Masonry itself — and every one of these readings is still in circulation, because
nothing exists that could retire one.

Chapter 1 called the proliferation evidence, and here is the principle stated
properly, carried from the preceding volume: **when a tradition's own sources
cannot agree what a symbol means, the most economical explanation is that the
symbol arrived without a settled meaning.**

If the Lost Word had entered Masonic use carrying a specific signification —
inherited, taught, transmitted — we would expect the sources to agree, or at least
to disagree along traceable lines of descent, with rival readings clustering by
jurisdiction or lineage. They do not cluster. They scatter, and they scatter in
proportion to the ingenuity of the commentator.

---

### The one thing that does not vary

Against all that variation, something is remarkably stable.

**Every jurisdiction that has the third degree has the loss.** The substituted word
differs; the staging differs; the wording differs; the explanations differ
completely. The structural fact — that the word is unavailable and the candidate is
told so — is everywhere.

That is worth noticing in a tradition otherwise so various. When Masonic practice
diverges, it diverges thoroughly, and the things that survive the divergence are
the things nobody was willing to change.

**The loss is one of them.** Whatever else the Craft has been prepared to revise
across three centuries and six continents, it has not been prepared to hand the
candidate a word and tell him it is the true one.

I take that to be the strongest available evidence that the refusal is doing
something, and the weakest possible evidence about what.

---

### Leaving Part IV

The Masonic material is now on the table. A word that functions as a credential; a
legend assembled in the 1720s from biblical names; a loss that is narrative in
origin and structural in effect; a substitute that varies and therefore cannot be a
cipher; a recovery degree that undid the refusal and left the refusal standing.

What has not been examined is the machinery that the popular literature insists is
underneath all this: ciphers, magical alphabets, Kabbalistic structure, encoded
numbers.

Part V goes down to that level and asks what a cipher actually is, what Masonry
actually used, and why a body with something to hide would not have chosen it.
