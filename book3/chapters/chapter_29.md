# CHAPTER TWENTY-NINE
## Enochian

This is the most important chapter in Part VI, and it is about a language that did
not exist before 1582 and did afterwards.

We know when it appeared. We know who was in the room. We have the diaries, kept
day by day, in the handwriting of a man who was among the most learned in England.
We have the tables, the alphabet, the vocabulary, the grammar — such as it is — and
the texts.

**It is the one sacred language in this book whose entire birth is documented**, and
what the documentation shows is that it was made.

---

### The two men

**John Dee** was not a crank. He was mathematician to the age, author of the preface
to the first English Euclid, adviser on navigation to the ventures that were
opening the Atlantic, owner of the largest private library in England, consulted by
the Crown. Whatever one concludes about the angelic conversations, they were
conducted by a first-rate mind with nothing to prove.[^1]

**Edward Kelley** was a scryer, a man who looked into a polished surface and
reported what he saw. His character is a matter of some doubt — he had a criminal
past, and the arrangement eventually came apart over a proposal that the two men
should share their wives, which the angels were reported to have commanded and
which Dee accepted with evident distress.

Between 1582 and 1589 the two of them held hundreds of sessions. Kelley looked into
the stone and described. Dee sat at the table and wrote.

---

### How the language was delivered

The method is the part that matters and it is minutely documented.

Angels displayed tables — grids of letters. Kelley reported which cell was being
indicated, letter by letter, and Dee recorded. Much of the material was given
**backwards**, letter by letter in reverse order, on the stated grounds that the
texts were too dangerous to be transmitted forwards.

The result, over seven years, was substantial: a script of twenty-one characters;
a body of text including the "Calls" or "Keys"; a vocabulary with translations
supplied by the angels; a tablet system; and an elaborate apparatus of names
derived by reading letters out of the grids in various directions.[^2]

**The angels also supplied the translations.** This is worth pausing on. The
practitioners did not have to work out what the language meant by philological
labour, because the meaning came with it — which removes the one check that would
otherwise have been available.

---

### Is it a language?

The scholarly assessment is mixed and the mixture is instructive.

**Against**: the syntax of the Enochian texts follows English closely, which is what
one would expect if the material was produced by English speakers and not if it
were an independent tongue. The vocabulary is limited. There is no corpus outside
the sessions. Nobody has demonstrated productive grammar — the ability to generate
new well-formed sentences that competent speakers would recognise.

**For**: the material has considerably more internal structure than random
generation would produce. The alphabet is consistent. The texts are long, and
constructed rather than improvised. Whatever was happening, it was not
straightforward fabrication of a casual kind, and the labour involved was
enormous.

**The honest summary** is that Enochian is a constructed language of limited
extent, produced under unusual conditions, by two men at least one of whom appears
to have believed in what he was doing.

And for this book's purposes, the linguistic verdict hardly matters. What matters
is the provenance, and the provenance is unusually complete.

---

### Why this is the control case

Here is the argument, and it is the reason Enochian sits where it does in the
book's structure.

**Every other sacred language in this book is claimed to have been received.**
Hebrew as the language of creation. The lost pronunciation of the Name. The
Adamic tongue in which names fit their objects. The *voces magicae* as fragments of
something older. In each case the claim is that the material came from elsewhere —
from antiquity, from revelation, from a tradition — and that the practitioners are
custodians rather than authors.

**Enochian is the one case where we can watch**, and what we see is production.

Two named men, in dated sessions, in a documented room, with surviving manuscripts
in their own hands, generated a complete sacred language in seven years.

**They believed they were receiving it.** That is the part that makes the case
valuable rather than merely debunking. Dee's diaries are not the record of a hoax
from his side; they are the record of a man carefully transcribing what he took to
be angelic instruction, worrying about its implications, and following it into
decisions that cost him a great deal.

**Sincere belief in reception is entirely compatible with production.** That is the
finding, and it generalises.

---

### What it licenses and what it does not

Precision here, because this argument can be run too hard.

**It does not prove that every claimed reception was production.** One documented
case establishes that production happens, not that it always happens. Enochian does
not refute the Adamic language or the antiquity of the Hebrew alphabet; it is not
that kind of evidence.

**What it does establish** is that the mechanism exists, that it can generate
material of considerable complexity, that the producers need not be dishonest, and
that from inside the process it does not feel like making.

And it establishes something about the evidential situation. **The one case where
we have complete documentation runs toward production.** Anyone claiming reception
elsewhere — where the documentation is absent — should be asked why the best-attested
instance goes the other way.

That is not a proof. It is a prior, and it is a reasonable one.

---

### The afterlife

Enochian did not stay in Dee's manuscripts.

The papers passed through various hands, were partially published in the
seventeenth century by Meric Casaubon — who printed them to discredit Dee, as an
example of a learned man deceived — and were eventually acquired by the British
Museum.[^3]

In the 1880s the founders of the Golden Dawn found them there and built them into
their system. Enochian became a major component of the order's higher grades, and
through the Golden Dawn it passed to Crowley and to twentieth-century ceremonial
magic generally, where it remains in use.

**Note the pattern**, because it is the same one Book I traced for Masonic material
and Chapter 35 will trace for Kabbalah. A sixteenth-century manuscript sits in a
library. Nineteenth-century occultists find it, adopt it, and build a system. Within
two generations, practitioners describe the material as ancient and the
nineteenth-century assembly drops out of the account.

The distance from Dee's table to a modern magical lodge is four hundred years and
two deliberate reconstructions, and the manuscripts that would show this are in a
public collection.

---

### The Masonic connection: none

Stated for completeness.

**Enochian does not appear in Craft Freemasonry in any period.** Not in the
catechisms, not in the exposures, not in the ritual editions, not in the
jurisdictional variants. There is no Enochian material in Masonic practice.

It appears in the Golden Dawn, which was not a Masonic body — founded by Masons,
borrowing Masonic grade structure, operating on a forged warrant, as Book I
established.

The distinction the series has insisted on throughout applies: **bodies that are
Masonic, bodies whose members were Masons, and bodies that borrowed Masonic form
are three different things**, and conflating them is how practices Masonry does not
contain are routinely attributed to it.

Enochian is in the third category, twice over.
