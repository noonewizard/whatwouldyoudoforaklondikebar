# CHAPTER FORTY
## Pattern Recognition

Three people look at the same wall.

The first sees plaster. The second sees a face in the damp stain above the window
and cannot unsee it. The third sees that the cracks radiate from a point and
concludes the wall is subsiding.

**One of them is right, one is experiencing a well-understood perceptual
phenomenon, and one is looking at plaster.** Nothing about the experience tells any
of them which they are.

---

### What perception does

The visual system does not deliver the world. It delivers a reconstruction, built
from fragmentary and ambiguous input, and the reconstruction is aggressively
committed to finding structure.

**Faces are the clearest case.** We have dedicated neural machinery for face
detection, it operates fast and below conscious control, and it is tuned to fire on
weak evidence — two dots and a line will do. The result is **pareidolia**: faces in
clouds, in electrical sockets, in the front ends of cars, on the surface of Mars.

The tuning is not a bug. The cost matrix is asymmetric. Failing to notice a face
that is there — a predator, a rival, an infant in distress — was historically far
more expensive than noticing one that is not. **A system optimised under that
asymmetry will produce false positives, and ours does, constantly.**

The same asymmetry applies to sequence, causation and meaning. We detect runs in
random data, agency in weather, and intention in coincidence, for the same reason
and with the same reliability.

---

### The experience is not evidence

Here is the finding that matters for this book, and it is uncomfortable.

**A pattern that is genuinely there and a pattern that is not feel exactly the
same.**

There is no phenomenal marker. The click of recognition — the sense that something
has fallen into place — arrives identically whether the structure is real or
constructed. Subjective confidence tracks the *strength of the perceptual signal*,
not its accuracy, and the two are only loosely related.

This is why sincerity is not a defence and why intelligence is not a protection. A
person who reports being struck by a correspondence is reporting accurately on
their own experience, and their experience contains no information about whether
the correspondence is designed.

**Which means the question can only be settled from outside the experience**, by
procedures that do not depend on how compelling it feels. That is what Chapters 41
and 42 are for.

---

### Confirmation bias, and the missing denominator

The second cognitive mechanism, and it compounds the first.

Once a hypothesis is in hand, attention reorganises around it. Confirming instances
are noticed, remembered and counted. Disconfirming instances are not noticed, or
are noticed and not remembered, or are remembered and explained.

**The result is that the denominator disappears.**

Someone convinced that a Masonic symbol encodes a Kabbalistic doctrine will find
correspondences, because correspondences are findable. What they will not assemble
is the list of symbols that failed to correspond, because those were examined,
found unproductive, and forgotten within the hour.

**The published claim reports the hits. The misses were never recorded by anybody,
including the author.**

This is the reason Chapter 39's remedy is a notebook rather than a technique. The
failures have to be written down *as they occur*, because they will not survive
memory. I kept such a record while working on the Atbash material and I recommend
the practice mainly because the record is embarrassing, which is what it is for.

---

### Why clever people are worse at this

A counterintuitive point, and it is reasonably well supported.

Greater knowledge and greater facility do not protect against these failures and in
some respects make them worse.

**A person with a large vocabulary can find more words.** A person who knows Hebrew,
Greek and Latin has three times the target lexicon of a person who knows one. A
person who knows many symbolic systems has more frameworks to map onto.

**And a person with greater facility generates better explanations for anomalies.**
When a datum fails to fit, the skilled interpreter can accommodate it — plausibly,
learnedly, and without noticing that accommodation has replaced testing.

The literature this book examines is not written by fools. It is written, much of
it, by people of real learning: Pike read widely, Mathers taught himself several
languages, Waite's erudition was genuine. **Their learning was the instrument of the
error, not a defence against it**, because it supplied more material to find
patterns in and more resources for explaining away the failures.

The only thing that helps is the procedural discipline that Chapter 42 sets out,
and it helps precisely because it does not depend on the analyst's judgement.

---

### The wall is subsiding

And now the other half, because this chapter would be corrosive if it stopped here.

**Some patterns are real, and the same faculties that produce false positives
produce true ones.**

The third person at the wall — the one who noticed that the cracks radiate from a
point — has made a real observation using the same pattern-detection machinery that
produced the face in the damp stain. Structural engineers rely on exactly that
faculty. So do diagnosticians, navigators, cryptanalysts and archaeologists.

**A tradition of thought that treated all pattern-perception as suspect would be
unable to do science.**

The difference is not in the perceiving. It is in what happens next.

The engineer who notices radiating cracks does not publish. He measures. He checks
other walls in the building. He looks for a documented cause — a drain, a tree, made
ground. He predicts something that could fail: *if this is subsidence, the door
frames on this side will be out of true.* Then he goes and looks at the door frames.

**The noticing is the hypothesis. The checking is the finding.** Everything in this
book that goes wrong goes wrong by publishing the hypothesis.

---

### The honest position

Which yields a stance the rest of Part IX formalises.

**Notice freely.** Pattern-detection is the faculty that generates hypotheses and
there is no reason to suppress it. The person who spots that two Hebrew words share
a value, or that a floor plan admits a pentagram, has done something a researcher
must be able to do.

**Believe cautiously.** The click of recognition is not evidence. It feels
identical in both cases and carries no information about which case you are in.

**And test procedurally.** By methods specified in advance, run against controls,
with the alternatives counted — because the whole point of a procedure is that it
does not consult your confidence.

The next two chapters set out what a demonstrated pattern looks like, and then state
the tests formally.
