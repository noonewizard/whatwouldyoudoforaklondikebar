# CHAPTER FORTY-ONE
## Designed Codes

Everything in this part so far has been about how to be wrong. This chapter is
about what being right looks like, because a method that can only reject is not a
method.

Four cases where concealment is demonstrated, and what makes the demonstration
work.

---

### Case one: the gravestone

Somewhere in a churchyard in England or New England is a headstone with an
inscription in angular marks: hooks, corners, wedges, some with dots.

It is pigpen, and it says a man's name and the year he died.

**Why this is a demonstrated encoding**, item by item:

The object is **physical, located and dated**. It is not a report of a document; it
is a stone with a date on it.

The **rule is documented independently** of the inscription, in cipher literature
and in Masonic sources, and it was described before any particular stone was read.

The rule is **fixed and public before the reading**. Nobody adjusts the grid to make
the output come out.

It **survives a control**: apply the same grid to non-Masonic monuments and nothing
emerges.

The **output is continuous text**, not a single lucky word. Sentences, not
coincidences.

It **replicates**: hand the grid to a stranger and they read the same inscription.

It is **historically plausible**: Masons demonstrably used the cipher.

And somebody **carved it**, which settles intentionality in the most literal way
available.

**Ten for ten**, and note what it encodes: a name and a date. A genuine cipher,
genuinely used, demonstrably intentional, carrying nothing secret whatever.

---

### Case two: Trithemius

The *Steganographia*, composed 1499, presents itself as a treatise on angel magic.
Substantial parts of it are a cryptographic manual, with ciphertext concealed inside
the invocations and the spirit names functioning as keys.

**Why this is demonstrated:** the key was published and the method reproduced.
Somebody worked out the system, stated it, and showed that applying it to the text
produces coherent messages — and others then did the same thing independently and
got the same results.

**This is the crucial feature and it deserves emphasis.** Trithemius did not tell
anyone. The concealment was discovered, and it was established as concealment
because the discoverers published a procedure that other people could run.

**A decipherment that can be handed to a stranger, who then reproduces the result
without further guidance, has been demonstrated.** One that requires the discoverer
to be present, adjusting, is not a decipherment.

---

### Case three: Enochian

Chapter 29's case, and it passes for different reasons.

**Provenance**: manuscripts in Dee's hand, in a public collection.
**Dating**: sessions recorded day by day between 1582 and 1589.
**Rule**: the tables and the method of derivation are written down.
**Replication**: the derivations can be checked and reproduce.
**Intentionality**: Dee wrote it all down, including his doubts.

What Enochian demonstrates is not a concealment but a **construction**, and the
construction is as thoroughly evidenced as anything in this book.

**The category had to be corrected** — it is a produced language, not a recovered
one — and the correction was possible because the evidence was complete enough to
support it. That is what good evidence does: it tells you not only whether a claim
is true but what kind of claim it actually was.

---

### Case four: the acrostic psalms

The oldest of the four and the most secure.

Several psalms are alphabetic acrostics: each verse, or group of verses, begins
with a successive letter of the Hebrew alphabet. Psalm 119 does it in eight-verse
blocks, twenty-two of them, one per letter.

**Why this needs no argument at all:** the pattern is structurally undeniable once
pointed out, requires no transformation to see, and could not arise by chance in a
text of that length. It is visible in the Hebrew to any reader who looks.

**This is the gold standard**, and it is worth naming what makes it so. There is no
procedure between the text and the pattern. Nothing is substituted, transformed,
reordered or interpreted. **You look, and it is there.**

Compare with any claim in this book that requires a transformation. The
transformation is where the search space lives, and the search space is where
spurious results come from. A pattern that needs no transformation has no search
space and therefore no room for selection.

---

### What the four have in common

Five features. A claim possessing all five is demonstrated; a claim missing any is
not yet.

**The rule exists independently of the claim.** Somebody other than the claimant
documented it, or it is structurally self-evident. The pigpen grid is in the cipher
literature. The alphabet is the alphabet.

**The rule was fixed before the result was known.** No adjustment to make the output
work.

**The output is substantial.** Continuous text, or a structure spanning a whole
composition — not a single word, which is what chance supplies.

**It survives a control.** Applied to matched material that cannot contain the
message, it yields nothing.

**And there is independent evidence of intention.** Somebody carved it, wrote it,
composed it — and we know this from something other than the pattern itself.

**That last point is the whole of Part IX in a sentence.** Where the only evidence
for design is the pattern, there is no evidence for design.

---

### A fifth case that fails, and why it belongs here

The four cases above all pass, which risks giving the impression that the tests are
easy once a claim is genuine. A borderline case is more instructive.

**The Voynich manuscript.** An illustrated codex, carbon-dated to the early
fifteenth century, written in an unknown script that has resisted every attempt at
decipherment for a century.

Run the tests on the *claim that it encodes a message*.

**Provenance: passes.** The manuscript exists, its ownership can be traced back some
centuries, and the vellum is dated.

**Dating: passes.** Early fifteenth century, established physically.

**Encoding rule: fails.** No rule has been documented. Dozens have been proposed and
none has been independently confirmed.

**Replication: fails, spectacularly.** This is the decisive one. Many people have
announced solutions. **No two have agreed**, and no proposed method has enabled a
stranger to reproduce the result unaided.

**Intentionality: passes.** Somebody wrote it, deliberately, at length, with
illustrations. The labour is undeniable.

So the Voynich passes the tests that concern the object and fails the tests that
concern the decipherments. **This is exactly the right result**, and it shows the
protocol discriminating properly: there is certainly something there — somebody made
it on purpose — and no one has shown what.

**The honest position on the Voynich is that it is an unsolved problem**, which is a
rarer and more respectable category than either "decoded" or "meaningless." A
protocol that could not produce that verdict would be too blunt to be useful.

And it supplies the contrast this chapter needs. Where a genuine encoding exists and
has been solved, the solutions converge — everyone with the pigpen grid reads the
same inscription. Where they do not converge, that is itself the finding.

---

### The asymmetry, and why it is fair

A final observation, because the standard above may look severe.

**A demonstrated encoding is cheap to establish and a disputed one is expensive.**

The gravestone takes a paragraph: here is the stone, here is the grid, here is the
text, go and look. Psalm 119 takes a sentence. Trithemius took some work and then
was settled.

The Atbash claim of Chapter 16 took three rounds of computation and a very
uncomfortable question about who wrote the answer key, and the result was still
negative.

**This asymmetry is not a bias against the interesting claims.** It is a property of
the evidence. Real encodings announce themselves — they produce sentences, they
replicate, they come with carvers and authors and manuscripts. Claimed encodings
that require elaborate defence require it because the evidence is thin, and the
elaboration is doing the work the evidence cannot.

**When a decipherment needs a long argument, the argument is the finding.**

Chapter 42 states the tests formally, and runs them.
