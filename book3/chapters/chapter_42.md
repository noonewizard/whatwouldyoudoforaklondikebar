# CHAPTER FORTY-TWO
## The Evidence Test

Here is the apparatus. It applies to any claim that something is concealed in a
text, a name, a number, a building or a symbol, and it is the most portable thing
in this book.

It is not a machine for saying no. Two of the four claims worked through below
**pass**, and a test that could not pass anything would be worthless.

---

## THE TEN TESTS

**1 — PROVENANCE.** Where did the material come from? Who held it, when, and who
vouches for the chain?

A decipherment of a document of unknown origin decodes nothing, because there is no
author to have done the encoding. Before asking what a text conceals, establish that
it is the text somebody wrote.

**2 — DATING.** When was the alleged encoding performed?

Date the artefact, not the tradition it claims. A claim requiring an
eighteenth-century author to encode a nineteenth-century doctrine is finished before
it starts, and a surprising number of claims are of exactly this shape.

**3 — ENCODING RULE.** Is the cipher independently documented as a real system, used
by real people, described somewhere other than in the claim itself?

**A rule reconstructed by the claimant is not a rule.** If two people advancing the
same claim reconstruct it differently — and they generally do — there is no cipher,
only a text and several readers.

**4 — PRECOMMITMENT.** Was the method specified before the result was seen?

**This is the decisive test and almost every claim fails it.** And it extends
further than most analysts notice: it covers not only the procedure but the
*acceptance criterion*. What would have counted as a meaningful output, and who
decided, and when?

Chapter 16 failed this test in its own controls and it took three attempts to
notice.

**5 — CONTROLS.** Run the identical procedure on matched material that cannot
contain the message. Does it yield comparably?

**Matched** is the operative word. The controls must match in *search-space size*,
not merely in subject matter. A control given fewer chances than the claim is not a
control; it is a demonstration that larger spaces find more, which was known.

**6 — BASE RATE.** How often does a meaningful output arise by chance, given the
size of the search space and the generosity of the acceptance criterion?

State both numbers. A result is only surprising relative to how often it happens
anyway.

**7 — REPLICATION.** Can an independent researcher, given only the stated method,
reproduce the result without further guidance?

If the claimant must intervene — *not quite, try this spelling, allow that vowel* —
the method is underspecified and what is being transmitted is judgment, not
procedure.

**8 — HISTORICAL PLAUSIBILITY.** Could the alleged author have used this method?

Did they have the language, the technique, the materials, the motive? Would they
have chosen a system that was already obsolete? Chapter 25's dates do a great deal
of work here.

**9 — ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS.** Is there a simpler account?

Coincidence. A common source. Convergent design. A scribal convention. A later
interpolation. The alternative does not have to be proved; it has to be excluded.

**10 — INTENTIONALITY.** Is there evidence that the pattern was *put there*?

Not that it is present, and not that it is meaningful — but that someone placed it.
**This is the only test that turns an observation into a finding, and no amount of
output can satisfy it.**

### Scoring

A claim failing **4** or **5** carries no evidential weight, however striking its
output.
A claim failing **2** or **8** is dead.
A claim failing only **9** may be true and is not yet demonstrated.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — THE BIBLE CODE — **FAILS**

In 1997 Michael Drosnin published *The Bible Code*, claiming that equidistant
letter sequences in the Hebrew text of Genesis encoded modern events, including
assassinations.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 Provenance | **Pass** — Masoretic text, well established |
| 2 Dating | Fail — requires foreknowledge of modern events |
| 3 Encoding rule | Fail — ELS is not an attested ancient practice |
| 4 Precommitment | **Fail** — skip distances and targets chosen after inspection |
| 5 Controls | **Fail decisively** |
| 6 Base rate | Fail — search space effectively unbounded |
| 7 Replication | Fail |
| 8 Plausibility | Fail |
| 9 Alternatives | Fail — chance suffices |
| 10 Intentionality | Fail |

**Test 5 is the one that ended it.** In 1999 Brendan McKay, Dror Bar-Natan, Maya
Bar-Hillel and Gil Kalai published in *Statistical Science* the control that should
have been run first: they applied the same method to other texts, and McKay found
assassination "predictions" for Indira Gandhi, Yitzhak Rabin, both Kennedys, Martin
Luther King, Abraham Lincoln and Leon Trotsky — **in the English text of
*Moby-Dick*.**[^1]

The method works. That is precisely what is wrong with it.

**Those twenty pages are the model for this entire chapter**, and a reader who takes
nothing else from this book should read them.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — ATBASH / BAPHOMET — **FAILS, AND NOT WHERE EXPECTED**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 Provenance | **Fail** — inquisitorial testimony under torture; the name appears in wildly variant forms |
| 2 Dating | Fail — the claim is Schonfield's, 1984 |
| 3 Encoding rule | **Pass** — Atbash is genuinely attested (Jer 25:26, 51:41, 51:1) |
| 4 Precommitment | **Fail** — the target lexicon was built knowing the answer |
| 5 Controls | **Fail** — matched controls return zero; so does Baphomet once *Sophia* is removed |
| 6 Base rate | Fail — 216 spellings against a generous target list |
| 7 Replication | **Pass** — the transformation reproduces exactly |
| 8 Plausibility | Fail — no evidence the Templars used Atbash, or Hebrew |
| 9 Alternatives | Fail |
| 10 Intentionality | Fail |

**Note what passes.** Tests 3 and 7 pass cleanly. The technique is real and the
arithmetic is exact. This is why the claim persuades, and why the assessment has to
go further than calling it nonsense. **It is not nonsense. It is an accurate
computation whose interpretation was fixed after the result.**

And even holding, it would be a claim about the Templars, and the Templar-Masonry
chain fails independently.

---

## WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — PIGPEN GRAVESTONES — **PASSES**

Ten for ten, as Chapter 41 set out. Physical monuments, dated, with an
independently documented rule fixed before reading, surviving controls, producing
continuous text, replicating in any stranger's hands, historically plausible, and
carved by somebody.

**This is what a demonstrated historical encoding looks like.** What it encodes is a
name and a date.

---

## WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — ENOCHIAN — **PASSES, CATEGORY CORRECTED**

Passes on provenance, dating, rule, replication and intentionality. The manuscripts
survive, the sessions are dated, the tables reproduce, and Dee wrote it down.

**But it is not a recovered language. It is a produced one** — and the evidence was
good enough to establish not merely that the claim was false but what kind of thing
had actually happened.

That is the best outcome a test can deliver, and it is why the apparatus is worth
having.

### Applying the tests to a claim you like

The protocol is easy to use on other people's claims and hard to use on your own,
and a chapter that did not say so would be incomplete.

Three observations from having tried.

**The tests are experienced as obstacles once a result is in hand.** Before the
result, a control is an obvious precaution. Afterward, it is a thing standing
between you and an interesting finding, and the mind supplies reasons why it is not
quite applicable here. I felt this distinctly at the second stage of the Atbash work
and I am not confident I would have gone on to the third if the book had not been
committed to printing its method.

**Test 4 is the one people exempt themselves from.** Everybody agrees in principle
that the procedure should be specified in advance. Almost nobody does it, because at
the moment when it would have to be done — before starting — it feels premature. You
do not yet know what you are looking for. That is the point, and it is also why the
discipline feels wrong from inside.

**And Test 10 is the one that is quietly skipped.** A striking pattern feels like
evidence of intention, so strongly that the question *what independent evidence is
there that someone placed this?* can seem pedantic. It is not pedantic. It is the
entire difference between an observation and a finding.

**The practical remedy is to write the tests down as a checklist and go through them
in order, in writing, including the ones that seem obviously satisfied.** This
sounds bureaucratic. It is bureaucratic. Bureaucracy is what you use when judgment
is known to be unreliable, and Chapter 40 established that on this particular
question everyone's judgment is unreliable, including the judgment of people who
have read Chapter 40.

### What the protocol cannot do

Three limits, stated so the reader does not over-trust it.

**It cannot establish that nothing is hidden.** Failing the tests means a claim is
not demonstrated, not that it is false. The Royal Arch question in Chapter 19 sits
at E4 for exactly this reason: no route has been shown, and no route has been shown
not to exist.

**It cannot weigh a claim that makes no testable move at all.** A reading that
declines to specify a procedure — "the symbolism suggests" — is not failing the
tests; it is outside their scope. Such readings may be valuable as interpretation,
and Chapter 52 argues that interpretation is what Masonic symbolism mostly invites.
They are simply not claims about what anybody encoded.

**And it cannot supply the historical knowledge that Tests 1, 2 and 8 require.**
Provenance, dating and plausibility are answered by archives, not by procedure. The
protocol tells you what to ask; the answers come from work.

---

### The distinction underneath everything

Stated once, plainly, because it governs all ten tests.

**A symbol can acquire meaning its maker never intended.** Legitimate; a fact about
reception; not evidence about origin.

**A reader can discover a real interpretation that was never placed there.** Also
legitimate, and not a discovery about the author.

**A coincidence can be meaningful and remain a coincidence.** Meaning is not scarce.
Intention is.

**An intentionally encoded message requires evidence of intentional encoding** —
which is Test 10, and which no quantity of output can supply.

---

### The discipline, in four lines

Everything above compresses to this, and it is what I would want a reader to carry
out of the book.

**State the procedure before you run it**, in enough detail that a stranger could
execute it without asking a question.

**Count the attempts, including the ones you abandoned.** Keep the list. It will be
embarrassing, which is what it is for.

**Build the control first** — before you know what the real material will yield, and
before the exciting result has made the control an obstacle.

**And notice who wrote the answer key.** If the criterion for success was set by
someone who already had the result, nothing has been tested, however rigorous
everything downstream looks.

That last line cost me three rounds of computation to learn properly, and it is the
sentence I would keep if I could keep only one.
