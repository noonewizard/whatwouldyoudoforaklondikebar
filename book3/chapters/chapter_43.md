# CHAPTER FORTY-THREE
## The Decoders

There is a large body of contemporary work on Masonic ciphers, produced outside the
academy, circulated online, and largely ignored by scholars.

This chapter takes it seriously, which means neither adopting its conclusions nor
ridiculing its authors. The brief for this book is explicit on the point and it is
the right instruction: show the difference between an interesting observation and a
demonstrated historical encoding, and let the difference do the work.

---

### What the material is

The genre is recognisable. Videos, forum threads, self-published books, long posts
with diagrams. The recurring subjects:

**Atbash applied to Masonic words**, following Schonfield's Baphomet argument into
new material.

**Gematria on English Masonic vocabulary**, usually A=1 through Z=26, with results
matched against a list of significant numbers.

**"Letter and halve it" reconstructed as a cipher**, with a rule supplied by the
interpreter.

**Geometry in city plans** — Washington above all, and the diagonal avenues that
can be made to yield pentagrams, compasses and squares.

**Numerical patterns** in degree counts, dates, dimensions and architectural
features.

**And symbol identification** — an all-seeing eye here, a pyramid there, a
correspondence proposed.

---

### The observations are often real

This is the part that a dismissive treatment gets wrong, and it matters.

**Washington does have diagonal avenues**, and they do produce angles, and some of
those angles are close to the angles of familiar figures. That is a fact about the
plan. L'Enfant laid out radiating avenues from focal points because that was the
baroque planning idiom he had absorbed, and radiating avenues necessarily generate
angles.

**English gematria does produce collisions.** The arithmetic is correct. When
someone reports that two Masonic words share a value, they have generally done the
sum right.

**Atbash does transform Hebrew strings into other Hebrew strings**, and some outputs
do resemble words.

**The all-seeing eye is genuinely a Masonic symbol**, and genuinely appears on the
reverse of the Great Seal, and the two facts are genuinely related in the sense that
both draw on a shared eighteenth-century visual vocabulary of providence.

**None of these observations is false.** They are, for the most part, accurate
reports of what the observer found.

---

### The inference is what fails

What separates this material from scholarship is not accuracy but **the step from
observation to conclusion**, and the step fails in the same four places every time.

**Test 4 — precommitment.** The procedure is almost never specified in advance. The
analyst tries transformations until something emerges, and the emerging is what
selects the method. Nothing in the write-up records the attempts that failed,
because they were not recorded by anybody.

**Test 5 — controls.** Controls are essentially never run. The same gematria is not
applied to a matched list of ordinary words. The same geometric fitting is not
applied to another city. Nobody asks what the method returns from material that
could not contain the pattern.

**Test 6 — base rate.** Search-space size is not stated, usually not computed, and
often not conceived of as a question.

**Test 10 — intentionality.** The pattern is taken as its own evidence of design.
This is the central error and it is almost universal: *here is a striking
correspondence, therefore someone placed it.*

**Four tests out of ten, and they are the four that matter.**

---

### The Washington case, worked

Because it is the most famous and the most instructive.

The claim: the street plan of Washington encodes Masonic symbols — a pentagram
north of the White House, a compass, a square, deliberate alignments.

**The observation is accurate.** Lines drawn between certain points do produce those
figures, approximately.

**The procedure was not specified in advance.** Which points count as significant?
The plan offers hundreds — circles, squares, intersections, the termination of every
avenue. Which lines are drawn? Between which of those points? How close must an
angle be to count?

**Nobody stated the tolerance.** And an unstated tolerance is set, unconsciously, by
whether the answer came out.

**And no control was run.** The preceding volume of this series ran one. I generated
a plain rectangular lattice — twelve by eight, ninety-six points with slight jitter,
the spacing of an ordinary street grid, containing nothing by construction — and
searched for a regular pentagram whose vertices land on lattice points. Four hundred
thousand random placements, refined by local optimisation.

**Result: a pentagram with maximum vertex deviation of 0.20 grid units — 5.7 per
cent of its own circumradius.** On a drawing at any normal scale, the line weight
alone would swallow the error. Reproduced as a figure with five points marked and
the other ninety-one omitted — which is how such figures are always reproduced — it
would look like a finding.[^1]

It is a grid.

**The method finds pentagrams in anything.** That is not an argument that Washington
contains none. It is the observation that this method cannot tell us, because it
returns a positive result either way, and a test that cannot come back negative is
not a test.

---

### The gematria case, worked

A second example, because it is the commonest claim in the corpus and the
arithmetic makes it checkable.

**The claim**, in its general form: certain Masonic words share numerical values
under A=1 to Z=26, and the sharing indicates design.

**The observations are accurate.** LEVEL and LIGHT both come to 56. APRON and PLUMB
both come to 64. The sums are right and anyone can verify them.

**The procedure was not specified in advance.** A=1 or A=0? Include or exclude
vowels? Reverse the alphabet? Sum the letters, or take the product? Use the English
spelling or a biblical one — HIRAM, CHIRAM or HURAM? **Each choice is defensible and
each gives different numbers**, and a researcher who tries several and reports the
one that worked has selected a pattern rather than found one.

**No control was run.** The preceding volume ran one: twenty Masonic words against
twenty household objects and root vegetables, both lists fixed in advance, identical
procedure. **Three internal pairs on each side. One significant-number hit on each
side.** TESCO = PUDDLE = 62; KETTLE = SAUSAGE = 73; POTATO = SPANNER = 87; PARSNIP =
93.

**The parsnips performed exactly as well as the pillars.**

**And the base rate explains it entirely.** Twenty words make 190 possible pairs,
spread over a range of about eighty values. Two or three collisions is what chance
predicts. Three is what both sets produced.

**Nothing was hidden in either list**, and the collisions are a property of the
arithmetic rather than of the words.

**There is also a chronological problem that disposes of the claim independently.**
English letters have no numerical values and never had. Any A=1 to Z=26 scheme is a
modern invention with no tradition behind it — unlike Hebrew, where the letters
genuinely served as numerals. Applying "gematria" to English Masonic vocabulary is
not the use of an ancient technique. It is a new procedure with an old name, and it
could not have been used by anyone in the eighteenth century because it did not
exist.

That is a Test 8 failure, and it is fatal on its own.

---

### Why people do this

Three reasons, none of them contemptible.

**Because the observations are genuinely interesting.** A person who notices that a
street plan admits a pentagram has noticed something, and the noticing is the same
cognitive act that produces real discoveries.

**Because the institutional structures that supply controls are absent.** An
academic researcher has supervisors, referees, and a discipline that will ask
embarrassing questions. An independent researcher has none of these, and the
questions do not occur spontaneously — they are a professional habit, acquired by
having them asked of you.

**And because the subject rewards it.** Freemasonry presents itself as containing
secrets. It says so. A reader who takes that at face value and goes looking is
responding rationally to what the institution advertises.

**This last point deserves weight.** The preceding volume argued that Masonic
secrecy is structural rather than informational — that the secret is that there is
no secret of the kind sought. But nothing in Masonic self-presentation says so, and
a person outside the system has no way to know it. **The Craft advertises a mystery
and then declines to explain that the mystery is not the kind you can solve.**

---

### What would change the picture

The constructive version, because a chapter of criticism owes one.

An independent researcher who wanted to establish a claim of this kind could do it,
and the requirements are not onerous.

**Write the procedure down first.** Publish it before running it, ideally somewhere
timestamped.

**State the tolerance in advance.** How close is a hit?

**Count the attempts.** Publish the denominator, including the transformations that
produced nothing.

**Run one control.** One. Apply the identical method to matched material that cannot
contain the pattern — another city, a list of ordinary words, a text from the wrong
century.

**And say what would change your mind.**

None of this requires funding, institutional affiliation, or specialist training.
It requires a notebook and the willingness to run the procedure that might take the
discovery away.

**That willingness is the whole difference**, and it is not a difference of
intelligence or of access. It is a difference of method, and method can be learned
by anybody in an afternoon.
