# PART XI — THE WORD AS KNOWLEDGE

# CHAPTER FORTY-SEVEN
## Knowing and Speaking

There are at least six different things a person can mean by saying they know a
word, and the Masonic system moves through most of them.

This chapter sets them out. The rest of Part XI asks which ones the ceremony
actually supplies.

---

### The six layers

**One: INFORMATION.** *I know the word.* I have the string. I could write it down,
spell it, recognise it if I heard it.

This is the thinnest relation and it is what the exposures transferred. Anyone who
bought *Masonry Dissected* in 1730 for sixpence acquired it.

**Two: KNOWLEDGE.** *I know what the word means.* I understand what it refers to,
what tradition it belongs to, what it is doing in the sentence where I found it.

Considerably more than information, and still available from a book.

**Three: AUTHORITY.** *I am permitted to use it.* I stand in a relation to an
institution that entitles me to employ the word in the contexts where it operates.

**Here the book stops being enough.** Chapter 3 established why: an utterance
requires appropriate persons as well as appropriate words, and the man in the
registry office had the words and no standing. Authority is conferred, not
acquired.

**Four: EXPERIENCE.** *I have undergone the ritual associated with it.* I was in the
room. It was done to me.

This is what initiation supplies and nothing else does. It is not information and
cannot be transmitted as such.

**Five: EMBODIMENT.** *I live according to what it signifies.* The word's content
has become a disposition — something visible in conduct rather than held in mind.

**Six: WISDOM.** *I understand when and why it matters.* Judgement about the
occasion: knowing which situations call for the thing the word names, and which do
not.

---

### Why the layers are not a ladder

An important qualification before the scheme is used, because schemes of this kind
invite a reading they do not support.

**These are not stages one passes through in order, and a person can have any
combination.**

A scholar can have one and two in abundance and none of the rest. A member who was
initiated and never returned has four without three in any meaningful sense, because
authority unexercised decays. A man may have five without two — living decently
without being able to articulate why — and a great many people do.

And six is not the crown of a sequence. Judgement about occasions is its own thing
and does not follow from the others; there are learned fools and wise illiterates,
and everyone knows it.

**What the scheme is for** is not ranking but **separation**. It exists so that
claims about "knowing the word" can be assigned to a layer, because almost all the
confusion in this subject comes from a claim at one layer being answered by a
denial at another.

*The word is not secret* — true at layer one. *The word cannot be given in print* —
true at layers three and four. Both parties are right and they are not
disagreeing.

---

### The exposures, layered

Run the scheme against the event that should have destroyed Freemasonry and did
not.

In 1730 the ritual was printed. What was transferred?

**Layer one: completely.** Every reader had the words.

**Layer two: substantially.** The exposures explain what things mean, more or less
accurately.

**Layer three: not at all.** A reader had no standing whatever. He could not be
admitted, could not confer a degree, could not be received as a visitor except by
deceit.

**Layer four: not at all, and not even in principle.** Reading about being
blindfolded in a dark room is not being blindfolded in a dark room, and nobody has
ever confused the two.

**Layers five and six: not at all.**

**The publication transferred the two thinnest layers and nothing else**, and the
institution's survival is thereby explained without any appeal to deeper secrets.
The exposures gave away what could be given away, which was the part that was never
carrying the weight.

This is the finding the preceding volume reached from ritual structure and Chapter
20 reached from security design. Three routes, one conclusion.

---

### Where the ceremony operates

Now the interesting question: which layers does Masonic initiation actually supply?

**Information: yes, and trivially.** The candidate is given words.

**Knowledge: partially.** He is given moral applications for the working tools and
is told a great deal about what things signify. He is not told what the larger
architecture means, because nobody can tell him — Chapter 21 established that no
authority within the Craft can adjudicate an interpretation.

**Authority: yes, and this is the ceremony's core business.** He leaves entitled to
things he was not entitled to before: to be received in lodges, to claim relief, to
participate, eventually to confer. **The degree confers standing, and standing is
exactly what cannot be got from a book.**

**Experience: yes, necessarily.** It was done to him.

**Embodiment and wisdom: not conferred, and the Craft does not claim to confer
them.** What it supplies is an instruction — square your conduct, keep your passions
within bounds — with no criterion, no assessor and no completion. The preceding
volume found no evidence that the instruction produces the disposition, and no
possibility of such evidence for any historical period.

---

### And the layer that is withheld

Which brings the scheme to the point it was built for.

The third degree gives the candidate a word at layer one — he has the string,
labelled as a substitute. It gives him authority at layer three: he is a Master
Mason with everything that entails.

**And it tells him that the thing itself is unavailable.**

Read through the scheme, the announcement is precise in a way the ceremony cannot
have intended. **He is given the thinnest layer and told that it is not the thing.**

Chapter 18 put it as a separation: the degree separates the word from everything
that makes a word work, and hands him the wrong half on purpose. The six layers
give that a finer resolution. He has information and authority and experience. What
he is told he lacks is whatever the word *refers to* — and the referent is not a
string, and never could have been handed over.

**Nobody in the eighteenth century thought in these terms.** The vocabulary did not
exist and the ceremony was assembled under ordinary institutional pressure from
materials to hand.

But the structure is there, and Chapter 50 will argue that its being there is why
the ceremony has outlasted everything built around it.
