## THE FACT THAT ORGANISES PART III

**Hebrew was written without vowels.** The consonantal text is ancient; systematic
vowel-pointing is the work of the Masoretes, principally at Tiberias, between
roughly the seventh and tenth centuries CE.

This single fact disposes of more confusion than any other in the book.

- A "lost pronunciation" is not an anomaly requiring mystical explanation. **It is
  the default condition of any word in a consonantal script whose oral tradition
  lapses.**
- The vowel points attached to יהוה in the Masoretic text are **not** the vowels of
  the Name. They are the *qere perpetuum*: the pointing of *Adonai*, placed there to
  cue the reader to substitute.
- "Jehovah" is the result of reading those substitution vowels as though they
  belonged to the consonants — a Christian misreading, generally traced to Galatinus
  (1518) and in circulation thereafter. **It is a scribal artefact, not a name.**

Sources: Khan, *A Short Introduction to the Tiberian Masoretic Bible* (2013);
Yeivin, *Introduction to the Tiberian Masorah* (1980); Tov, *Textual Criticism*
(3rd edn, 2012).

## THE DIVINE NAMES — KEPT APART

| Name | Form | Sense | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **YHWH** | יהוה | The proper name | Not pronounced in Jewish practice; *Adonai* or *ha-Shem* substituted |
| **Ehyeh asher ehyeh** | אהיה אשר אהיה | "I am / will be what I am / will be" | Exod 3:14. **Not a name given but arguably a name withheld** |
| **Elohim** | אלהים | God; grammatically plural, syntactically singular | Gematria 86 |
| **Adonai** | אדני | "My Lord" | The spoken substitute. Gematria 65 |
| **El Shaddai** | אל שדי | Traditionally "God Almighty"; etymology disputed | Shaddai 314 |
| **Tzevaot** | צבאות | "Hosts" | Epithet |

**These are distinct names with distinct histories and distinct textual
distributions**, and the documentary hypothesis and its successors have spent a
century on why. The popular literature merges them into "the name of God," which
makes every claim about "the Name" ambiguous at the outset.

**Exod 3:14 deserves its own treatment (ch 9).** Moses asks for a name. What he
receives is a sentence — a first-person verbal form, arguably a refusal, and
certainly not a word of the kind he asked for. **A tradition whose foundational
name-episode is a non-answer is the right place for this book to begin its Hebrew
material**, and the parallel with a substituted word is worth noting without
claiming descent.

## THE ALPHABET AS SYSTEM (ch 11)

- 22 consonants; 5 with final forms; matres lectionis (א ה ו י) doing partial vowel
  duty.
- Letters as numerals — the basis of gematria. **Note the direction: the numerical
  use is a consequence of the alphabet lacking separate numerals, not a mystical
  design.** Greek does the same thing with the same result (isopsephy), which is why
  both traditions developed number-word play independently.
- Letter *names* are words: *alef* (ox), *bet* (house), *dalet* (door), *ayin*
  (eye), *pe* (mouth), *resh* (head). The pictographic origins are real and the
  mystical readings built on them are later.

## THE TECHNIQUES (chs 14–15)

**Gematria** — numerical equivalence. Three systems tabulated and verified in
`research/computational_verification/gematria.py`. **Within its own tradition a
homiletic device, not a recovery method.**

**Notarikon** — a word read as an acronym, or an acronym built from a phrase.

**Temurah** — systematic letter substitution, of which **Atbash** is one scheme
among several (Albam, Atbaḥ and others are also attested).

**The constraint that makes them work and whose removal breaks them:** all three
operated inside a community with a fixed canon, a shared language, and rabbinic
judgement about what counted as a reading worth making. **Remove canon, community
and judgement, and the same techniques generate unlimited results from anything** —
which is the finding of the base-rate computation and the subject of Part IX.
