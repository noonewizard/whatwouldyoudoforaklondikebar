# KABBALAH RESEARCH MAP — AND THE §VIII PROTOCOL APPLIED

The brief requires this book to be "especially rigorous" about Kabbalah. The
rigour consists in keeping four things apart that the popular literature merges,
and then testing every proposed Masonic link against eight questions.

---

## THE FOUR KABBALAHS

| | **Jewish Kabbalah** | **Christian Cabala** | **Hermetic Qabalah** | **Modern occult Qabalah** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Period** | c. 1150– | 1486– | 1854–1900 | 20th c.– |
| **Language** | Hebrew, Aramaic | Latin, from Hebrew | English, French | English |
| **Key figures** | Isaac the Blind; Abulafia; Moses de León; Cordovero; Luria | Pico; Reuchlin; Knorr von Rosenroth | Lévi; Mathers; Westcott | Crowley; Regardie; Fortune |
| **Key texts** | *Bahir*, *Sefer Yetzirah*, *Zohar*, *Etz Chaim* | *De arte cabalistica*; *Kabbala Denudata* (1677) | Golden Dawn papers; *777* | Successor literature |
| **Purpose** | Exegesis and devotion within halakhic life | Christian apologetic — to prove the Trinity and Christ's messiahship from Jewish sources | A universal correspondence grammar | Personal magical practice |
| **The Tree** | A structure of divine emanation | Re-read christologically | **Cross-mapped to tarot, planets, Egyptian gods, elements** | As inherited |
| **Tarot attribution** | **None** | **None** | **Introduced — Lévi, 1850s** | Central |

**The single most consequential fact in this table:** the tarot–Hebrew letter
correspondence, which most modern readers assume is ancient Kabbalah, is Lévi's,
and dates from the 1850s. Anything resting on it is nineteenth-century French.

**The second:** Christian Cabala was not a neutral transmission. Pico and Reuchlin
were arguing *against* Judaism using its own mystical vocabulary. Material was
selected, re-pointed and re-read for that purpose. Hermetic Qabalah inherited the
Christian version, not the Jewish one — so the chain to any Masonic borrowing runs
through two deliberate transformations before it arrives.

## THE EIGHT QUESTIONS (§VIII), APPLIED

For every proposed Masonic–Kabbalistic connection:

1. When does the connection first appear?
2. Who made it?
3. What source documents it?
4. Was that person actually a Mason?
5. Was the relevant Kabbalistic system historically available?
6. Is there evidence of transmission?
7. Is the interpretation modern?
8. Is the relationship accepted, disputed, or speculative?

### Claim A — The two pillars are the Kabbalistic pillars of Severity and Mercy

| Q | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | Nineteenth century |
| 2 | Lévi, then Pike (1871), then the Golden Dawn |
| 3 | *Morals and Dogma*; Golden Dawn papers |
| 4 | Pike yes; Lévi no; Mathers/Westcott yes but the claim is made in a non-Masonic body |
| 5 | Available in Latin from 1677 |
| 6 | **None for the eighteenth century.** The pillars are in 1 Kings 7 / 2 Chron 3, which requires no Kabbalah |
| 7 | **Yes** |
| 8 | **Speculative.** The simpler source is scripture |

**Verdict: E5.** A nineteenth-century overlay on a biblical borrowing.

### Claim B — The Royal Arch's recovered Name is Kabbalistic

| Q | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | The degree itself, 1740s; Kabbalistic *readings* of it, 19th c. |
| 2 | Degree compilers unknown; readings by Oliver, Pike and after |
| 3 | Ritual texts (jurisdictionally variable); later commentary |
| 4 | Yes for the commentators |
| 5 | Knorr available from 1677; Christian Hebraism active |
| 6 | **Not established.** No route identified |
| 7 | The Kabbalistic reading is; the degree is not |
| 8 | **Disputed — and this is the strongest remaining candidate** |

**Verdict: E4.** Book II reached the same conclusion and left it open; Book III
must do the serious work. The concealment and recovery of a divine Name is a
specific Jewish preoccupation, it appears in Masonry a century before Lévi, and no
transmission route has been found. **It is also fully constructible from the
biblical and Christian-Hebraist materials any educated Protestant of the 1740s
could reach**, and the degree's apparatus is architectural and vault-centred rather
than sefirotic — which is weak evidence for independent construction, not proof.

### Claim C — Masonic gematria encodes Kabbalistic doctrine

| Q | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | Chiefly late 19th–20th c., and online after c. 2000 |
| 3 | Popular literature; no Masonic ritual source |
| 6 | **None.** Craft ritual contains no gematria |
| 8 | **Unsupported** |

**Verdict: E6.** English-letter gematria applied to Masonic words is not a Masonic
practice, not a Kabbalistic practice, and fails the base-rate test computed in

### Claim D — The 33 degrees correspond to the 32 paths plus one

**Verdict: E6.** Thirty-three is the Charleston degree count of 1801. The 32 paths
(10 sephirot + 22 letters) are from *Sefer Yetzirah*. The resemblance is arithmetic
coincidence between two numbers that arose independently, and the "plus one" is
supplied by the claimant to make it work — a textbook Test 4 failure.

## THE STANDING RULE

**Availability is not transmission.** *Kabbala Denudata* sat in libraries that
eighteenth-century Masons could reach. So did a great deal else. To convert
availability into transmission requires a person, a text, a date and a verbal
dependence — and for the Craft degrees, none has been produced.

**Scholarship to work from:** Scholem (foundational, and now revised on several
points); Idel (against Scholem on the primacy of the theosophical strand); Joseph
Dan; Arthur Green; Peter Schäfer (Hekhalot literature; and a sharp critic of loose
usage); Hanegraaff and Faivre on the occult reception; Julian Strube on Lévi's
context. **No scholar is treated as infallible (§XXXIV); Scholem's synthesis in
particular is the field's starting point and not its settled state.**
