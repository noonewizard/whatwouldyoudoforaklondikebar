# MAGICAL LANGUAGE — RESEARCH MAP

Per §§VIII, XXII. **Why would knowing a name be imagined to confer power?**
Analysed historically and psychologically. Supernatural efficacy is neither
asserted nor denied (§XXII, and the series' standing Type E rule).

---

## THE MATERIAL

**Voces magicae.** Strings in the Greek Magical Papyri (2nd c. BCE–5th c. CE) that
are not words in any language — *ablanathanalba*, *ablanathanalba*, the
*Ephesia grammata*. They are not corrupted Hebrew or Egyptian, though both have
been proposed. **Their unintelligibility is the point**: a name no one can
translate is a name no one can be shown to have got wrong.

**Divine and angelic names.** Names ending in *-el* and *-iah* proliferate in
Jewish and Christian magical material; Hebrew divine names appear throughout the
Greek papyri, in the mouths of practitioners who were not Jewish. **This is a
documented case of sacred-name borrowing across traditions** — and it is instructive
that we can see it happening, with texts, dates and transmission, which is exactly
what the Masonic claims lack.

**Amulets and binding texts.** Skemer's *Binding Words* and Bohak's *Ancient Jewish
Magic* establish the material practice: written words worn, buried, swallowed. The
word as object.

**Grimoires.** Davies, *Grimoires*; Kieckhefer, *Forbidden Rites*. Named spirits,
conjurations, seals. Instructions with stated procedures and claimed results — the
feature Craft ritual entirely lacks.

**Magical alphabets.** Celestial, Malachim, Passing the River (Agrippa, 1533);
Theban (Trithemius, attributed). Adopted by the Golden Dawn from 1888. **Absent
from Craft ritual in every period.**

## ENOCHIAN — THE CONTROL CASE

The most important item in this file, and the reason Part VI ends where it does.

Between 1582 and 1589, John Dee and Edward Kelley produced a complete language:
alphabet, lexicon, grammar, a corpus of "Angelical" text, and a set of tables.
Dee kept diaries. The manuscripts survive. The sessions are dated. The method —
Kelley scrying, Dee transcribing, letters called out from tables — is documented in
detail.

**Enochian is a sacred language whose birth we can watch in full.** And it was not
recovered from antiquity, not transmitted from a prior tradition, and not
deciphered. It was *produced*, in recorded time, by two named men.

**The argument this licenses**, stated carefully: it does not prove that every
sacred language was produced rather than received. It does establish that
production is a real and documented mechanism, that it can generate material of
considerable internal complexity, and that **the one case where we have complete
evidence is a case of making.** Anyone claiming recovery elsewhere should be asked
why the best-documented instance runs the other way.

Scholarship: Asprem, *Arguing with Angels* (2012); Harkness, *John Dee's
Conversations with Angels* (1999); Clulee, *John Dee's Natural Philosophy* (1988).

## WHY NAMES? — THE ANALYSIS

Four explanations, of differing evidential status. They are not exclusive.

**1. Names are how address works.** To act on something you must specify it. A
system in which the cosmos is populated by agents requires a way of singling one
out, and the name is it. **E2** — this is a claim about the internal logic of the
systems, and it is well supported by their own texts.

**2. Naming is the paradigm case of speech that does something.** Genesis 2:19;
Austin's performatives. Naming a child, a ship, a defendant — these are acts, not
descriptions. A tradition reasoning from the cases where words demonstrably change
the world will generalise. **E3.**

**3. Restricted names mark restricted access.** A name only some may speak encodes
a social structure in a linguistic fact. This is sociological and it is strong:
name-secrecy tracks priesthood, initiation and hierarchy with considerable
regularity. **E2–E3.**

**4. Unintelligible names are unfalsifiable.** *Voces magicae* cannot be checked
against a dictionary and therefore cannot be got wrong. A practitioner's authority
is safe. **E3** — analytically compelling, and the practitioners did not say this
about themselves, so it is an outside reading.

**Type E — whether names have real supernatural efficacy — is not addressed.** It is
outside what historical method reaches. The book reports what practitioners
believed and declines the further question explicitly, as both previous volumes did.

## THE MASONIC COMPARISON

The cleanest single distinction in Book III, and it belongs in Chapter 27:

**A magical word is uttered to accomplish something. A Masonic word is exchanged to
establish who you are.**

The magical word is directed outward, at a spirit, a substance or a state of
affairs, and it is expected to produce an effect. The Masonic word is directed at a
doorkeeper, and it is expected to produce admission. One is an operation; the other
is an authentication.

Craft ritual contains **no invocation, no named spirits, no correspondences, no
claimed results and no operator** — which is what Book II established from the other
direction, and which the magical-language material confirms from this one. Where
Masonic-adjacent bodies *did* practise theurgy, as Pasqually's Élus Coëns
demonstrably did from the 1760s, they did so visibly: named founders, dated
documents, explicit instructions. **Operative practice leaves operative texts.** Its
absence from the Craft degrees is not an absence of evidence; it is evidence of the
absence of that kind of material.
