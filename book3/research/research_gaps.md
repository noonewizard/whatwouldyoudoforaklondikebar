# RESEARCH GAPS — BOOK III

Stated in advance, per §XLIV and the series' practice. These are the points at
which the book's arguments are thinnest and where a reader should press hardest.

## TIER 1 — MUST BE DISCHARGED BEFORE PUBLICATION

**1. "Letter and halve it" at document level.** The earliest printed occurrence,
exact wording, edition and page are not established. The whole of §XI depends on
it, and the reading offered (delivery convention, not cipher) is E3 pending that
check. Corpus listed in `words/letter_and_halve_it.md` §4.

**2. Documented pigpen artefacts.** That Masons used the grid cipher is not in
doubt; the book must nonetheless cite objects — specific gravestones,
certificates or minute books, with dates and holding institutions — rather than
repeating the general claim. Currently **[SVR]**.

**3. Prescott and Sommers on the 1717/1721 dating.** The *AQC* printing was not
accessible to this study, as in both previous volumes. Direct consultation
required, or the citation must continue to carry its marker.

**4. Earliest Masonic attestation of the Lost Word.** The 1720s dating is secure at
the level of the third degree's emergence. The precise first appearance of the
*substitution* formula, as distinct from the Hiramic drama, needs pinning to a
named text and date.

## TIER 2 — WOULD MATERIALLY STRENGTHEN THE BOOK

**5. The Royal Arch transmission question.** Book II left it open; Book III's
Claim B (`research/kabbalah.md`) leaves it open again at E4. What is needed is
archival work on the degree's eighteenth-century compilers and their reading —
specifically, any evidence connecting any of them to Christian-Hebraist literature.
**This is the single most valuable piece of research anyone could do on this
subject, and this study is not equipped to do it.**

**6. A Biblical Hebrew lexicon in machine-readable form.** The Atbash control in
`atbash_controls3.py` used a hand-built target list, which is why the
contamination was detectable. A proper run against an independent lexicon (BDB or
HALOT in digital form) would let the test be specified fully in advance, as the
protocol demands of everyone else. **The book should not ask others to do what it
has not done.**

**7. Systematic sampling of the online decoding corpus.** Chapter 43 requires
archived URLs with capture dates, not recollection. Claims change and pages vanish.

## TIER 3 — ACKNOWLEDGED LIMITS, NOT FIXABLE

**8. No access to unpublished ritual.** Nor is any sought (§XXXVII). The book works
from published exposures and printed ritual editions. Where practice is
jurisdictionally variable, the book says so rather than resolving it.

**9. Hebrew pronunciation before 70 CE.** Unrecoverable. Any claim about how the
Tetragrammaton *sounded* is inference from transcriptions in other alphabets and
from later vocalisation, and ch 12 must say so plainly rather than adjudicating.

**10. *Sefer Yetzirah*'s date.** Genuinely unsettled across seven centuries
(2nd–9th). The book states the range and the stakes; it does not pick.

**11. The author's Hebrew.** Stated plainly for the reader's benefit: the
transformations in this book are machine-computed and checked against standard
published figures, and the philological judgements rest on the scholarship cited,
not on independent competence in the sources' languages. Where a reading depends
on Hebrew philology, the book names the scholar it is following.
