# APPENDIX B
## Biblical and Early Text Source Map

What each source gives, when it was composed relative to what it describes, and
what it is usable for.

| Source | Describes | Composition | Usable for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Kings 5–8** | First Temple: plan, materials, furnishings, dedication | Deuteronomistic History; composition debated, substantially later than the tenth century | The fullest account of the first Temple; primary for what the tradition held, not for the building |
| **2 Chronicles 2–7** | First Temple, parallel account | Post-exilic, later than Kings | A second tradition; its differences from Kings are evidence about transmission |
| **2 Kings 25** | The destruction, with some measurements | Deuteronomistic | The 586 destruction; a third and divergent pillar measurement |
| **Jeremiah 52** | The destruction, with measurements | Exilic/post-exilic | A fourth pillar measurement, agreeing with 1 Kings |
| **Ezekiel 40–43** | A *visionary* temple, in great dimensional detail | Exilic | Level 3. **Not a description of the first Temple**, though Villalpando treated it as one (ch 67) |
| **Mishnah, tractate Middot** | The *second* Temple in Herodian form | c. 200 CE, ~130 years after the destruction | The best structural description of the second Temple; the memory-preservation case of ch 85 |
| **Mishnah, order Kodashim** | The sacrificial service | c. 200 CE | The service as specification; not performable when written |
| **Josephus, *Antiquities* and *War*** | Both Temples; the second from autopsy | Late first century CE | Eyewitness for the Herodian Temple; dependent on scripture for the first |
| **Philo** | Cosmological reading of the sanctuary | First century CE | Level 3, first century |
| **Testament of Solomon** | Demons compelled to build the Temple | Greek MSS; present form usually 3rd–5th c. CE | Level 3 legend; not evidence about construction (ch 29) |

### The rule of direction

A source is primary for its own period only. Middot is a first-rate source for what
Jews around 200 CE believed and preserved about the second Temple, and not a
source for the tenth century BCE. Villalpando is a first-rate source for 1600 and
not for Solomon. Anderson is a first-rate source for 1723.

This rule is applied without exception throughout the book, including where it is
inconvenient.
