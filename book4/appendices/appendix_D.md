# APPENDIX D
## The Evidence Hierarchies

Two scales are used throughout this series. They are not the same scale and they
answer different questions.

### Source quality: Levels A–F

*How good is this source?*

| Level | Description | Examples in this book |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Contemporary documentary evidence, primary, datable | Lodge minutes; the Schaw Statutes (1598–99); the Grand Lodge MS No. 1 (1583) |
| **B** | Near-contemporary primary sources; archaeological reports | Josephus on the Herodian Temple; excavation reports from 'Ain Dara and Tel Motza |
| **C** | Later primary sources describing earlier periods | Mishnah Middot (c. 200 CE) on the second Temple; the Babylonian Talmud |
| **D** | Peer-reviewed secondary scholarship | Standard archaeological, Masonic, and religious-studies literature |
| **E** | Non-peer-reviewed but serious secondary work | Older Masonic historiography; popular scholarly synthesis |
| **F** | Polemic, hearsay, anonymous, promotional, or self-published | Barruel; the Taxil material; internet claim literature |

Level F material is analyzed in this book **as cultural evidence** — evidence of
what people believe and circulate — and is never treated as authoritative about
the events it describes. Chapters 77 and 78 do exactly this.

### Claim strength: E1–E6

*How well supported is this particular claim?*

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **E1** | Documented, uncontested, multiply attested |
| **E2** | Well supported; minor scholarly dispute |
| **E3** | Supported but with real gaps or genuine dispute |
| **E4** | Plausible; insufficiently evidenced or untested |
| **E5** | A later interpretation projected onto earlier material |
| **E6** | Unsupported; fails multiple tests |

### Two clarifications used constantly

**E6 is not "disproved."** It means the claim has no evidential support and
multiple structural obstacles. Chapter 29 states this explicitly for the
magical-construction claim.

**A good source can carry a bad claim, and vice versa.** Anderson's
*Constitutions* is a Level A source for 1723 (what Londoners then wished to say)
and supports an E6 claim about 950 BCE. Keeping the two scales apart is what makes
that statement possible.
