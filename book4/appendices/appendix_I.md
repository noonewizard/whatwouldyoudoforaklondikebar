# APPENDIX I
## The Old Charges on Solomon

Summarized from Chapter 32. Something over a hundred versions survive, from the
early fifteenth century to the eighteenth.

### Structure of a typical Old Charge

1. **Invocation** — in the great majority of pre-1723 copies, explicitly
   Trinitarian.
2. **Legendary history** — geometry and masonry from antiquity to England.
3. **The charges** — practical and moral obligations of the trade.

### The legendary history's spine

Children of Lamech → the two pillars against flood and fire → Nimrod → Abraham →
**Euclid in Egypt** → the craft travels → **Solomon's Temple** → … → England
under Athelstan.

**Solomon's Temple is a station on a route, not the route's origin.** The craft
arrives there already possessing its charges and its geometry.

### The master mason's name

Across the corpus the figure sent by the King of Tyre appears under a remarkable
spread of names — transmitted variously as Aynon, Aynone, Dyon, Ajuon, Benaim, and
others, with copyists working from exemplars they could not always read. The
variation was catalogd by Knoop, Jones and their contemporaries.

He is not called Hiram Abiff.

**By the disagreement principle (ch 8): sources agreeing on a role while disagreeing
wildly on a name transmitted the role, not the name.**

### What is absent from the Old Charges

- No death of the master mason; no murder, search, or recovery
- No lost word
- No interpretation of the Temple's dimensions
- No doctrine of the pillars
- No Kabbalah, magic, or sacred geometry in the modern sense
- Geometry is Euclid's: a practical and dignified liberal art

Everything in the later structure that is not in this list has to be accounted for
by what happened after these documents — which is the subject of Chapters 33 and 34.
