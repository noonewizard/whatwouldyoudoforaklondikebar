# APPENDIX J
## The Eight-Question Kabbalah Protocol

Established in Book III and applied in this book to Temple material. Any proposed
Masonic–Kabbalistic link is run through all eight.

1. When does the connection first appear?
2. Who made it?
3. What source documents it?
4. Was that person actually a Mason?
5. Was the system available to be borrowed?
6. Is there evidence of actual transmission into the ritual?
7. Is the interpretation modern?
8. Is it accepted, disputed, or speculative?

### Worked: Jachin and Boaz as the pillars of Severity and Mercy (ch 38)

| Q | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | The nineteenth century |
| 2 | Lévi (1854–56), then Pike (1871), then the Golden Dawn (1888–) |
| 3 | Lévi's works; *Morals and Dogma*; Golden Dawn papers. **No eighteenth-century Masonic source** |
| 4 | Pike yes, in one jurisdiction of one rite. Lévi no. The Golden Dawn figures included Masons making the claim inside a non-Masonic body |
| 5 | Yes — *Kabbala Denudata* in print from 1677; Christian Kabbalah current since Pico |
| 6 | **None.** This is the decisive answer |
| 7 | Yes |
| 8 | Speculative, and recognized as such by documentary Masonic scholarship |

**Verdict: E5** — a nineteenth-century interpretive overlay on an
eighteenth-century biblical borrowing.

### The simpler explanation, which fits everything

The two pillars are in 1 Kings 7:21. Men who founded lodges read the Bible. A
fraternity organized around the Temple, looking for architectural features to use,
finds two named pillars at the entrance and uses them.

No transmission is required, because no transmission is needed.

### The standing distinction

*Kabbalah* (Jewish) / *Cabala* (Christian appropriation) / *Qabalah* (modern
occult). Three bodies of material with different dates, purposes, and
relationships. Most of what popular writing calls Kabbalistic symbolism in
Freemasonry belongs to the third, and the third was assembled after 1854.
