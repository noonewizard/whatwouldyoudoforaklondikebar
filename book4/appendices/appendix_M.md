# APPENDIX M
## The Golden Ratio Control Run, in Full

Reported in Chapter 73. Method fixed before any number was examined.

**Procedure.** Every ordered pairwise ratio of a dimension list. A hit is a ratio
within **5%** of φ (1.618034), i.e. the band 1.538–1.699. Tolerance and procedure
fixed in advance. Eleven values give 110 ordered pairs.

**Target.** The Temple's stated dimensions: 60, 20, 30, 20, 10, 20, 20, 20, 5, 6, 7.

### Result — target

**1 hit in 110 pairs.** 10/6 = 1.6667, error 3.01%.

### Post-hoc diagnostic, flagged as post-hoc

The Temple's 11 values collapse to **7 distinct values** (20 occurs four times),
and its 110 ordered pairs to only **35 distinct ratios**, dominated by clean small
integers — 3, 2, 1.5, 0.5, 6 — which sit far from 1.618.

A random set of the same size has more distinct values, more distinct ratios, and
more chances to approach φ.

**The Temple's dimensions are not merely non-golden. They are the kind of numbers
that make the golden ratio hard to hit** — whole-number, rope-and-peg proportions,
which is the opposite of what a design around an irrational ratio would produce.

### What would have changed the verdict

A text from the period stating a proportional intention; the ratio appearing across
many independent buildings of the same tradition above the control rate; φ
approximations systematically tighter than chance allows; or a single match precise
to a fraction of a per cent.

None exists. What exists is one hit in a hundred and ten, beaten by the numbers one
through eleven.
