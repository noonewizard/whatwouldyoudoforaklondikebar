### Result

**0 hits out of 18** against the 62-word independent lexicon.

### What zero means

Not a refutation of Atbash claims and not a finding requiring explanation. **It is
the base rate.** At this lexicon size and word-length distribution, the expected
number of chance hits is close to zero, and an observed zero is what the null
predicts.

Its value is comparative: it fixes the baseline against which any positive claim
must be measured. The questions to ask of any reported Atbash hit are how many
terms were tested, against how large a lexicon, and whether the lexicon was fixed
before the search.

### Why Atbash is a fair test

It passes plausibility — attested within the Hebrew Bible, where Jeremiah appears
to render Babel as Sheshach. And it passes the encoding-rule test: one rule,
unambiguous, reproducible by anyone. That is rare in this field and is why Atbash
claims deserve testing rather than dismissal.

### The near-miss

Shlomo = **375**. Shalom = **376**. Adjacent integers, thematically connected
words, in a book about a temple built in a reign of peace.

Not a coincidence and not a discovery. The words share the root *sh-l-m*; Solomon's
name is formed from it; they differ in consonantal spelling by one letter. **The
numerical relation is a consequence of the etymological relation**, which philology
already knew and states more precisely.

This is the commonest form of gematria "result" in the popular literature:
converting a known etymology into a number and presenting the number as news.
