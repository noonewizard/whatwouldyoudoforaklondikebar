# APPENDIX O
## Conspiracy Claim Audit — Summary

From Chapter 77. Each claim is given with its actual source, the real fact
underneath it, and its rating.

| Claim | Source | The real fact underneath | Rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freemasons control governments | Barruel (1797–98), Robison (1797) — contemporaneous political polemics | Some Masons have held office, as members of any large voluntary association eventually will | **E6** |
| Freemasons seek to rebuild the Temple | Conflation of genuine, non-Masonic Third Temple movements with Masonic ritual's symbolic building language | Masonic ritual uses Temple-construction imagery, declared symbolic within the tradition itself | **E6** |

### The shared structure

**Each begins with a real, mundane, documented fact.** A fraternal organization
exists; it uses Temple symbolism; its officers hold titles; some members have held
office; its ceremonies are private. Every one of these is true.

**Each substitutes a stronger claim for the fact**, by a small number of moves: a
symbol read as a plan; a title read as a different kind of office; a correlation
read as a mechanism; a closed door read as a container.

**Each is transmitted with the true fact still attached**, which is what makes
these claims durable. A listener who checks finds the premise confirmed and takes
that as confirmation of the conclusion.

### A necessary distinction

Religious objections to Freemasonry are not all of this kind. There are serious
theological objections — concerning oaths, religious indifferentism, and whether a
non-denominational ceremonial system competes with a church's claims — which
deserve engagement on their merits. Lumping them together with a nineteenth-century
hoax does them a disservice (ch 77).
