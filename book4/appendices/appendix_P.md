# APPENDIX P
## Jurisdictional Variation — A Standing Caution

**There has never been a single body called Freemasonry with a single ritual.**
Any sentence beginning "In Freemasonry, X" without a jurisdiction and a date is
malformed.

### Where variation matters most in this book

**The Three Great Lights** (ch 39). The term has not always denoted the same three
things; in earlier and some current usage, *lights* meant illumination and the
Bible, square and compasses were the lodge's *furniture*. The modern English
arrangement is substantially a post-1813 standardization.

**The Volume of the Sacred Law** (ch 39). Some jurisdictions require a Bible
specifically; some permit the candidate's own scripture; one major body requires
none. This single object is the fault line of a schism.

**Officers** (ch 40). English lodges have an Inner Guard; most American lodges do
not. Scottish and Irish arrangements differ again; Continental rites differ more.

**The Royal Arch** (ch 33). The Antients incorporated it; the Moderns accommodated
it through a separate chapter organization. The 1813 Articles of Union declared
pure Antient Masonry to consist of three degrees including the Royal Arch — a
formula that settled a jurisdictional dispute rather than a historical question.

**Admission** (ch 82). Jurisdictions have differed, sometimes bitterly, over race
and over the admission of women. The disagreements are real, are not resolved, and
are not the same everywhere.

### A note on private ritual material

This book does not reproduce private Masonic ritual material. Where it discusses
ritual structure, it does so from published sources: the exposures from 1730
onward, printed constitutions, and the scholarly literature. Exposures are hostile
and commercial sources and are used with the caution Chapter 33 sets out.
