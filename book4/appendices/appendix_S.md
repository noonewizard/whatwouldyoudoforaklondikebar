# APPENDIX S
## What Would Change These Findings

A finding that nothing could overturn is not a finding. This appendix states, for
each of the book's principal negative conclusions, the evidence that would change
it.

### On the Temple's construction and design

**"The Temple's dimensions encode no hidden ratio."**
Would change with: a text from the relevant period stating a proportional
intention; the ratio appearing across many independent buildings of the tradition
at a rate above the control rate; φ approximations systematically tighter than
chance allows; or a single match precise to a fraction of a per cent.

**"The magical-construction claim is unsupported."**
Would change with: any source contemporary with or close to the construction
connecting it to magical means, or a demonstrated transmission channel from the
period to the *Testament of Solomon*.

### On Masonic origins

**"Freemasonry did not inherit Temple knowledge."**
Would change with: a craft document earlier than the eighteenth century containing
material demonstrably derived from Temple sources beyond the shared biblical text —
a dimension, a doctrine, or a name that could only have come from a non-biblical
Temple tradition.

**"The Kabbalistic reading of the pillars is post-1854."**
Would change with: an eighteenth-century Masonic source stating it. One document
would do.

**"The Hiramic legend does not descend from an operative tradition preserving the
name."**
Would change with: a pre-eighteenth-century craft document giving the master mason
a stable name matching later usage, or a systematic collation showing the Old
Charges' name variation to be narrower than Chapter 32 reports.

### On the psychological claims

**"Initiatory institutions' moral effects are untested (E4)."**
Would change with: a study using baseline measures, a comparison group of people
who wanted to join and did not, long follow-up, and defensible outcome measures.
This is difficult but not impossible, and nobody has done it.

**"Architecture does not induce specific inner states."**
Would change with: well-powered, pre-registered, directly replicated experiments
showing that specific architectural properties produce specific cognitive effects.
The awe literature is the most likely place for this to happen.

### On the conspiracy claims

**All six E6 ratings in Appendix O.**
Would change with: primary documentation. A minute, a directive, a fund, a policy,
a correspondence. The absence of such documentation after two centuries of
assertion is the finding; a single authenticated document would be a different
finding.

### On this book's method

**The controls.** If a reader runs the published scripts with different but
defensible parameters and gets materially different results, the conclusions drawn
from them change. The scripts are short and the parameters are visible, which is
the point of publishing them.

### What would not change these findings

Repetition. Confidence. The age of a claim. The prominence of the person making it.
The fact that a great many people believe it. The absence of evidence being
interpreted as evidence of suppression — which Chapter 78 identified as the
mechanism by which a framework becomes unfalsifiable, and which this book does not
accept from anyone, including itself.
