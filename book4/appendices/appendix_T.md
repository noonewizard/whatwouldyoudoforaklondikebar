# APPENDIX T
## Glossary

**Ashlar.** A squared building stone. A technical term of the trade before it was a
symbol. The rough ashlar is undressed, the perfect ashlar dressed and true (chs
43–44).

**Atbash.** A Hebrew letter substitution exchanging the first letter for the last,
the second for the second-to-last, and so on. Attested within the Hebrew Bible
(ch 74).

**Availability is not transmission.** That a text or idea existed and could in
principle have been read establishes possibility, not influence. The recurring
distinction in Parts XI–XII.

**Concinnitas.** Alberti's term for the condition in which nothing can be added,
taken away, or altered without harm (ch 56).

**Disagreement principle.** When sources agree on a role but disagree widely on the
details attached to it, the role was transmitted and the details were not (chs 32,
40). Carried from the preceding volumes.

**Emplacement.** Jonathan Z. Smith's thesis that ritual works substantially by
declaring where it is, making ordinary actions significant by location (chs 34,
36).

**Event segmentation.** The parsing of continuous experience into discrete events,
with boundaries making prior information less accessible (ch 48).

**Forking paths.** Andrew Gelman's term for the undeclared analytic freedom that
gives a single analysis the error rate of many (ch 72).

**Half-of-everything rule.** A feature with very low cardinality — a shape anyone
can draw, a direction with two options — carries almost no information about
descent (chs 5, 26, 36, 39). Carried from the preceding volumes.

**Hekhalot / Merkavah.** Late-antique Jewish visionary literature of ascent through
heavenly palaces to the divine throne-chariot (ch 52).

**Old Charges.** The manuscript tradition of English craft documents, c. 1400–1700s,
containing an invocation, a legendary history, and the charges of the trade
(ch 32, App. I).

**Parallel is not descent.** Two traditions sharing a feature is not evidence that
either learned it from the other. The book's most frequently applied rule.

**Precommitment.** Fixing the target, the rule, and the tolerance before the search.
The single most important methodological practice in this subject (chs 72, 73).

**Retrofitting.** Discovering a held system inside an object that does not contain
it, by exercising undeclared analytic freedom. Requires no bad faith (ch 72).

**Rule of direction.** A source is primary for its own period only (App. B).

**Sefirot.** The ten emanations of Kabbalistic cosmology, arranged in the structure
known as the Tree of Life.

**Shamir.** In rabbinic narrative, the agent capable of cutting stone without iron,
obtained by Solomon from the demon Ashmedai — a story that exists to explain 1
Kings 6:7 (ch 28).

**Status Quo.** The arrangement governing religious sites in Jerusalem, deriving
from Ottoman decrees of the 1850s and carried forward, with modifications, by
successive authorities (ch 63).

**Symbolic universe.** A frame in which other symbols are located, rather than a
symbol among others. Recognizable by its capacity to absorb new material without
being altered (ch 34).

**Tracing board.** A painted board carrying the emblems of a degree, set up in the
lodge. A development of the earlier practice of drawing the lodge on the floor and
erasing it (ch 35).

**Zecher l'churban.** The custom of leaving a portion of a house unfinished in
remembrance of the Temple's destruction (ch 85).
