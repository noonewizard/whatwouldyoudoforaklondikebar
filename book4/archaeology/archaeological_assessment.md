# ARCHAEOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT — DELIVERABLE 5

What is known, disputed, and unknown, structured per the master prompt's
Archaeological Standard: textual source / archaeological evidence / comparative

---

## THE CENTRAL FACT GOVERNING THIS ENTIRE FILE

**No archaeological excavation has ever been conducted, or under current
conditions can be conducted, on the Temple Mount platform itself**, where the
First and Second Temples are believed to have stood. This is not a gap that more
funding or better technology would close; it is a structural condition of the site
(see historiography.md §2). Every claim below about "the Temple" archaeologically
is therefore either (a) about the surrounding City of David / Jerusalem more
broadly, (b) about comparative Iron Age and Bronze Age temples elsewhere in the
Levant, or (c) inference from later, non-contemporary sources (Josephus, the
Mishnah). **None of it is a direct excavation of Solomon's Temple**, because that
excavation has never happened.

---

## WHAT IS KNOWN (E1–E2)

**Jerusalem was inhabited and fortified in the Bronze and Iron Ages.** The
Gihon Spring fortifications, the Stepped Stone Structure, and other City of David
finds establish continuous occupation and significant, if contested-in-scale,
building activity in the relevant periods.

**A monumental structure existed on the Temple Mount by the Herodian period at the
latest.** The Herodian platform, retaining walls (including the Western/Wailing
Wall), and associated structures are extensively documented archaeologically and
by Josephus, who describes the Herodian reconstruction in detail (*Antiquities*
XV; *Jewish War* V).

**The Babylonian destruction of 586 BCE is externally corroborated.** The
Babylonian Chronicles and the archaeological destruction layer across Judah
(including Jerusalem) at this horizon are independent of the biblical text and
confirm a real, dated catastrophe consistent with (though not proof of the
specific details of) 2 Kings 25.

**Comparative Iron Age temples exist and are excavated.** 'Ain Dara in Syria (a
temple with striking ground-plan similarities to the biblical description — tripartite
division, comparable proportions) is the single most frequently cited comparandum
in current scholarship (see ch.5, Appendix E). Tel Motza, near Jerusalem, has
produced a 9th–8th century BCE Judahite temple structure, the closest EXCAVATED
temple to Jerusalem itself, though smaller and not identical in plan.

## WHAT IS DISPUTED (E4)

**Whether a 10th-century BCE Jerusalem could have supported the building project
1 Kings describes** (per the minimalist/maximalist debate).

**The Low Chronology question** — whether strata conventionally dated to the 10th
century (traditionally "Solomonic") should be redated a century later, which would
remove much of the material record sometimes associated with a United Monarchy
building program.

**Whether any specific structure outside Jerusalem (e.g., at Megiddo, once
claimed as "Solomon's stables") is correctly attributed to Solomon's reign at
all** — the "Solomonic gates" at Megiddo, Hazor, and Gezer, once a cornerstone of
the maximalist case, are now dated by some scholars to the Omride period
(9th century), a full century later.

## WHAT IS UNKNOWN, AND LIKELY TO REMAIN SO

**The Temple's actual physical form, materials, and construction sequence**,
beyond what the biblical text states — because the site cannot be excavated.

**Whether the biblical measurements (1 Kings 6–7) reflect an actual building's
dimensions, a idealized/theological figure, or some mixture** — there is no
independent check.

**The length of the "cubit" used**, which is itself disputed among at least two
candidate values (the "common" cubit vs. the "long"/"royal" cubit, differing by
roughly one handbreadth), meaning every physical-scale claim about the Temple's
size in meters carries an unavoidable uncertainty band before any other question
is asked (see Appendix D).

## THE STANDING RULE FOR THIS BOOK

**Every Temple "reconstruction" — Villalpando's, any modern museum model, any
digital rendering — is an artist's interpretation constrained by, but not
identical to, the textual evidence, and none of them is a report of an excavated
building.** The book states this at the head of every reconstruction discussed
(ch.3, ch.66–68) rather than once and then forgetting it, because forgetting it is
precisely how "the Temple looked like X" claims acquire false authority.
