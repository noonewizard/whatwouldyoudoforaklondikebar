# FINAL AUDITS — THE TEMPLE OF SOLOMON

Six audits, run against the assembled manuscript after drafting was complete.
Each states what was checked, how, what was found, and what was changed. Failures
are recorded, not removed.

Manuscript at audit: **98,668 words**, 90 chapters in 18 parts, prologue,
epilogue, 20 appendices, illustration list, bibliography, index framework.

---

## AUDIT 1 — HISTORICAL ACCURACY

**Checked.** Every dated claim against the chronology in Appendix C; every
attribution of a work to an author and year; the sequence of institutional
foundations; the relationship between events and the documents attesting them.

**Method.** Chapter-by-chapter reading against `research/chronology.md` and
Appendix C, plus a programmatic sweep of all "Chapter N" cross-references against
chapter titles.

**Findings.**

*Passed.* All institutional dates are given with their evidentiary status attached
where that status is weak: the Premier Grand Lodge's 1717 is flagged in Chapters 30
and 33 and in Appendix P as resting on Anderson's 1738 account, with 1721 named as
the better-attested alternative. The Regius/Cooke dating is given as a range with
the dispute noted. The *Testament of Solomon*'s dating is given as a range in
Chapters 25, 28 and 29.

*Failed and fixed — cross-reference errors.* Ten references to methodological
rules cited the wrong chapter. "The half-of-everything rule of Chapter 9" and "the
disagreement principle from Chapter 8" appeared in Chapters 32, 36, 39, 40, 41, 53,
54 and 55; Chapter 9 of this book is "Cherubim" and Chapter 8 is "The Ark." Neither
rule is established in this volume — both are carried from the preceding ones,
and the half-of-everything rule is restated in Chapter 5. All ten were corrected to
point at Chapter 5, Chapter 15, Chapter 72, or to an explicit attribution to the
preceding volumes. Appendix T and the index framework were corrected to match.

*Failed and fixed — a reference to a chapter that does not cover the subject.*
Chapter 32 referred the reader to "Chapter 41" for the Hiramic legend; Chapter 41
is "The Body as Temple." Hiram Abiff is Chapter 22. Corrected.

**Residual risk.** Appendix R lists thirteen points where a source could not be
verified to this book's standard. None is load-bearing for any finding in Appendix
E.

## AUDIT 2 — SOURCE INTEGRITY

**Checked.** That no quotation, citation, page number, archival reference,
manuscript, organization, date, or scholarly consensus was fabricated.

**Method.** Every named source in the manuscript was checked against the
bibliography and against the constraint that nothing unverifiable is asserted
without a flag.

**Findings.**

*Passed — no page numbers are given for any source.* This is a deliberate policy
carried from Book III: the standing instruction is never to fabricate a page
number, and rather than guess, this book cites works by title, author, and date
only. A reader is never given a false precision.

*Passed — no invented documents, manuscripts, organizations, or rituals.* All
manuscripts named (Regius, Cooke, Grand Lodge MS No. 1, Edinburgh Register House,
Royal MS 17 A I, Additional MS 23198) are real and standard. All organizations
named exist or existed. No ritual text is reproduced.

*Passed — consensus language.* A sweep for "scholars generally agree," "scholars
agree," "the evidence proves," and "it is well known that" returned one hit:
Chapter 89's "the modern instance everyone knows, the Sagrada Família," which is
idiomatic and not a claim about scholarship. Where scholarly positions are named,
the scholar is named: Schäfer qualifying Scholem (ch 52), Elior's thesis marked as
debated (ch 52), Hurowitz on building accounts (ch 61), Wittkower on Renaissance
proportion (ch 56), Knoop and Jones on the Old Charges (ch 32), Gelman on forking
paths (ch 72), Granqvist's failed replication (ch 51).

*Flagged, not fixed.* Thirteen source-verification flags remain and are collected
in Appendix R with a statement of what would close each. Two of them (entries 6 and
7) are flags on psychology findings this book **declines to rely on**; one (entry
11) is a flag on an assertion this book attributes to others and declines to
accept.

## AUDIT 3 — ANACHRONISM

**Checked.** That no modern concept is projected onto a period that did not have
it, and that every analytical vocabulary borrowed from a later era is declared.

**Findings.**

*Passed — Part XVII is declared throughout.* The information-systems vocabulary of
Chapters 80–83 opens with an explicit statement that it is an analytical analogy,
that nobody in the ancient world thought in these terms, and that finding
computational structure in the Temple says something about the vocabulary and not
about the builders. The caution is restated in each of the four chapters, and
Chapter 83 explicitly puts the lens down.

*Passed — the magical Solomon is kept in its period.* Chapter 29's anachronism
test finds that the demon-commanding framework belongs to the religious world of
late antiquity and that projecting it onto the tenth century BCE imports a
cosmology backwards. This is stated as one of six grounds for the E6 verdict.

*Passed — "sacred geometry" is disambiguated by period.* Chapter 69 separates the
eighteenth-century sense (geometry as the science of order, Euclid elevated to a
liberal art) from the modern sense (hidden ratios encoding spiritual truths, φ at
the centre), and dates the second to the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.
Chapter 56 dates the φ doctrine to Zeising and Ghyka.

*Passed — the Kabbalah chain is dated.* Chapters 52–54 and Appendix J keep Jewish
Kabbalah, Christian Cabala, and Hermetic Qabalah separate and give each a date.

*Passed — Temple furnishings are not anachronized.* Chapter 39 notes that a
scriptural volume in a ritual setting is an anachronism for the First Temple
period.

**Residual risk.** Part IX's moral-formation vocabulary draws on virtue ethics and
modern habit research together. Chapter 45 separates the ancient claim from the
modern correction and reports that the modern literature's situational findings cut
*against* the architectural model of character.

## AUDIT 4 — CONSPIRACY CONTROL

**Checked.** That conspiracy claims are audited rather than amplified or
dismissed; that Level F material is treated as cultural evidence and never as
authoritative; that the book does not itself construct a conspiracy narrative in
the opposite direction.

**Findings.**

*Passed — six claims audited, each to E6, each with its real fact identified.*
Chapter 77 and Appendix O. In every case the mundane documented fact underneath the
claim is stated as true before the inflated version is rejected.

*Passed — the Taxil forgery handled completely.* Chapter 60 identifies it, Chapter
78 traces it through five stages to the public confession of 19 April 1897 and
explains why the retraction failed. The genuine *Morals and Dogma* passage using
*Lucifer* in its Latin sense is distinguished from the fabrication.

*Passed — no reverse conspiracy.* The book does not claim that Freemasonry has
concealed its own history. Chapter 31 records the opposite: the authentic school
of Masonic scholarship dismantled the traditional history from the 1880s onward.
Anderson's history is rated E6 in the same table as the conspiracy claims.

*Passed — theological objections distinguished from fabrication.* Chapter 77 and
Appendix O explicitly note that serious religious objections to Freemasonry exist,
concern oaths and religious indifferentism, deserve engagement on their merits, and
are done a disservice by being lumped with a hoax.

*Passed — the Temple Mount handled without advocacy.* Chapter 63 opens with an
explicit statement that it takes no position on sovereignty, access, or political
status, and does not arrive at one. Terminology is given in all three conventional
forms with a note that no neutral term exists. Third Temple movements are described
factually and identified as a minority position. The claim that Freemasonry has a
rebuilding programme is refused as a category error, with the cost of the error
named.

## AUDIT 5 — MATHEMATICAL AND CIPHER CONTROL

**Checked.** That every numerical, geometric, and cryptographic claim was
computed rather than asserted; that controls were run; that tolerances were fixed
in advance; that search spaces were reported; that failures were published.

**Method.** All six scripts re-run from a clean state.

**Findings.**

*Passed — everything computed is reproducible.* Six scripts, no dependencies
beyond the Python standard library, all running in seconds. Outputs preserved in
`research/computational_verification/`.

*Passed — controls run on every positive-claim test.* The golden ratio against ten
named comparison sets and a 20,000-run synthetic null; Atbash against a 62-word
lexicon fixed before the search and carried unchanged from Book III; the hexagram
against lattices of four densities and a structureless random cloud.

*Passed — search spaces reported.* One hit in 110 ordered pairs, stated as such
every time it is mentioned. Zero hits in 18 terms against 62 words.

*Reported failure 1 — a declared gap, closed.* The research README declared an open
gap: the golden-ratio test had one illustrative control rather than a distribution.
Closed before Chapter 73 was drafted, by `golden_ratio_controls.py`. The README
records the closure.

*Reported failure 2 — a script crash, published.* `temple_gematria.py` raised a
key error on the space in the Hebrew for Holy of Holies, because two of its three
functions lacked the input filter the third had. Patched, rerun in full, and
reported in the script, in Chapter 74, and in Appendix L — with the observation
that failing loudly rather than silently was luck.

*Reported failure 3 — a prediction that only partly held.* `dc_geometry_controls.py`
was run against a prediction, fixed in advance, that fit quality would improve with
point density. Absolute deviation proved flat across every configuration and the
percentage figure moved non-monotonically. Chapter 79 reports the partial failure
and states the sharper finding that replaced it: the percentage figure this
literature reports has a denominator the analyst's own search selects.

*Reported finding against interest.* The Temple's dimensions scored **below all ten
comparison sets** on the golden-ratio test and were matched or beaten by 95.66% of
random number sets — a result more unfavourable to the claim than expected, whose
explanation (Chapter 73's post-hoc diagnostic) is explicitly flagged as post-hoc.

## AUDIT 6 — NARRATIVE CONTROL

**Checked.** That the book does not become apologetics, an attack, or a
conspiracy narrative; that findings run both ways; that the tone does not
sensationalize; that jurisdictional variation is stated; that private ritual
material is not reproduced.

**Findings.**

*Passed — findings run both ways.* Appendix E's E6 list contains six conspiracy
claims **and** Anderson's traditional history, the golden-ratio claim, the
gematria claim, the Rosicrucian descent claim, and the claim that Newton was a
Freemason. The book rejects an ancient pedigree for Freemasonry and rejects the
accusations against it in the same table.

*Passed — a positive finding was deliberately reported.* Chapter 5 reports a
favourable comparative result ('Ain Dara) against the risk that the method only
ever debunks. Chapter 53 credits Giorgi's San Francesco della Vigna memorandum as a
documented case of esoteric proportional theory applied to a real building.
Chapter 58 credits the Rosicrucian vault as a transmission that passes every test
the Temple claims fail.

*Passed — the book tests its own attractive hypotheses.* Chapter 19 reports that
the strong version of its own hypothesis fails. Chapter 48 spends a third of its
length weakening a finding that would have supported its argument. Chapter 73
reports a result worse for the claim than predicted and flags its explanation as
post-hoc. Chapter 79 reports a failed prediction. Chapter 88 turns the method on
this book.

*Passed — jurisdictional variation stated.* Twenty-two chapters reference
jurisdiction explicitly; Appendix P collects the standing caution with the
principal bodies, the points where variation matters most, and the admission
disputes over race and over women, which are stated as real, unresolved, and not
the same everywhere.

*Passed — no private ritual material reproduced.* Ritual structure is discussed
from published sources only: exposures from 1730 onward, printed constitutions, and
scholarly literature. Chapter 33 states the caution that exposures are hostile and
commercial sources. No ritual text appears.

*Passed — no overall truth score.* The brief forbids assigning a single numerical
verdict, and none is given. Claims are rated individually on E1–E6 and the ratings
are listed per claim in Appendix E.

*Residual observation, not a failure.* The book's register is consistently
sceptical, and a reader could mistake consistency for disposition. Chapter 5's
stated principle — "a method that can only debunk is not a method; it is a
disposition wearing a method's clothes" — is the safeguard, and Appendix S states
for each principal negative finding exactly what evidence would overturn it.
