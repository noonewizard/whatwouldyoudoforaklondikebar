# CHAPTER FOURTEEN
## The Number of the Temple

Every numerical claim requires provenance. This chapter supplies it for the
numbers most often asserted to be significant, and the exercise is worth doing
systematically because the pattern is so consistent.

---

### Ten

**In the Temple**: ubiquitous. Ten cubits for the porch's depth, the sea's
diameter, the cherubim's height and wingspan. Ten lavers, ten lampstands, ten
tables.

**Provenance**: base-ten counting, which follows from having ten fingers. There is
no mystery here and none is needed. A culture counting in tens will produce round
numbers in tens.

**Level**: not arbitrary. Carries nothing.

### Twelve

**In the Temple**: twelve oxen supporting the molten sea.

**Elsewhere**: twelve tribes, twelve months, twelve hours.

**Provenance**: twelve is the most divisible small number — by two, three, four
and six — which makes it enormously convenient for a culture dividing quantities
without decimal notation. And the year contains approximately twelve lunations
(12.37, which is why every lunar calendar requires correction).

**Level**: explained by arithmetic convenience and by the sky. Not arbitrary.

### Twenty-two

**The claim**: twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet, twenty-two paths on the
Kabbalistic Tree of Life, therefore a structural correspondence to the Temple.

**Provenance**: the alphabet has twenty-two letters as an accident of its
development from a Phoenician ancestor. The Tree's twenty-two paths derive from
*Sefer Yetzirah*'s thirty-two (ten *sefirot* plus twenty-two letters), a text
whose date is disputed across seven centuries.

**And twenty-two does not appear as a Temple number.** The correspondence is
constructed by counting something in the Temple until twenty-two is reached, which
Chapter 72 identifies as the retrofitting procedure.

**Level: E6.**

### Forty

**In the Temple**: the main hall is forty cubits.

**Elsewhere**: forty days, forty years, recurring throughout the Hebrew Bible.

**Provenance**: a conventional figure for "a long but bounded period," used
formulaically. Its appearance as a Temple dimension is a consequence of the hall
being twice the width of a twenty-cubit building, not a citation of the
formulaic usage.

**Level**: coincidence of a round number with a conventional one. Not evidence.

### Forty-seven

**The claim**: the forty-seventh proposition of Euclid — the Pythagorean theorem —
is foundational to Masonry, therefore forty-seven is a significant number.

**Provenance**: Anderson's 1723 *Constitutions* refers to it.[^1] And the number is
**an index**: the proposition is forty-seventh because forty-six results had to be
established first in Euclid's ordering. Nobody chose it.

**Level: E6** as a significant number. **A page reference is not a message.** The
preceding volume treated this at length and the finding stands.

### Seventy-two

**The claim**: a number of great traditional weight — the seventy-two-fold divine
name of later Jewish and occult tradition, seventy-two angels, seventy-two
translators of the Septuagint in the traditional account, seventy-two goetic
spirits.

**And it is not a Temple number.** It does not appear in 1 Kings 6–7. It does not
appear in the Craft degrees.

**This absence is worth stating positively**, because it is the kind of negative
finding that could have come out otherwise. If the Temple description had been
composed or transmitted by people steeped in the traditions where seventy-two
carries weight, one would expect to find it. **One does not.**

### One hundred forty-four

**The claim**: 144 as 12 squared, appearing in Revelation and in later
speculation, retrojected onto the Temple.

**Provenance**: Revelation 21:17 gives the New Jerusalem's wall as 144 cubits. That
is a first-century-CE Christian apocalyptic text describing a visionary city, not
Solomon's Temple, and the number is explicitly a multiple of twelve in a passage
saturated with twelves.

**Level**: a real number in a real text about a different structure. Transferring
it to Solomon's Temple is a category error, not a finding.

---

### The pattern

Seven numbers examined. **Every one either has a documented mundane origin, or
does not appear in the Temple material at all.**

That consistency is itself the chapter's finding. It is not that each claim
happens to fail; it is that **the claims fail in the same way each time** — by
importing a number from elsewhere, or by treating an index or a round figure as a
choice.

Chapter 71 asks whether, taken together, these constitute evidence of a hidden
code. They do not, and the reason is arithmetical rather than rhetorical: **a
large set of numbers searched against a large set of "significant" values will
produce matches at a predictable rate**, and reporting the matches without the
denominator is the entire mechanism.
