# PART VI — SOLOMON AS MAGICIAN

# CHAPTER TWENTY-FIVE
## The Magical Solomon

The biblical Solomon does not do magic. The legendary Solomon does almost nothing
else. The distance between them is about a thousand years, and the route is
traceable step by step.

---

### Step one: wisdom, in the biblical sense

Solomon's gift at Gibeon is explicitly **an understanding heart to judge**, and
what it produces in the narrative is governance: the judgment between the two
mothers, administrative competence, diplomatic standing.[^1]

It extends to natural knowledge — "he spake of trees, from the cedar tree that is
in Lebanon even unto the hyssop that springeth out of the wall: he spake also of
beasts, and of fowl, and of creeping things, and of fishes."[^2]

**That verse is the seed of everything in this Part**, and it is worth noticing
what it actually says. It describes a man who *speaks about* the natural world —
who classifies, describes, knows. It is proto-naturalist wisdom, of the kind the
wisdom literature elsewhere praises.

**It says nothing about command.** Solomon discourses on beasts; he does not
summon them.

---

### Step two: Josephus, and the hinge

The first clear ancient attestation of a magical Solomon is in Josephus,
*Antiquities* VIII, and it is worth quoting the situation rather than the text
since translations vary.

Josephus reports that God granted Solomon knowledge of the art used against
demons — incantations by which illnesses are relieved, and methods of exorcism by
which demons are driven out and do not return. And he adds that this art remains
strong among Jews of his own day, describing a practitioner named Eleazar
expelling a demon in the presence of Vespasian, using a ring and formulae
attributed to Solomon.[^3]

**Three things to mark.**

**The date**: first century CE. Roughly a thousand years after any historical
Solomon.

**The claim's structure**: Josephus is not reporting Solomonic practice. He is
reporting a *first-century* practice that **attributes itself** to Solomon, and
vouching for its efficacy from what he says is personal observation.

**And the direction**: the attribution flows backward. Practitioners in Josephus's
own time claimed Solomonic authority for what they were doing, exactly as texts of
the period claimed Solomonic authorship.

---

### Step three: the Testament of Solomon

The fully developed narrative appears in the *Testament of Solomon*, a
Jewish-Christian pseudepigraphon of composite date — proposals range across the
first to fourth centuries CE, with later redaction — and it is the single most
important text for this Part.[^4]

Its plot: a demon is troubling a boy working on the Temple. Solomon prays; the
archangel Michael brings him a ring with a seal. With the ring Solomon summons,
binds and interrogates a long series of demons, each of which names itself,
describes its function and its affliction, and names the angel or formula that
thwarts it.

**And he compels them to labour on the Temple.**

That is the origin of the claim that the Temple was built by magic. Not a
tradition independently recording how the building went up, but a narrative
composed many centuries later, whose purpose is to display Solomon's command over
spirits — with the Temple supplied as the setting because it is the great thing
Solomon is known to have built.

**The Temple is the stage, not the subject.**

---

### Step four: the grimoires

From the medieval period the attribution industrialises. The *Ars Notoria*, the
*Clavicula Salomonis*, the *Lemegeton* — a substantial manuscript tradition,
circulating in Latin and later in vernaculars, attributing full ceremonial magical
systems to Solomon's authorship.

Chapters 27 and 28 treat these. What matters here is that **they are the end of a
chain, not the beginning of one**, and each link in the chain is datable.

---

### The chain, stated

**Biblical wisdom (10th c. BCE traditional date)** → *he speaks of beasts and
plants*

**Second Temple / early Roman period** → a practice of exorcism attributing itself
to Solomon

**Josephus (1st c. CE)** → the first clear attestation, reporting that practice

**Testament of Solomon (1st–4th c. CE)** → the full narrative, demons compelled to
build the Temple

**Medieval grimoires (13th c. onward)** → complete magical systems under Solomon's
name

**Renaissance and early modern** → printed, translated, expanded

**Nineteenth century** → absorbed into the occult revival (Part XII)

**Every step is documented and every step is late.** The chain is real. What it is
a chain *of* is the growth of a literary attribution, and Chapter 29 tests whether
it can carry any weight back onto the building.
