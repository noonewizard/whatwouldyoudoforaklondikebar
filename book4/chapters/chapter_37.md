# CHAPTER THIRTY-SEVEN
## North and South

If the lodge's east–west axis came largely from the parish church, its north–south
axis came from something simpler still: the sun's position in a northern-hemisphere
building.

---

### The stated reason

Masonic instruction in the English-derived jurisdictions explains the north as a
place of darkness, and the explanation given is geographical. In the northern
hemisphere, the sun at its meridian is in the south; the north side of a building
therefore receives no direct sunlight. The north is dark because in Britain the
north *is* dark.

This is one of the few pieces of Masonic symbolic reasoning that is straightforwardly
and verifiably correct about the physical world, and it is worth pausing on for
that reason alone. It is also, as an explanation of where the symbolism came from,
almost certainly right — and it has an awkward consequence, which the tradition has
never quite faced. The reasoning does not travel. South of the equator, the sun at
noon is in the north. Lodges in Australia, South Africa, and Argentina inherited a
symbolism whose stated justification is false in their latitude, and they kept it
anyway.

That is not a criticism. It is evidence. A symbol retained where its rationale
fails is a symbol whose function has detached from its reason — which is a normal
and important thing for symbols to do, and which tells us the geography was the
origin of the image, not the ground of its use.

---

### The Temple's north and south

The biblical Temple accounts do use these directions. Side chambers are described
around the building; the great laver or molten sea and the ten smaller basins are
given positions; furnishings are placed to the north and south of the hall. 2
Chronicles places the two pillars in relation to right and left.

But there is no developed north–south *symbolism* in the accounts — no doctrine
that the north signifies darkness or the south light. The associations exist
elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible, and they are not uniform: Jeremiah has evil
breaking out of the north, and Ezekiel places an offending image at a north gate,
while other passages use the directions without valuation.

So the Masonic north–south symbolism is not drawn from the Temple description. It
is drawn from the experience of light in a building at fifty-odd degrees of
northern latitude, and then placed inside a Temple frame that accepted it.

---

### The place of the newly admitted

The north-east and other intermediate positions carry specific significance in
several jurisdictions' instruction, and the details differ enough between
jurisdictions that no single account should be given as Masonic practice. What can
be said generally is that the lodge's floor is treated as a map on which a
candidate's position is meaningful — that where one stands is part of what one is
being told.

This is the same principle as Chapter 36's, and it is the principle underlying the
whole of Part VIII: the lodge uses **location as language**. A person placed in the
north has been told something about their condition without a word being spoken
about it. Book II examined at length why this form of instruction is unusually
durable — why a thing learned by standing somewhere is remembered differently from
a thing learned by being told.

---

### The honest summary of the lodge's geography

Taking Chapters 36 and 37 together:

The east–west axis matches Christian ecclesiastical practice and inverts the
Temple's.

The north–south axis matches the solar geography of northern Europe and has no
counterpart in the Temple accounts.

The sense that the room as a whole is the Temple is supplied by ritual declaration,
by the pillars at the entrance, and by the narrative framing — not by the plan.

None of this makes the lodge a fake Temple. It makes it a real room, assembled from
the materials its builders had, and then given a name that let it mean more than a
rented dining room could otherwise mean. What the name made possible is the
subject of Part IX. What it obscured is the subject of this Part — and the
obscuring was never deliberate. It was simply what happens when a frame is
powerful enough to explain anything placed inside it.
