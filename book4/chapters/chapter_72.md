# CHAPTER SEVENTY-TWO
## The Problem of Retrofitting

Retrofitting is the process by which a system a person already holds gets
discovered inside an object that does not contain it. It requires no dishonesty,
it is extremely difficult to detect from the inside, and it is the single most
productive source of false claims in this book's entire subject.

This chapter describes the mechanism, in enough detail to recognize it in
operation.

---

### The four conditions

Retrofitting reliably occurs when four things are true at once.

**An underdetermined object.** The target must be ambiguous enough to admit
multiple readings. The Temple qualifies exceptionally well: a dozen numbers, four
partly inconsistent textual sources, no physical remains, and a two-thousand-year
reception history supplying candidate meanings.

**A held system.** The interpreter arrives with a framework they already believe —
the classical orders, the Tree of Life, the golden ratio, a chronology of prophecy,
a fraternal pedigree.

**Freedom in the method.** The interpreter may choose which numbers to use, which
source to prefer, what tolerance to accept, which units to convert to, and when to
stop looking. Each choice is individually defensible. Together they constitute
enormous latitude.

**No control condition.** Nobody runs the same procedure on something that is known
not to contain the pattern.

Remove any one and retrofitting becomes much harder. Remove the fourth in
particular and it usually becomes visible immediately, which is why this book runs
controls on everything.

---

### The garden of forking paths

The statistician Andrew Gelman's phrase for the third condition is the best
available, and it names something more subtle than cheating.

An analyst who would have accepted a five per cent tolerance if the data had
required it, and a seven per cent tolerance if they had, has effectively tested
both — even though only one test was performed. The flexibility exercised is the
flexibility *available*, not the flexibility used. A single analysis conducted
with many undeclared degrees of freedom has the error rate of many analyses.

Applied here: an investigator measuring the Temple may work in cubits or convert
to feet; may use the 1 Kings figures or the Chronicles ones; may include the porch
or not; may take the Holy of Holies as a separate structure or as part of the house;
may use interior or exterior dimensions; may accept a two per cent or a ten per cent
match. Each decision is arguable. The combination provides so many paths that a
result is nearly guaranteed.

**This is why precommitment matters more than any other single methodological
practice.** A rule fixed before the data are seen removes the forking paths, and
nothing else does.

---

### Villalpando as the type specimen

Chapter 67 set out the case and it is worth naming as the model.

He held that the classical orders were perfect. He held that the Temple was
perfect. He reconstructed the Temple in the classical orders, because Ezekiel does
not specify what the columns looked like and something had to be drawn. He then
presented the reconstruction as evidence that the orders descended from the
Temple.

Every condition is present. The object was underdetermined. The system was held.
The method was free — Ezekiel constrains the plan far less than the engravings
imply. And no control existed, because nobody asked what the same procedure would
produce if applied to a building known not to be the origin of the orders.

Villalpando was a competent mathematician acting in good faith by the standards of
his time. **The mechanism does not require incompetence or bad faith. That is the
point.**

---

### How to detect it in your own work

Five questions, and this book has tried to ask them of itself throughout.

**Did I decide what would count as a hit before I looked?** If the criterion was
set afterward, the result is a description of the data, not a test of it.

**Would I have accepted a different result as confirming?** If a ratio of 1.62
would have confirmed, and 1.60, and 1.65 — and if 1.50 would have been explained
as a corrupted transmission — then nothing could have disconfirmed, and the test
had no power.

**What does the same method do to a control?** The single most informative thing
anyone in this field can do, and the least often done. Chapter 73 reports what it
did here.

**Is there a simpler explanation I have not eliminated?** The molten sea's rim, in
Chapter 71, is the model: a mundane textual detail that dissolves a famous anomaly.

**Do I need this to be true?** Not a methodological question, but the one that
predicts the others. An investigator with an interest in the outcome will find the
forking paths without noticing that they are forking.

### The honest concession

A method that finds patterns where none exist will also, occasionally, miss a
pattern that does.

If some ancient building somewhere was genuinely proportioned by a ratio its
builders regarded as significant, the procedures this book recommends might fail to
credit it — because a single building's numbers, absent a text stating the
intention, cannot be distinguished from coincidence by measurement alone.

That is a real cost and it should be acknowledged rather than argued away. The
trade is deliberate. A method that occasionally misses a real signal is preferable
to a method that reliably manufactures signals from noise, because the second kind
of error is self-reinforcing: each false discovery supplies the framework that
generates the next.

And there is a way out of the impasse that measurement alone cannot provide.
**Find a text.** Where someone wrote down what they were doing and why — Giorgi's
memorandum on San Francesco della Vigna, in Chapter 53, is the model — the
intention is evidenced and no inference from dimensions is required.

Chapter 73 shows what happens when there is no such text and one measures anyway.
