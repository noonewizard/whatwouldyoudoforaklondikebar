# CHAPTER SEVENTY-EIGHT
## From Evidence to Myth

A claim does not become a conspiracy theory by being false. It becomes one by
passing through a sequence of transformations, each of which is individually
small.

This chapter traces the sequence through a single case, chosen because it is
unusually well documented: we have the fact it started from, the fabrication that
was built on it, the moment of maximum acceptance, the confession, and more than a
century of subsequent circulation.

---

### Stage one: a real fact

Albert Pike existed. He wrote *Morals and Dogma*, published in 1871 for one
jurisdiction of one appendant rite. In the course of a long expository work drawing
on comparative religion, he used the word *Lucifer* in its literal Latin sense of
light-bearer, in a passage discussing how a term had been understood in antiquity.

Every element of that paragraph is true and checkable.

### Stage two: interpretation

A word used descriptively is read as used devotionally. The context — an exposition
of what others believed — is dropped. The genre of the book, which Chapter 60 set
out, is not consulted.

This stage requires no malice. It requires only reading a sentence out of a
thousand-page book.

### Stage three: fabrication

Here the case becomes extraordinary.

Gabriel Jogand-Pagès, writing as Léo Taxil, had built a career in France producing
scurrilous anticlerical works. He was briefly a Freemason — initiated in the early
1880s and removed shortly afterward. In 1885 he announced a conversion to
Catholicism and began producing anti-Masonic literature instead.

Over the following decade he constructed an elaborate fiction. He invented
**Palladism**, a supposed Luciferian order concealed within Freemasonry. He
invented **Diana Vaughan**, its high priestess, who converted and published her
memoirs. And he fabricated an **instruction attributed to Albert Pike**, addressed
to the Supreme Councils of the world and dated 1889, in which the Masonic god is
identified as Lucifer.

Pike had died in 1891. The document has no manuscript, no archival provenance, and
no existence prior to Taxil's publication of it.

### Stage four: repetition by authority

The fiction was widely believed by those it was designed for. Taxil was received in
papal audience by Leo XIII in 1887. An international anti-Masonic congress at Trent
in 1896 treated the Palladist material as evidence. Catholic and Protestant
anti-Masonic literature across Europe reproduced it.

This stage is the one that gives a fabrication durability. Once respectable
institutions have repeated a claim, later repetitions can cite *them* rather than
the original — and the fabrication acquires a citation trail that looks like
corroboration but is a single source reflected in many mirrors.

### Stage five: the confession, and its failure

On 19 April 1897, having advertised that Diana Vaughan would appear in person,
Taxil addressed a crowded meeting in Paris and announced that the whole thing was
his own invention. He explained how he had constructed it, and said plainly that he
had done it to make fools of his opponents.

**The confession is a matter of public record.** It was reported at the time. It
has never been in serious doubt.

The Pike quotation has circulated continuously ever since — through
twentieth-century anti-Masonic literature, through the fundamentalist publishing of
the 1970s and 80s, and now through the internet, where it appears daily, generally
without attribution to Taxil and frequently with a fabricated page reference to
*Morals and Dogma*.

---

### Why retractions fail

Three mechanisms, and they are general.

**The correction does not travel with the claim.** A fabrication is interesting; a
retraction is administrative. They are copied at different rates, and after a
generation the claim has been reproduced ten thousand times and the retraction a
hundred.

**The claim has detached from its source.** Most people repeating the Pike
quotation have never heard of Taxil. They encountered the quotation attributed to
Pike, with a book title and often a page number, and had no reason to look further.
A correction addressed to the fabricator's name does not reach an audience that
does not know the name.

**And the retraction can be absorbed.** This is the most important and the most
uncomfortable. A believer encountering the confession can read it as the
confession being the lie — that Taxil was pressured, bribed, or threatened into
recanting, which proves how much was at stake.

Note what has happened there. **The evidence against the claim has been converted
into evidence for it.** A framework that can do that is unfalsifiable, and
unfalsifiability is not a flaw in such systems. It is the feature that keeps them
alive.

### The second case, briefly

The same five stages produced the older and larger claim that Freemasonry caused
the French Revolution.

The fact: lodges existed in France, and some revolutionaries were members, as were
some royalists. The interpretation: membership read as causation. The
amplification: Barruel's *Mémoires* and Robison's *Proofs of a Conspiracy*, both
1797–98, both written by men with declared political positions in the immediate
aftermath. The repetition: two centuries of citation, in which each generation
cites the previous rather than the archives. And the absorption: absence of
documentary evidence read as proof of the conspiracy's competence.

No new documentary evidence has been added at any stage since 1798. The claim has
grown entirely by repetition.

---

### The diagnostic

The most useful question to ask of any claim in this territory is not *is it
true?* — which can take a book — but:

**What documentary evidence was added after the original assertion?**

For a claim that is tracking reality, the answer accumulates: archives open, new
sources are found, the picture sharpens or changes. For a claim of the kind this
chapter describes, the answer is nothing. The material is the same material,
reproduced at increasing volume, with the confidence of each retelling supplied by
the number of previous retellings.

A claim that has grown for two centuries without acquiring a single new document
is not a body of evidence. It is an echo with a long tail.
