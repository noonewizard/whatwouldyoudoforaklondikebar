## FOUR THINGS THAT MUST NOT BE COLLAPSED

**1. Documented historical proportional practice.** Vitruvius (1st c. BCE),
Alberti's *De re aedificatoria* (1452), Palladio's *Quattro Libri* (1570) — real,
documented-by-the-architect-themselves systems of proportion, verifiable because
the designer wrote down the method (Book III ch.45's "gold standard" test: the
rule exists independently of the measurement).

**2. Medieval operative construction technique.** *Ad quadratum*/*ad triangulum*
methods, documented in surviving lodge books and the Milan Cathedral dispute of
the 1390s — genuine geometric CONSTRUCTION technique, not numerical symbolism (per
Book III ch.45's careful distinction between "geometry as technique" and "geometry
as revelation").

**3. Masonic use of geometric symbolism.** Eighteenth-century Craft ritual
elevates geometry rhetorically (the 47th Proposition, per Book III ch.37) without
performing construction — geometry PRAISED, not geometry PRACTICED, in the ritual
itself.

**4. The modern "sacred geometry" movement.** Emerging substantially from the
1970s onward (associated with popularizers such as Robert Lawlor's widely read
*Sacred Geometry: Philosophy and Practice*, 1982, and predecessors), asserting a
continuous esoteric geometric tradition from antiquity through the present that
the documentary record does not support in the unbroken form claimed.

## APPLIED TO THE TEMPLE SPECIFICALLY

**The golden ratio claim** — tested computationally (see
`research/computational_verification/golden_ratio_test.py`) against all pairwise
ratios of the Temple's stated dimensions, with a matched control building. **The
control produces a comparable hit rate to the Temple set**, which is the
demonstration this section needs: the method (unconstrained pairwise ratio search
with unstated tolerance) cannot distinguish designed from arbitrary dimension
sets, so a "hit" proves nothing about intention (per the master prompt's own
Sacred Geometry Standard: report tolerance, alternatives, and comparison against
chance).

**The "Temple as microcosm of the cosmos" claim** — this is a THEMATIC/COSMOLOGICAL
claim (ch.10, ch.55), not a geometric-encoding claim, and the book must not let the
two bleed into each other. Ancient Near Eastern temple-as-cosmic-model is a
genuine, well-evidenced conceptual category (Jon D. Levenson's work on Zion as
cosmic mountain is the standard scholarly treatment). This is entirely separate
from, and does not support, claims that specific numerical ratios in the Temple's
measurements encode astronomical or geometric data.

**The Villalpando reconstruction's geometric regularity** — real, and real evidence
of RENAISSANCE architectural theory, not of the ancient building. Villalpando
IMPOSED Vitruvian/classical geometric order onto his reconstruction; this is his
authorial choice, extensively documented in his own text, and should be presented
exactly that way (ch.67) — a positive, well-evidenced case of "geometric design,
demonstrated by the designer's own words," precisely as the master prompt's Sacred
Geometry Standard requires, but about a 1604 book, not a 10th-century-BCE building.

## WHAT WOULD COUNT AS EVIDENCE FOR ANCIENT TEMPLE SACRED GEOMETRY

Stated per Book III's disconfirmation discipline: a documented ancient text
(Israelite, Phoenician, or comparative ANE) explicitly specifying a proportional
RULE (not merely reporting the result) for temple design, analogous to what
Vitruvius later provides for Roman architecture. **No such text is known to survive
for the First Temple.** 1 Kings 6–7 reports dimensions; it does not report a
generative rule ("the width shall be such that..."). This absence is itself
evidence-worthy and the book should say so rather than filling the gap with
inference.
