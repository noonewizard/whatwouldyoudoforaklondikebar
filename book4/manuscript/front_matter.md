## A NOTE ON FIVE TEMPLES

This book distinguishes five things that are routinely treated as one, and almost
every argument in it depends on the distinction holding.

**The building** — what physically stood in Jerusalem, insofar as evidence can
establish it. This is the thinnest of the five, for a reason stated in Chapter 4
and never afterwards forgotten.

**The text** — what ancient authors wrote about it, which is a different claim
from what existed.

**The symbol** — what later interpreters made it mean, which is where most of
what any reader has ever encountered about Solomon's Temple actually lives.

**The ritual** — how Freemasonry specifically used it, which has its own
documentary history and its own datable beginning.

**The inner Temple** — what it became as a figure for the human being.

Sliding between these without saying so is, this book argues, the mechanism that
produces nearly every overstated claim in the literature. The five are named here
so that a reader can hold the author to them.

## A NOTE ON RECONSTRUCTIONS

Every image of Solomon's Temple ever made is a reconstruction. Not one is a
record. The site where direct evidence could exist has never been excavated and
under present conditions cannot be.

Where this book reproduces a reconstruction — Villalpando's engravings, a modern
archaeological line drawing, a museum model — the caption says what it is and
what it is not evidence of. This is stated at every instance rather than once at
the beginning, because stating it once is how the later images quietly acquire an
authority they have not earned.

## A NOTE ON METHOD

Six levels of evidential strength are used throughout and set out in Appendix R.
Where scholars genuinely disagree — and on the central question of this book's
first part, they do — the disagreement is reported with its participants named,
not resolved by authorial preference.

Every geometric ratio, gematria value, cipher transformation and probability in
this book was computed rather than copied, and the scripts are published with it.
One of them failed on its first run. That failure is described in the text where
it occurs.

## A NOTE ON RITUAL AND ON SENSITIVITY

No private Masonic ritual material is reproduced here and none is invented. The
Masonic material quoted comes from sources publicly in print since 1730. Masonic
practice differs between jurisdictions; where this book describes a ceremony it
says which tradition it is describing.

Chapter 63 concerns a site sacred to more than one living religious community and
subject to active political dispute. It is written to describe history and
historiography with precision, not to advance any contemporary claim. The
terminology used there is explained at the head of the chapter.
