# ILLUSTRATION LIST

Seventy-eight plates are proposed, within the brief's range of sixty to ninety.
The full plan, with holdings, dates, and chapter placements, is in
`illustrations/illustration_plan.md`. This list states the principles and
summarizes the sections.

## Principles

**No image is invented.** Every item is either a real, locatable object — a
manuscript, an excavation plan, a building, a printed plate, a photograph — or an
original diagram declared as such.

**Reconstructions are labelled as reconstructions.** Chapter 66 established why
this matters: a drawing removes the uncertainty a text contains, and a reader who
sees only the drawing cannot tell which parts were given and which supplied. Every
Temple reconstruction plate carries its artist, its date, and a note of which
textual source it is built on.

**Comparative material is labelled with its own provenance**, not as illustrating
the Temple.

**[SVR]** marks items whose shelfmark, accession number, or museum attribution
requires confirmation before captioning.

## The five original diagrams

1. **The five-level model** (Appendix A) — building, text, symbol, ritual setting,
   inner Temple, with the direction of movement and its datable transitions.
2. **The four-source pillar discrepancy** (Appendix F) — 1 Kings, 2 Kings, 2
   Chronicles, and Jeremiah drawn to their own stated measurements, side by side.
3. **The golden-ratio control distribution** (Chapter 73, Appendix M) — the
   synthetic null's hit-count histogram with the Temple's single hit marked on it.
4. **The hexagram-in-a-lattice control** (Chapter 79) — the jittered street grid
   with the best-fit hexagram drawn on it, at map scale.
5. **Lodge orientation against Temple orientation** (Chapter 36) — the two plans
   with their sacred poles marked, showing the reversal.

Diagrams 3 and 4 are outputs of the published scripts and are reproducible by any
reader who runs them.
