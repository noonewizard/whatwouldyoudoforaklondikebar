## THE FIVE-LEVEL MODEL (recurs throughout)

Per the master prompt's required central conceptual model:

**LEVEL 1 — THE BUILDING.** What physically existed? (Parts I, IV; the archaeological
assessment.)

**LEVEL 2 — THE TEXT.** What did ancient authors say? (Parts I–IV, the biblical and
Josephan material.)

**LEVEL 3 — THE SYMBOL.** What did later interpreters make it mean? (Parts VI, XI,
XII, XIV.)

**LEVEL 4 — THE RITUAL.** How did Freemasonry use it? (Parts VII, VIII.)

**LEVEL 5 — THE INNER TEMPLE.** What does the Temple become as a metaphor for the
human being? (Parts IX, X, and the final synthesis in Part XVIII.)

Each chapter is tagged internally (in the manuscript, via a marginal note in the
drafting file, not printed in the finished text) with which level(s) it primarily
addresses, so that a reader — and the author, during drafting — can see when the
argument is sliding between levels without flagging the slide, which is precisely
the collapse the master prompt's central intellectual problem forbids.

## RELATION TO BOOKS I–III

Findings settled in earlier volumes are stated in a sentence and cited, not
re-argued: the 1717/1721 dispute, the Taxil forgery, the four-link Kabbalah chain,
Pike's unattributed transcription of Lévi, the absence of operative apparatus in
Craft ritual, the Lost Word's narrative (not cryptographic) origin, the ten-test
hidden-message protocol, the half-of-everything rule, the disagreement principle,
the rule of direction, and the standing distinction between availability and
transmission.

**Genuinely new in this volume**: the archaeological-absence problem specific to
the Temple Mount; the five-Solomons and Hiram-triad distinctions; the
comparative-ANE-temple study; Villalpando and the Renaissance reconstruction
tradition; Newton's Temple manuscripts; the architecture-as-cognitive-environment
material (Part X, explicitly distinguished from Book II's ritual-transformation
material); the conspiracy-claim audit; and the Washington D.C. geometry control
test.

Book I asked *where did it come from?* Book II asked *what does it do?* Book III
asked *what is a word, that losing one could matter?* Book IV asks *what is a
building, that becoming an idea could matter more than remaining a building?*
