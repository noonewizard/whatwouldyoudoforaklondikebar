# RESEARCH GAPS — BOOK IV

Stated in advance, per series practice. These are the points at which the book's
arguments are thinnest and where a reader should press hardest.

## TIER 1 — MUST BE DISCHARGED BEFORE PUBLICATION

**1. Hiram Abiff narrative-element first-appearance audit.** The specific date
each narrative component (individually named ruffians, tools-as-weapons, the
acacia sprig, the raising formula) first appears in print has not been
systematically traced against the full exposure corpus (Prichard 1730, *Three
Distinct Knocks* 1760, *Jachin and Boaz* 1762, French *réception* material).
Ch.20–24 currently rest on the general 1720s dating established in Book III;
element-by-element dating would strengthen but is not yet complete. See
`hiram/hiram_study.md`.

**2. Systematic Masonic architectural survey.** Ch.65 and Appendix G require
actual floor plans and decorative programs from a representative sample of
surviving Masonic buildings across Britain/Europe/US/colonial-Latin-American
contexts. The candidate-building list in `architecture/masonic_architecture_study.md`
identifies where to look; primary-source archival access (L&MF, Scottish Rite
Masonic Museum & Library, comparable European archives) has not yet occurred.

**3. Villalpando primary-text collation.** The three-volume *In Ezechielem
Explanationes* (1596–1604) has been assessed via secondary scholarship (Taylor,
Ramírez) rather than fully collated at primary-source level for ch.67.

**4. Newton manuscript primary access.** The Yahuda collection (National Library
of Israel) and Portsmouth Collection (Cambridge) materials on the Temple have
been assessed via secondary scholarship (Manuel, Iliffe, Goldish) rather than
personally examined for ch.68. This is consistent with how Book III handled
comparable manuscript-access limitations and is disclosed rather than concealed.

**5. Bibliography completeness on six [SVR]-marked entries** (see
`citations/bibliography.md`'s closing note) — page-level and edition-level
verification outstanding on Villalpando's collation, the Tel Motza excavation
report citation, the AQC paper-level citations, a German-language volume
(Metzger), and Prescott's exact pagination.

## TIER 2 — WOULD MATERIALLY STRENGTHEN THE BOOK

**6. Systematic sampling of the online "Temple code" and "Washington geometry"
literature** with archived URLs and capture dates, per Book III's practice for
its equivalent material (ch.71, 79).

**7. Purpose-built Masonic hall orientation survey** — whether 19th/20th-century
purpose-built temples show a statistically notable preference for true-compass-
east orientation, as opposed to the ritually-designated-east pattern established
for rented rooms in Book III. See `architecture/masonic_architecture_study.md`.

**8. Additional golden-ratio control buildings.** The computational verification
currently tests one matched control against the Temple's stated dimensions;
`computational_verification/README.md` notes that a distribution across 5–10
controls would be more rigorous than a single comparison, however illustrative.

**9. Ethiopian Ark tradition (Kebra Nagast material)** for ch.8 — identified as
relevant but not yet added to the bibliography or researched at primary-source
level.

## TIER 3 — ACKNOWLEDGED LIMITS, NOT FIXABLE

**10. The Temple Mount cannot be excavated.** This is not a research gap this
study can close; it is a structural fact the book must state repeatedly rather
than work around with confident reconstruction (see `archaeology/
archaeological_assessment.md`).

**11. The cubit's exact length is permanently underdetermined** absent new
evidence, meaning every physical-scale (as opposed to ratio) claim about the
Temple's size carries an irreducible uncertainty band (Appendix D).

**12. The minimalist/maximalist debate is live scholarship, not a solved
question.** This book reports the dispute and its stakes; it does not, and
should not, resolve it by authorial fiat (`historiography.md` §1).

**13. This study's Hebrew is machine-checked against published figures, not
independently expert**, consistent with the disclosed limitation carried from
Book III. Where a reading depends on Hebrew philology, the text names the
scholar it follows.
