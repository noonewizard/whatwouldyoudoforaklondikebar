# HISTORICAL SOLOMON — DELIVERABLE 3

Five Solomons, kept apart per the master prompt's required distinction.

---

## 1. HISTORICAL SOLOMON (E4 — heavily disputed)

What can be said with any confidence: a king named Solomon (Hebrew *Shlomo*, from
the root *sh-l-m*, "peace/completeness") reigned over some polity centered on
Jerusalem in roughly the 10th century BCE, as the son and successor of David.
**Beyond that bare claim, essentially everything is contested** — the extent of his
kingdom, the scale of his building projects, the nature of his "wisdom"
reputation's historical kernel (if any), even the reliability of the regnal dates.
No contemporary Israelite/Judahite inscription naming Solomon has been found; the
so-called "Solomonic" material culture is a retrospective archaeological
attribution, not a labeled find. See the minimalist/maximalist debate,
historiography.md §1.

**What would change this**: a contemporary (10th c. BCE) inscription naming
Solomon or clearly referencing his reign, comparable to the 9th-century Tel Dan
stele's reference to the "House of David" (which IS such external corroboration
for David, and is a useful contrast case — David has one kind of external evidence
Solomon currently lacks).

## 2. BIBLICAL SOLOMON (E1 for what the text says; E4 for its historicity)

The Solomon of Kings and Chronicles: builder of the Temple, author-figure of
Proverbs/Ecclesiastes/Song of Songs (by later attribution — see below), possessor
of divinely granted wisdom (1 Kings 3), diplomat and trader (the Queen of Sheba
narrative), and, in a more critical strand of the text itself, a king whose
excesses (multiple foreign wives, forced labor, heavy taxation) sow the seeds of
the kingdom's later division (1 Kings 11). **The biblical text is not
straightforwardly hagiographic** — this internal criticism is itself evidence the
book must not flatten into "Solomon = wise good king," a correction several
Masonic-adjacent popular treatments skip.

## 3. LEGENDARY SOLOMON (E1 for existence of the tradition; not a historical claim)

Post-biblical Jewish, Christian, and Islamic legendary elaboration: Solomon
commanding the winds, understanding the language of animals and birds (a theme
with roots already visible in the Quranic Solomon, Surah 27, and paralleled in
extra-canonical Jewish material), building with the aid of a demon (Asmodeus) in
some traditions. Ch.25 traces this properly.

## 4. MAGICAL SOLOMON (E1 for the textual tradition; separate question re: any
historical practice)

The Solomon of the *Testament of Solomon* (a Jewish-Christian pseudepigraphon,
likely composed in stages between the 1st and 4th centuries CE, in which Solomon
binds demons using a magic ring to compel their labor on the Temple) and of the
medieval-to-early-modern grimoire tradition (*Clavicula Salomonis*/Key of
Solomon, *Lemegeton*/Lesser Key of Solomon, and others), which attributes
authorship to Solomon centuries or over a millennium after any historical Solomon
could have lived. **This is a textual attribution tradition, structurally
identical to pseudo-Solomonic authorship of wisdom literature (Wisdom of Solomon,
Psalms of Solomon, Odes of Solomon) — attributing a text to Solomon was a
recognized ancient and medieval literary convention for conferring authority, not
evidence that Solomon wrote or practiced any of it.** Ch.27–28 treat this at
length; the key methodological point (why texts get attributed to famous
authority-figures) belongs here as groundwork.

## 5. MASONIC SOLOMON (E1 for the tradition; assessed per usual transmission
standard)

Solomon as the patron/builder figure of Masonic traditional history from Anderson
(1723) onward — primarily the *builder-king*, not (in Craft ritual) the magician.
**This is the single most important finding of this file for the book's overall
argument**: eighteenth-century Craft Masonry's Solomon is overwhelmingly the
*architectural* Solomon of Kings, not the *magical* Solomon of the grimoires. The
magical Solomon enters Masonic-adjacent discourse later and through different
channels (ch.29, and the Lévi/Pike chain established in Book III), which means
**"Solomon was a magician, therefore the Masonic Temple is a magical structure" is
exactly the kind of inference this book's brief instructs be tested rather than
assumed — and it fails the transmission test as stated.**

## LEDGER SUMMARY

| Claim | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| A king named Solomon reigned in Jerusalem, 10th c. BCE | Biblical text; no independent contemporary inscription | **E4** |
| Solomon built a Temple substantially as described in 1 Kings | Biblical text only; no excavation possible | **E4–E5** |
| Solomon authored biblical wisdom literature attributed to him | Attribution convention, not authorial evidence | **E5–E6** for literal authorship |
| Solomon practiced ceremonial magic / commanded demons | Post-biblical legendary and pseudepigraphal literature only, centuries+ after any historical Solomon | **E6** as history; **E1** as literary tradition |
| Masonic ritual's Solomon derives from the magical/grimoire Solomon rather than the biblical builder-king | No documented transmission; contrary evidence (Craft ritual's architectural, not magical, Solomon) | **E6** |
