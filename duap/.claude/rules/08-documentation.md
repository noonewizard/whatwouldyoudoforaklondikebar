# Documentation style

The audience is cryptographers, distributed-systems engineers, economists,
privacy engineers, regulators, standards bodies, enterprise architects,
security auditors and open-source maintainers. Write for the most sceptical
of them.

## Requirements

1. **State the limitation next to the capability**, in the same paragraph,
   not in a "known limitations" appendix nobody reads.
2. **No marketing register.** "Revolutionary", "seamless", "world-class",
   "trustless", "unhackable", "game-changing" do not appear.
3. **Explain the reasoning, not only the mechanism.** A reader must be able
   to tell why an alternative was rejected.
4. **Cite primary sources.** RFCs by number and section, regulations by
   provision, papers by author, title, venue and year. No citation is
   included that has not been checked against the source.
5. **Mark uncertainty as uncertainty.** "We believe", "we have not measured",
   "this has not been audited" are acceptable and expected; vagueness that
   conceals the same thing is not.
6. **Every document has a status line** naming its maturity and its date.
7. **No invented references.** A citation that cannot be verified is deleted,
   not approximated.
