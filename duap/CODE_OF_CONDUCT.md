# Code of conduct

**Status:** REFERENCE · 2026-09-21

## The standard

Be straightforward, be accurate, and assume the other person is trying to
get it right.

Harassment, personal attacks, and discrimination on any basis are not
tolerated. Neither is the quieter failure: dismissing someone's finding
because of who raised it rather than what it says.

## What this project specifically asks for

This repository is infrastructure that makes claims about cryptography,
privacy, economics and regulation. Its rules forbid unearned claims
because an unearned claim here is worse than a missing feature. That
discipline only works if the social norms match it.

**Disagree with the claim, not the person.** "This benchmark does not
support that sentence" is the right form. "You always overstate results" is
not, and is also less useful.

**Findings are contributions.** Someone demonstrating that a maturity
marker is inflated, a benchmark is misread, or a security argument has a
hole has done the project a service. The correct response is to fix it or
explain why it is not a defect — never to defend the original claim out of
attachment to it.

**Say when you do not know.** "I have not measured this" and "this has not
been audited" are expected here and carry no cost. Concealing uncertainty
does.

**Be correctable.** Changing a conclusion because the evidence moved is the
point of the exercise, not an admission. This repository contains several
worked examples, including an economic model whose output contradicted the
conclusions first written for it, and a beachhead that was removed by
competitive research.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in issues and reviews, and anywhere someone
is representing the project.

## Enforcement

There is no governing body yet — `GOVERNANCE.md` is explicit that nothing
it describes exists — so there is no formal enforcement process to
describe, and inventing one would be the kind of claim this project
forbids elsewhere. Until a body exists, maintainers handle this directly:
raise a concern with them privately, and expect it to be taken seriously
and kept confidential.

When a governance structure is established, a proper reporting and
enforcement process is part of what it must define.
