## Visual thesis

**Where did the ideas come from?** The book's finding is that the transmission
chains are far shorter and better documented than either proponents or critics
believe, and that 1854 keeps appearing. The dominant visual mode is therefore
**transmission**: chains, chronologies, and stemmata in which the *gaps* are
drawn as carefully as the links.

## Visual vocabulary

| Element | Encoding |
|---|---|
| Attested transmission | Solid arrow, source named on the arrow |
| Proposed transmission | Dashed arrow |
| **Refuted transmission** | Dotted arrow with an `✕` at its midpoint — drawn, not omitted |
| Text | Rectangular node |
| Person | Rounded node |
| Institution | Double-ruled node |
| Chronological gap | **Horizontal distance is proportional to elapsed time** |

That last rule is the book's argument made structural. A claimed chain with a
four-century gap *looks* like a four-century gap.

## Keystone figures

**`@fig-transmission-gaps`** (A). The book's central chain drawn twice: once as
proponents present it, once with horizontal distance proportional to elapsed
time. Same nodes, same arrows, radically different picture. One figure carries
the book's thesis.

**`@fig-1854-hinge`** (A). A timeline of esoteric correspondence systems with
everything before 1854 in ink-muted and everything after in accent. The density
after the line is the finding.

**`@fig-rosicrucian-vault-pair`** (A). The *Fama*'s described vault (1614) beside
the Golden Dawn's constructed vault (1890s). This is the book's model of what
genuine transmission looks like — text to object, three centuries later, feature
by feature — and it earns a full-page plate.
