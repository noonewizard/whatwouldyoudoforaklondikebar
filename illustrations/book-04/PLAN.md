## Visual thesis

**How does meaning become physical space?** Dominant mode: **plan** —
orthographic, measured, dimensioned. And the book's hardest visual problem:
*every* Temple image in history has been a reconstruction, and reconstructions
conceal their own inference. The illustration program must do the opposite.

## THE RECONSTRUCTION PROTOCOL

Every Temple figure declares, in the drawing itself:

| Element | Stroke |
|---|---|
| Stated in the cited text | Solid |
| Inferred from a comparandum, named | Dashed |
| Conjectural | Dotted |
| **Not specified by any source** | **Omitted — the drawing stops** |

**Nothing is completed for tidiness.** Where the text does not give a roof, the
figure has no roof. This is the "unfinished edge" motif (Deliverable 2) doing
argumentative work.

Every Temple reconstruction caption names **which text it is built from**, since
1 Kings, 2 Chronicles, Ezekiel, and Middot describe different buildings.

## Keystone figures

**`@fig-reconstruction-gallery`** (A, full-page spread). Eight reconstructions of
the same building across eight centuries — Nicholas of Lyra, Arias Montano,
Villalpando, Leon Templo, Newton, a Victorian engraving, a mid-century
archaeological reconstruction, a current comparative one. Each looks like the
architecture of its own moment. Caption: *nobody has ever reconstructed the
Temple in anything other than the architecture they already knew.* The single
most efficient figure in the book.

**`@fig-pillar-discrepancy`** (A). The pillars drawn four times, each to the
measurements its own source gives — 1 Kings, 2 Kings, 2 Chronicles, Jeremiah.
Eighteen cubits three times and thirty-five once. The chapter argues that a
transmission which could not preserve this did not carry a cipher; the figure
makes that immediate.

**`@fig-golden-ratio-controls`** (A). Registered as FIG-04-031. Generated
directly from `golden_ratio_controls.py`, not redrawn.

**`@fig-dc-hexagram-control`** (A). The jittered lattice with its best-fit
hexagram, at map scale, beside the claim literature's figure. Both fit. One
contains nothing by construction.

## Temple Mount figures — special handling

Figures touching the platform are **descriptive only**. They show what is
physically there and what is archaeologically known, with the three naming
conventions given in the caption and the unexcavated status stated. No figure
depicts a proposed Third Temple, a sovereignty claim, or a location for the
Temple presented as settled — Chapter 63 establishes that the location is not
established, and the figures must not exceed the text.
