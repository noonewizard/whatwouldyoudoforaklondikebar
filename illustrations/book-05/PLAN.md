## Visual thesis

**How does a person construct himself?** Dominant mode: **material** — process
from rough to finished, tools, stages, and the transformation of a substance.
Where Book IV draws plans, Book V draws *making*.

That last one carries Book IV Chapter 43's finding into Book V: the ashlar
metaphor is *subtractive*, and a figure that shows only the finished block loses
the argument.

## The comparative mythology matrix — the book's hardest figure

Osiris · Attis · Adonis · Tammuz · Dionysus · Christ · Hiram, against features:
death · dismemberment · search · lament · recovery · raising · calendar
association · craft association.

**The figure must visually distinguish similarity from historical relationship.**
Required construction:

1. **Date column, to scale.** Each tradition's attested date, with the
   chronological distance from Hiram Abiff (1720s–30s) shown as actual
   horizontal distance. Most of the distance is enormous.
2. **A transmission column that is empty.** A dedicated column headed *attested
   transmission channel to Masonic usage*, with every cell reading **none
   demonstrated**. The empty column is the finding.
3. **Feature cells encode presence only**, never degree of similarity — a
   five-point similarity scale would be an invented measurement.
4. **Caption carries the rule:** *similarity is not proof of transmission; the
   half-of-everything rule applies to every low-cardinality feature in this
   table.*

Without construction rules 1 and 2, this figure would be the most misleading
image in the entire series. It is precisely the figure that comparative-mythology
popularizations get wrong, and the reason the Visual Argument Audit has a
dedicated "resemblance trap" check.

## Standing constraint

No figure may depict the third degree, its ceremony, or its furniture beyond what
the published exposures print. Diagrams of degree *structure* are permitted;
depictions of degree *content* are not.
