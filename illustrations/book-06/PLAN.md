## Visual thesis

**Who constructs the systems that construct people?** Dominant mode:
**structure** — organizational and authority topology. The series ends where it
began, with plans; but these are plans of institutions rather than of buildings.

## The central visual problem

> **An organizational diagram of a secretive institution looks exactly like a
> conspiracy diagram.**

This book is the one where the visual system is most likely to betray the
argument, and the mitigations must be structural rather than editorial.

| Mitigation | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Separate the eight authority types** | Formal · informal · ritual · social · legal · moral · expert · symbolic each get a distinct connector. A single undifferentiated "influence" arrow is the conspiracy diagram's whole grammar and is **not available in the component library** |
| **Every relation is sourced** | A connector carries its evidence badge like any other element. An unsourced connector cannot be drawn |
| **Draw the boundaries of knowledge** | Where the institution's actual workings are undocumented, the figure shows an explicit *undocumented* region rather than a plausible-looking structure |
| **No composite actors** | Never a node labelled "the Masons" or "the elites." Nodes are named bodies with constitutions and dates |
| **Comparative framing** | Masonic governance figures appear beside those of contemporaneous trade guilds, friendly societies, and chartered companies — the comparison is the antidote to exceptionalism |

## The closing figure

**`@fig-who-designs-the-designers`** (A). The series' final image. It must
**resist** the conspiracy reading it invites: a recursive diagram in which every
designing body is itself shown as having been constituted by a prior process,
with the regress explicitly unterminated and the terminal node reading
**undocumented** rather than resolving to a hidden hand.

The figure's argument is that the regress does not terminate — which is the
opposite of what a conspiracy diagram asserts, and the reason the series ends
with a question rather than a revelation.

## Standing constraint

Contemporary political claims are presented neutrally, with attribution. No
figure may place a living person in an authority diagram, and no figure may
assert an undocumented institutional relationship regardless of how widely it is
believed.
